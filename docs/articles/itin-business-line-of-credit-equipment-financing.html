Immigrants own 19.1% of all U.S. employer companies despite representing just 13.9% of the population, according to the U.S. Census Bureau. Many of those owners operate on an ITIN, not an SSN, and when they need working capital or equipment to grow, they hit a wall that most lending guides don’t explain clearly: which products actually exist, who approves them, and what they cost.

This guide answers that with verified lender data, real rates, and a clear action plan. It covers two products specifically: revolving business lines of credit and equipment financing. Those are the two tools that established immigrant business owners most often need once a basic term loan isn’t enough anymore.

Why can’t I just go to my bank for a business line of credit?

Traditional banks use an SSN as the primary identifier for personal credit pulls, underwriting systems, and CRA compliance checks. Without one, most bank portals reject the application before a human ever sees it. That has nothing to do with your revenue, your business history, or your ability to repay. It’s a systems problem, not a creditworthiness problem.

The fix is a separate category of lenders: Community Development Financial Institutions (CDFIs), nonprofit microlenders, and specialty equipment finance companies that have built ITIN-compatible underwriting from scratch. For many immigrant entrepreneurs, not having a Social Security Number is the first obstacle they hit when seeking a business loan. A growing number of lenders have built their underwriting around the ITIN as a valid alternative for verifying identity and financial history.

One more thing worth knowing before you start applying: as of March 1, 2026, SBA Policy Notice 5000-876441 requires that 100% of all direct and indirect business owners be U.S. citizens or U.S. nationals. ITIN holders, green card holders, and visa holders are all excluded under the current rule. That means the two most common small-business funding pathways, a bank line of credit and an SBA-backed loan, are both closed to you. This guide focuses on what’s actually open.

What is a business line of credit, and why does it matter more than a term loan for working capital?

A question we hear often: most ITIN borrowers understand term loans because they work like a car payment: fixed amount, fixed rate, fixed monthly payment. Lines of credit are different, and for many businesses, more useful.

A revolving business line of credit gives you a credit limit you draw from as needed. You pay interest only on what you actually use. When you repay it, that credit becomes available again, like a credit card but with much higher limits and lower rates. That makes it well-suited for businesses with uneven cash flow: a landscaping company waiting 45 days to be paid by a commercial client, a restaurant buying extra inventory before a holiday weekend, or a cleaning service that has to pay employees before the invoices clear.

The DreamSpring Power Line is a fixed-rate, revolving line of credit with a term up to 24 months that business owners can draw from as needs come up during normal operations. The line runs $1,000 to $100,000, with a revolving term up to 24 months, an interest rate of 9.99%-18.74%, a credit score requirement of 600+, a community benefit fee of 4%-10%, and a maximum APR of 36%.

The fee structure matters. On a DreamSpring line of credit, you pay the community benefit fee (essentially an origination fee) at the time of each draw, not upfront on the full limit. That makes the real cost of a small draw higher than the interest rate alone suggests, but you’re not paying fees on credit you never touch.

Which CDFI has the best line of credit for ITIN holders right now?

This one comes up a lot: there are only a handful of CDFIs that offer a true revolving line of credit, as opposed to a term loan with flexible draws.

DreamSpring Power Line of Credit is the strongest product available to ITIN holders in 2026. DreamSpring offers one of the only CDFI-issued revolving lines of credit, serving 27 states and accepting ITIN applications with no application fee.

To qualify, you need an SSN or ITIN and a business you want to start or grow (DreamSpring doesn’t offer personal loans). Your business must be in one of their 27 states: Alabama, Arizona, California, Colorado, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Iowa, Kansas, Louisiana, Michigan, Mississippi, Missouri, Nebraska, Nevada, New Mexico, New York, North Carolina, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Washington, and Wyoming.

Many borrowers receive funds within 24 hours of approval, which is faster than most lenders in this space. DreamSpring also offers uncollateralized options for loans under $20,000 with a 650+ credit score (since July 2024).

Ascendus Business Line of Credit is a solid second option, particularly for borrowers with credit scores between 575 and 599 who don’t yet meet DreamSpring’s 600+ floor. To be eligible, you need a credit score above 575, no more than $3,000 in unpaid or past-due debt, and no repossession or foreclosure within the last two years. Lines go up to $10,000 at 13.99%, with minimal paperwork to apply. For borrowers who meet the one-year in-business requirement, Ascendus offers a larger line up to $50,000 at 9.99%-15.99%.

One practical note on fees: Ascendus charges a $200 application fee, a 5% closing fee, and an $80 UCC filing fee. No out-of-pocket costs are required during the application itself; those fees are rolled into the loan and only apply if you’re approved and funded.

I need equipment, not working capital. What are my options with an ITIN?

Readers frequently ask: equipment financing is often easier to get with an ITIN than a line of credit, because the equipment itself secures the loan. The lender’s risk is lower, and requirements are sometimes looser as a result.

ITIN holders can access invoice factoring (qualified on customer creditworthiness), equipment financing through Conserv Capital and Cultiva Financial, DSCR real estate loans, and MCAs from specialty providers.

Conserv Capital is the most accessible specialized equipment lender for ITIN holders in 2026. Equipment financing runs from $10,000 to $2,000,000 for construction companies, with flexible terms for contractors across all credit levels. They accept ITIN numbers and don’t require an SSN. What they do need: your ITIN, business documentation, and proof of your ability to repay. Their focus is construction, manufacturing, and food and beverage.

Conserv is a direct lender that retains leases and loans in-house rather than selling them off, which matters if your needs change over time. Their stated focus is getting experienced contractors to work even when those contractors wouldn’t qualify at a traditional bank.

Accion Opportunity Fund also finances equipment, including commercial trucks ($5,000-$250,000) and food trucks ($5,000-$200,000, APR 14.50%-16.50%). Accion charges simple interest on its term loans. Rates start at 8.49% for the most qualified borrowers and can reach 28.99% for borrowers with weaker credit or shorter operating histories.

What does equipment financing actually cost by credit tier?

Equipment financing rates in 2026 by credit tier run approximately 6%-10% APR for excellent credit (720+), 8%-14% for good credit (680-719), 12%-20% for fair credit (640-679), 18%-28% for challenged credit (600-639), and 25% or higher for those under 600 or startups, where lenders often use asset-based pricing instead.

What documents does an ITIN business owner need to apply?

A question we hear often: the application process for a CDFI line of credit or equipment loan is different from a bank’s. Here is a realistic document checklist based on what DreamSpring, Ascendus, Accion, and Conserv Capital actually require.

Identity documents (all lenders):

  • IRS ITIN assignment letter (Form CP565)
  • Valid foreign passport, consular ID (matrícula consular), or national ID card
  • EIN confirmation letter from the IRS (free to obtain at irs.gov)

Business documents (most lenders):

  • 12 months of business bank statements (some accept 6 months for newer businesses)
  • Most recent 1-2 years of business tax returns (if filed)
  • Business license or registration documents
  • Proof of business address (utility bill, lease agreement)

Financial documents (varies by loan size):

  • Profit and loss statement (required by Accion for loans over $50,000)
  • Accounts receivable or active contracts (especially helpful for equipment financing, as they demonstrate repayment ability)
  • Invoices or quotes for the equipment being financed (required by Conserv Capital and Accion for equipment loans)

The documents that cause the most delays are tax returns, especially for borrowers who have been filing with an ITIN for fewer than two years. If you’re missing returns, lead with your bank statements and any existing contracts. DreamSpring and Kiva are the most flexible on this point.

Kiva U.S. offers 0% interest loans up to $15,000 with no credit score requirement and no SSN needed. The catch: Kiva is crowdfunded. A community of lenders has to fund your campaign over a 15-30 day window before you see any money. It’s not fast capital, but it’s the single best starting point for an ITIN holder with no U.S. credit history.

At Ascendus, credit is just one factor. If your credit isn’t well established or has some damage, they look for strengths elsewhere in your business or finances to make the loan work.

For equipment financing with a 575-600 score, expect a down payment of 20%-30% rather than the 10%-15% available at higher scores. The down payment reduces the lender’s risk by lowering the loan-to-value ratio on the equipment. Conserv Capital takes a different approach and puts more weight on your business potential than on your documentation alone.

What does a realistic ITIN business line of credit application timeline look like?

This one comes up a lot: many borrowers are surprised by how different the timeline is from a bank product. Here’s a realistic step-by-step.

Week 1: Prepare Gather your CP565 ITIN letter, EIN confirmation, passport, and 12 months of business bank statements. If you’ve filed taxes with your ITIN, download your transcripts from irs.gov. Open a dedicated business checking account if you haven’t already; nearly every CDFI requires one.

Week 2: Prequalify (no hard credit pull) Apply to DreamSpring and Ascendus at the same time. Both offer soft-pull prequalification, and you’ll get an initial decision with rate and term options within 24-72 hours. Don’t apply to Camino Financial until you’ve checked CDFI options first; their rates (32%-45% APR) are significantly higher.

Week 3: Submit full application Once you’ve chosen a lender, send the complete document package. DreamSpring can fund within 24 hours of final approval. Ascendus typically takes 1-2 weeks from full submission to funding.

Month 1-12: Draw and repay strategically On a revolving line, only draw what you need and repay as fast as your cash flow allows. Because Ascendus loans are personally guaranteed, your payment activity affects your personal credit history directly. That helps small business owners build or strengthen their personal credit profile, which matters a lot for future financing. Treat the first 12 months as a credit-building exercise, not just a funding exercise.

Month 13-24: Graduate to larger products After 12-24 months of on-time payments, your ITIN credit file will show both installment and revolving history. At that point you can approach Accion for a term loan at better rates, or go back to DreamSpring for a larger line or a commercial real estate loan.

Are there ITIN business line of credit options in Texas, California, and Florida?

Readers frequently ask: state-level availability matters because some CDFIs are regional.

Texas: LiftFund is headquartered in San Antonio and covers Texas and 12 neighboring states, offering loans from $500 to $1 million for underserved business owners. DreamSpring also covers Texas within its 27-state footprint. The Jewish Free Loan Association, in partnership with KAFLA, offers interest-free loans up to $50,000 and accepts applicants with either an SSN or an ITIN.

California: Working Solutions CDFI serves immigrant entrepreneurs with no minimums. Accion Opportunity Fund operates in California with its full product range. DreamSpring also covers California. Immigrants start about 25% of new businesses nationally, and in California that share exceeds 40%.

Florida: DreamSpring covers Florida, and Ascendus operates nationwide (except Vermont), so both are available to Florida-based ITIN holders. Accion Opportunity Fund does not currently lend to businesses in Washington D.C., Montana, North Dakota, South Dakota, Tennessee, or Vermont, which means Florida is covered.

If you’re in one of these three states, you have access to the full roster of national CDFIs. Consider reading our state-specific guides: ITIN business loans in Texas, ITIN business loans in Florida, and our broader ITIN business loans overview.

What is the true cost of an ITIN line of credit versus what the rate says?

A question we hear often: the stated interest rate and the actual cost of borrowing can differ a lot once you factor in origination fees. Here’s how to read the numbers honestly.

DreamSpring Power Line of Credit example:

  • Draw: $30,000
  • Interest rate: 13% (midpoint)
  • Community benefit fee: 7% (midpoint of 4%-10%) = $2,100 charged at draw
  • 12-month revolving draw, paid down over the period
  • Total interest on $30,000 for 12 months at 13%: approximately $3,900
  • Total cost for the year: approximately $6,000 on a $30,000 draw
  • Effective cost: roughly 20% all-in for a 12-month draw

DreamSpring Small Business Term Loan example (for comparison):

Small Business Loans run $1,000 to $250,000+ at 7%-18.99% interest, with a community benefit fee of 2.5%-10%, over 24 to 72 months. A 3-year term loan at 10% with a 4% fee on $50,000 costs approximately $8,000 in fees and interest, or about 16% annualized. For a lump-sum investment like equipment or a buildout, the term loan is usually cheaper per dollar than the line.

The CDFI origination fee (DreamSpring calls it a “community benefit fee,” Ascendus calls it a “closing fee”) is the biggest hidden cost for borrowers who are only watching the interest rate. Factor it into every comparison you make.

What about Camino Financial? Is it worth the higher rate?

Camino Financial is an AI-powered lending platform and the first neo-CDFI built to serve underbanked microbusinesses, Latinx owners, credit-invisible individuals, ITIN holders, and minority-owned businesses.

Its main advantages: faster approvals (prequalification within 72 hours), no collateral required, and loan amounts up to $400,000 that exceed most CDFI caps. Interest rates run 32%-45% APR with a 5% origination fee, no prepayment penalties, and fixed terms of 24-60 months.

Minimum requirements are a 670 credit score, at least 12 months in business, and $30,000 in annual revenue.

Our editorial take: Camino Financial is worth considering only if you’ve already been declined by DreamSpring and Accion, or if you need a larger amount faster than a nonprofit CDFI can deliver. At 32%-45% APR, borrowing is expensive. A $50,000 loan at 40% APR over 36 months costs approximately $37,000 in total interest, more than double what DreamSpring charges on a comparable loan. If your business can service that debt, fine. If not, wait until you qualify for a cheaper product.

How do immigrant-owned businesses fit into the larger U.S. economy?

The numbers are worth knowing. Nearly 46% of Fortune 500 companies in 2026 (229 out of 500) were founded by immigrants or their children, and those companies generated $9.8 trillion in revenue in fiscal year 2025.

At the small-business level, 20% of all small employer firms and 25% of non-employer firms are immigrant-owned. Immigrants start about 25% of all new businesses in the country.

The lending gap for this group is real. New business formation by immigrants ran at more than twice the rate of U.S.-born workers, and 10.5% of the immigrant workforce were business owners, compared to 9.3% of U.S.-born workers. Many of the fastest-growing businesses in that group are in construction, food service, transportation, and manufacturing, exactly the industries where equipment financing matters most. Closing that lending gap is the entire point of the CDFIs and specialty lenders listed in this guide.

Can I get a business line of credit with an ITIN and no SSN?

Which lenders offer equipment financing to ITIN holders?

Conserv Capital ($10,000-$2,000,000 for construction, manufacturing, and food businesses) and Cultiva Financial both accept ITINs for equipment financing. Accion Opportunity Fund also funds commercial truck and food-truck equipment loans for ITIN holders.

Are SBA loans available to ITIN holders in 2026?

No. As of March 1, 2026, SBA Policy Notice 5000-876441 requires all business owners to be U.S. citizens or nationals. ITIN holders are excluded from all SBA-guaranteed programs under the current rule.

What credit score do I need for an ITIN business line of credit?

DreamSpring requires 600+, Ascendus requires 575+, and Kiva has no minimum credit score. For equipment financing through specialty lenders, most require 600-640+ with a 15%-25% down payment if your score is in the fair range.

How much revenue do I need to qualify?

It varies by lender. Accion Opportunity Fund requires at least $100,000 in annual revenue with 12 months in business. DreamSpring evaluates revenue on a case-by-case basis. Camino Financial requires $30,000+ annually with 12 months in business. Kiva has no minimum revenue requirement.

What documents do ITIN holders need to apply?

Typically: your ITIN assignment letter (IRS Form CP565), a valid foreign passport or consular ID, your EIN confirmation letter, 12 months of business bank statements, last 1-2 years of tax returns if available, and a basic business plan or revenue summary.

How fast can I get funded?

DreamSpring funds within 24 hours of approval. Accion Opportunity Fund averages 6 business days. Ascendus typically takes 1-2 weeks. Conserv Capital offers same-day structure on clean equipment financing files.

Can I use a business line of credit or equipment loan to build U.S. business credit?

Yes, and it’s one of the best reasons to borrow. Ascendus reports all loan payments to personal credit bureaus. DreamSpring repayment also builds credit history. Timely repayment over 12-24 months can raise your score enough to qualify for traditional bank products later.

What is the difference between a business line of credit and a term loan for ITIN holders?

A term loan gives you a lump sum repaid in fixed monthly installments. A business line of credit is revolving: you draw what you need, repay it, and draw again. Lines of credit are better for unpredictable cash flow needs; term loans are usually cheaper per dollar for one-time capital investments like equipment or expansion.

Does Camino Financial accept ITIN for business loans?

Yes. Camino Financial is a neo-CDFI that explicitly serves ITIN holders and Latinx entrepreneurs. Loan amounts run $5,000-$400,000 with terms of 24-60 months. Rates run 32%-45% APR, which is higher than nonprofit CDFIs, so exhaust DreamSpring and Accion first before applying.
