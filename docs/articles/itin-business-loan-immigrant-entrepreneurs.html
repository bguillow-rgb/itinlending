ITIN Business Loans: Who Lends & What They

Quick Answer

ITIN holders can qualify for business loans in 2026 through CDFIs, nonprofit microlenders, and community credit unions, even though SBA-guaranteed programs now require U.S. citizenship. Verified ITIN-accepting lenders include Accion Opportunity Fund, DreamSpring, Kiva, and several mission-driven credit unions, with loan amounts from $1,000 to $500,000.

This guide covers what those lenders actually require, what they charge, and how to build a capital stack from your first $5,000 to $500,000.

What happened to SBA loans for ITIN holders?

On February 2, 2026, the SBA issued a policy notice announcing revisions to Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) 50 10 8, prompted by Executive Order 14159. The updated policy took effect March 1, 2026, and significantly narrowed borrower eligibility.

Under SBA Policy Notice 5000-876441, SBA rules now require that 100% of all direct and indirect owners of a small business applicant be U.S. citizens or U.S. nationals who maintain their principal residence in the United States, its territories, or possessions.

Legal Permanent Residents can no longer hold any ownership interest in an SBA loan applicant. The business is also ineligible if any owner or guarantor is a foreign national, asylum seeker or refugee, visa holder or nonimmigrant alien, or DACA recipient.

This is a hard wall, not a gray area. In fiscal year 2025, the SBA approved over 3,300 loans to businesses partially owned by lawful permanent residents. Those borrowers would not qualify under current rules. ITIN holders were never the primary SBA audience, but the complete closure of the program to any non-citizen owner eliminates even workaround structures.

The practical consequence: immigrant entrepreneurs who previously relied on SBA-backed lending now need private lenders, nonprofit microloans, and other alternatives.

So where can I actually get a business loan with an ITIN?

A question we hear often:

The answer is CDFIs, and the market is more developed than most people realize. CDFIs are mission-driven, federally certified lenders that underwrite for cash flow and character rather than FICO score alone, which is why they dominate this space. The best CDFI for an ITIN-only or thin-credit immigrant founder is DreamSpring: it accepts ITINs across all of its loan products in the 27 states it serves and offers a no-collateral option under $20,000.

With SBA-backed microloans now restricted to citizens, private microloans from community lenders and nonprofits have become the primary small-loan option for immigrant entrepreneurs. These lenders set their own eligibility criteria, and many do not impose citizenship requirements.

The national CDFI landscape that explicitly accepts ITIN holders is anchored by a handful of names. DreamSpring covers 27 states. Working Solutions CDFI in California serves immigrant entrepreneurs with no minimums. Accion Opportunity Fund operates in 44-plus states. Grameen America has served immigrant women since its founding.

Which lender should I start with?

This one comes up a lot:

The right starting lender depends on how much you need and what you can document.

Kiva (up to $15,000, 0% interest): A Kiva business loan is a crowdfunded microloan with 0% interest, no fees, and no minimum credit score. For entrepreneurs who cannot qualify for traditional bank financing or who want to avoid high interest rates, it is a genuine alternative built on community support rather than profit. Kiva’s underwriting is based largely on “social underwriting,” meaning your ability to demonstrate community trust and support rather than traditional financial metrics. The tradeoff is time: the crowdfunding phase typically adds two to six weeks to funding. For an ITIN holder with no U.S. credit history and minimal documentation, Kiva is the single best starting point.

Accion Opportunity Fund (up to $250,000, starting at 9.99% APR): Accion is one of the few nonprofit CDFI microlenders that will approve borrowers with credit scores as low as 600 and APRs starting at 9.99%, which is competitive for this end of the lending market. It accepts an ITIN in place of a Social Security number for certain applicants, making it accessible to immigrant entrepreneurs who have not yet obtained an SSN but have an established business and some financial track record. One drawback: a 3%-5.99% origination fee is deducted from proceeds, and funding realistically takes 3-7 business days rather than the advertised 48 hours.

DreamSpring (up to $500,000, line of credit available): DreamSpring considers applications from individuals with an ITIN rather than an SSN. Applicants should confirm they operate in one of DreamSpring’s 27 eligible states. For revolving capital needs, it offers a Power Line of Credit from $1,000 to $100,000 with a 24-month revolving term at 9.99%-18.74%. One caution: some borrowers have reported interest rates on certain term loan products well above what was advertised, in some cases as high as 66%, so read the full loan agreement before signing.

How much does an ITIN business loan actually cost?

A question we hear often:

Cost is the honest conversation most guides skip.

0% interest (Kiva): The floor of the market. Kiva offers 0% interest loans up to $15,000 with no credit score requirement. You pay back only the principal. The real cost is the three to six weeks it takes to crowd-fund the loan and the effort of recruiting your first lenders from your personal network.

9.99%-18.74% APR (Accion Opportunity Fund, DreamSpring line of credit): This is mission-rate territory, comparable to a decent personal credit card. Accion advertises a starting rate of 9.99%, though higher rates apply to riskier profiles. Factor in Accion’s origination fee of 3%-5.99%, deducted from your proceeds before you receive funds.

18.75%-45%+ APR (some CDFI term loans, online lenders): Rates climb steeply when collateral is absent, credit is thin, or the loan amount is very small. Verify the full APR, not just the monthly rate, before comparing offers.

The editorial stance here is straightforward: for a first ITIN business loan, start with Kiva for amounts under $15,000, then move to Accion or DreamSpring for larger needs once you have a payment history. Avoid any offer above 35% APR unless you have no other option and a clear plan to repay within 12 months.

How do I build a capital stack from zero to $250,000?

Readers frequently ask:

The most successful ITIN borrowers treat funding as a multi-step ladder rather than a single application. A total capital stack of $375,000 in 24 months, starting as an ITIN holder with no U.S. credit, is achievable by stacking programs sequentially. Here is a realistic version of that path.

Months 0-6: Apply for a Kiva loan (up to $15,000, 0% interest). Use the proceeds for equipment, inventory, or initial working capital. Make all payments on time. At the same time, open a dedicated business bank account and start separating business and personal finances.

Months 6-12: With a six-month payment history on Kiva and documented revenue, apply to Accion Opportunity Fund or DreamSpring for a term loan of $25,000-$50,000. At this stage, a FICO score of 600 may be sufficient.

Months 12-24: With two established tradelines and 12-plus months of business bank statements showing consistent revenue, you qualify for larger CDFI loans or credit union business loans. A CDFI loan that reports to business credit bureaus creates a verified tradeline, which is what lets you graduate to larger financing.

Year 2 and beyond: At this point your U.S. business credit profile is real and documentable. Community banks, regional banks with ITIN-friendly programs, and business credit cards all become accessible.

Geography matters because many CDFIs are regional. Here is what is specifically available in the three states with the largest ITIN-holder populations.

Texas: LiftFund is headquartered in San Antonio and covers Texas and 12 neighboring states, offering loans from $500 to $1 million for underserved business owners. DreamSpring covers Texas within its 27-state footprint. National CDFIs such as DreamSpring, Accion Opportunity Fund, and Kiva operate alongside regional CDFIs including Justine PETERSEN and Pursuit. For ITIN borrowers in Texas, a combination of LiftFund for smaller local loans and Accion for larger amounts covers most needs. For Florida-based ITIN holders, Kiva (nationwide) plus Accion are the most consistent options, supplemented by locally chartered credit unions serving immigrant communities. What about credit unions for ITIN business loans?

Credit unions are an underrated channel for ITIN business lending. Red River Credit Union offers ITIN business loans covering working capital, equipment financing, and business lines of credit for entrepreneurs and small business owners. The limitation is membership: most credit unions require you to live, work, or worship within a defined geographic area.

The advantage over CDFIs is the relationship. Credit unions report to consumer credit bureaus, which builds your personal credit alongside your business credit, and once you are a member in good standing, credit limits can grow considerably. If you already have a bank account opened with your ITIN, ask that institution whether they have a business lending arm or can refer you to a partner credit union.

Do ITIN business loans help me build business credit?

CDFIs are often the only institutional lending option for ITIN holders, and a CDFI loan that reports to business credit bureaus creates a verified tradeline, which is the building block you need to graduate to larger financing.

Not all ITIN-accepting lenders report to Dun & Bradstreet, Experian Business, or Equifax Business. Ask before you sign:

  • Does this loan report to business credit bureaus?
  • Which bureaus specifically?
  • What name and EIN will appear on my tradeline?

Kiva typically does not report to bureaus. Accion and DreamSpring generally do, though policies can change. Confirm in writing. If your goal is eventually to access conventional bank financing, bureau reporting should be a non-negotiable feature of your first loan.

For a deeper look at building the credit file that unlocks those larger loans, see our guide on building credit with an ITIN.

What if my business is a startup with no revenue yet?

This is one of the hardest cases, and honesty matters here. If you have no revenue history, your personal credit score, business plan quality, and willingness to provide a personal guarantee will determine which startup loan options are available to you.

For pre-revenue ITIN entrepreneurs, the realistic options are:

  1. Kiva: No minimum credit score, no minimum revenue, no minimum time in business. Qualification is based on your story, community support, and a soft credit inquiry. Maximum is $15,000.

  2. Grameen America: Designed for women entrepreneurs with no prior credit. It operates a group-lending model where small cohorts of borrowers guarantee each other’s loans, eliminating the need for collateral or a credit score.

  3. ORR Microenterprise Development Program: The Refugee Microenterprise Development Program provides small business loans up to $15,000 through ORR-funded nonprofit organizations, targeting individuals who lack the credit history or assets to qualify with commercial lenders. This is available only to refugees and asylees, not to all ITIN holders.

  4. Personal ITIN loan used for business: A personal loan from an ITIN-friendly credit union or CDFI can serve as startup capital for very small operations. See our ITIN personal loan guide for that path.

The honest advice: spend the first 6-9 months building documented revenue before applying for a business loan. Lenders are buying your future cash flow, and the more of it you can document, the better your terms.

What is the application process and timeline like?

This one comes up a lot:

The process is simpler than a bank loan but still requires preparation.

Step 1: Gather documents (1-2 weeks if organized, 3-4 if starting from scratch). Collect your ITIN letter, valid ID, EIN confirmation, three to six months of business bank statements, and any business registration documents.

Step 2: Apply online (30-60 minutes per lender). Most CDFIs have fully digital applications. DreamSpring’s digital platform has reduced average processing times by 30%. Kiva’s online application takes between 30 minutes and an hour to complete.

Step 3: Underwriting review (2 days to 4 weeks depending on loan size). For amounts under $50,000 at most CDFIs, expect 1-2 weeks. Larger amounts can run 3-4 weeks. Loans under $50,000 are typically approved in approximately two weeks; amounts above that can take up to four.

Step 4: Funding. DreamSpring advertises funding within 24 hours after approval and charges no application or prepayment fees. Accion typically funds within 3-7 business days after final approval.

One practical tip: apply to two lenders simultaneously. CDFI applications are soft-pull or no-pull events, so multiple applications within the same 30-day window will not materially damage your credit file.
