Getting a home loan without a Social Security number is not a long shot in 2026. It is a documented, widely available product offered by regulated lenders across all 50 states. The problem most borrowers run into is not eligibility; it is knowing which lenders actually approve these loans, what they charge, and what they need from you before you apply. This guide puts all of that in one place so you can compare and move.

What rates and terms should I expect on an ITIN mortgage right now?

In mid-2026, ITIN mortgage rates are running between 6.87% and 8.50%. That range reflects the added risk and manual underwriting involved in non-QM files that do not meet government-backed standards.

To put that in context: ITIN loans carry a yield premium of 0.5%-2% over conventional 30-year fixed rates. If a conventional borrower is quoted 6.5% today, an ITIN borrower with a solid profile might pay 7.0%-7.5%. Someone with no U.S. credit history or a smaller down payment could land closer to 8.5%.

A few variables move your rate in either direction:

  • Down payment size: more equity at closing signals lower lender risk and earns a better rate.
  • Credit profile: if you have a FICO score, most programs require a minimum between 620 and 660 to qualify for competitive pricing.
  • Income documentation type: full-doc (tax returns) typically prices better than bank-statement-only loans, which in turn price better than profit-and-loss-only submissions.
  • Loan term and amount: loan amounts typically range from $100,000 to $1 million, with fixed-rate and adjustable-rate options available, and 15- and 30-year terms being most common.

There is a structural reason ITIN rates stay elevated. Conventional and government-backed loans require Social Security numbers and can be sold to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, or insured by federal agencies. ITIN mortgages do not meet those criteria, so they fall into the non-QM category and are typically held by lenders or sold to private investors. Because lenders cannot easily offload that risk to the secondary market, they price for it upfront.

How does First National Bank of America’s ITIN program work?

First National Bank of America is a non-QM mortgage lender licensed in all 50 states and one of the most frequently cited options for ITIN borrowers. Its programs were built specifically for this population.

FNBA has four distinct non-QM loan programs: “Alt-A Premier,” “Near Miss,” “FlexFirst HELOC,” and “Dual Core.” All four accommodate SSN and ITIN borrowers, and each can be obtained using alternative income documentation.

FNBA’s non-QM loan programs allow ITIN or SSN borrowers a path to homeownership when conventional mortgage guidelines do not fit their situation. Borrowers can qualify with 12 months of income using business or personal bank statements, profit and loss statements, seasoned assets, or traditional full-doc options.

FNBA’s published wholesale production topped $1 billion in 2024, which points to substantial volume and lender stability. Because FNBA does not publish its ITIN rate matrix publicly, you will need to work through a mortgage broker to get a live quote and verify current overlays. This is standard practice for non-QM lenders and is not a red flag.

What makes Quontic Bank different from other ITIN lenders?

Readers frequently ask why Quontic keeps appearing on best-of lists for immigrant borrowers. There are two concrete reasons.

First, structure: as a federally chartered digital bank and U.S. Treasury designated Community Development Financial Institution (CDFI), Quontic is one of very few mortgage lenders that can accept alternative income documentation from borrowers who have strong credit and a substantial down payment.

Second, documentation flexibility: Quontic’s ITIN loan program does not require tax returns, W-2s, or extensive bank statements to qualify. It also accepts 100% gift funds for down payment and closing costs.

The tradeoff is the down payment floor. Depending on which program you qualify for, the minimum is 20% down. For loans above $1.5 million, the minimum rises further. A loan up to $3 million requires at least 30% down and a minimum FICO of 740. Quontic is best suited for borrowers who have the equity but lack traditional income documentation.

Does Angel Oak Mortgage Solutions take ITIN borrowers?

Angel Oak’s ITIN loan program lets borrowers with Individual Tax Identification Numbers qualify for mortgages through a full-doc non-QM mortgage, with flexibility for individuals residing in the United States.

The program is available in 46 states and Washington, D.C., and runs through approved broker and lending partners rather than directly with borrowers. Borrowers may qualify using 12 or 24 months of bank statements or full documentation, subject to debt-to-income ratio and income verification requirements.

Eligible transactions include purchase, rate and term refinance, and limited cash-out refinance, subject to loan-to-value and credit guidelines. Angel Oak is a strong pick for borrowers who have a credit score and want a non-QM lender with institutional depth and consistent underwriting.

What about community bank and regional programs?

Not every ITIN borrower needs a national non-QM lender. Regional banks and credit unions sometimes offer better pricing because their programs are mission-driven rather than built around investor returns.

Sunrise Banks (Minnesota and South Dakota): Sunrise Banks’ Pathway2Home mortgage program is built for people with an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number. Requirements are concrete and publicly disclosed: borrowers must have at least one year of credit history, a minimum credit score of 670, two years of tax returns, and a picture ID. A passport, Matricula Consular, or driver’s license all work. The program requires a minimum 10% down payment and covers purchase and refinance of a primary residence. Down payment and grant assistance programs can be layered in to offset costs.

Simmons Bank (Texas and surrounding states): The Simmons Bank ITIN Affordable Advantage Mortgage is offered for qualified properties in Dallas, Fort Worth, Conroe, Houston, The Woodlands, Corpus Christi, San Antonio, Austin, St. Louis, Kansas City, Little Rock, Fayetteville, Memphis, Nashville, Oklahoma City, and Tulsa. The program is tied to Community Reinvestment Act (CRA) obligations, which often translates to more community-friendly underwriting.

Prysma Lending Group (Northeast and Texas): Prysma Lending Group has helped thousands of families since 2003 reach homeownership through ITIN/Tax ID loans and traditional mortgage products. Prysma serves Connecticut, Florida, Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York, Pennsylvania, and Texas, with products designed for first-time buyers, refinances, and more.

Does my down payment amount really change what I can borrow?

This one comes up a lot. Yes, meaningfully. The down payment on an ITIN mortgage does three things at once: it determines your maximum LTV, it signals creditworthiness to the underwriter, and it directly affects your interest rate tier.

The minimum down payment for an ITIN mortgage in 2026 typically runs 10%-25%. While 20% is the standard for most non-QM products, some programs accept 15% with strong alternative credit.

Property type and loan-to-value ratios will ultimately set your specific cash-to-close requirement. A two-unit or three-unit property will generally require more down than a single-family home, and investment properties typically require more than primary residences.

A practical illustration: on a $350,000 home purchase, the difference between 10% and 20% down is $35,000. That $35,000 also buys you a lower rate. If a 10% down scenario prices at 8.25% and a 20% down scenario prices at 7.50%, the rate difference on a $315,000 loan versus a $280,000 loan compounds into tens of thousands of dollars over 30 years. Running both scenarios through an amortization calculator before you decide how much to put down is worth the 15 minutes it takes.

Do not overlook closing costs. They typically run 3%-5% of the loan amount, so your true cash-to-close on a $350,000 purchase with 20% down can reach $80,500 to $87,500 total. Plan for that number, not just the down payment.

I have bad credit. Is an ITIN mortgage still possible?

Yes, though it narrows your lender pool and raises your rate. The key distinction is between having low U.S. credit and having no U.S. credit history at all.

No U.S. credit history: This is actually the more manageable scenario. A traditional U.S. credit score is not always required for approval. Lenders build an alternative credit profile using 12 months of on-time payments for rent, utilities, and insurance. FNBA has no published minimum FICO and accepts alternative credit references. Quontic’s flexible documentation program does not require W-2s or tax returns for income qualification either.

Low U.S. credit score (below 620): Options narrow. Most non-QM programs floor at 620-660. Some portfolio lenders and community banks will go lower with compensating factors, such as a 25%+ down payment, 6-12 months of mortgage payments sitting in reserves, or a co-borrower with stronger credit.

Building credit in parallel is worth doing regardless of where you stand today. See our guide on building credit with an ITIN for a step-by-step plan, and our article on ITIN mortgages with bad credit for lender-specific options.

Can I refinance an existing mortgage using my ITIN?

Readers frequently ask about refinancing, especially borrowers who bought at higher rates in 2023-2024 and now want to lower their payment.

Most ITIN mortgage programs cover purchase, rate-and-term refinance, and limited cash-out refinance, subject to loan-to-value and credit guidelines. The documentation requirements for a refinance are the same as for a purchase, and the same lenders that originate ITIN purchase loans also handle refinances.

One caveat specific to Simmons Bank’s ITIN Affordable Advantage program: there is no cash-out on refinances under that program. For cash-out refinancing, you would need to work with a non-QM lender rather than a community-bank program. For a full breakdown of your refinance options, see our article on refinancing a mortgage with an ITIN.

What is the fastest path to getting approved?

The most common reason ITIN mortgage applications stall is documentation gaps discovered mid-process, not initial eligibility.

ITIN loan approvals often fall apart for reasons that never came up in the first conversation with a lender. Down payment thresholds, how income is documented, and whether your credit references are considered acceptable can all change the outcome, even when you qualify in principle.

Five things that move your application forward fastest:

  1. Confirm your ITIN is active. An expired or soon-expiring ITIN must be renewed before you apply. See our ITIN renewal guide if yours is approaching expiration.
  2. File two years of tax returns with your ITIN before applying. This is the single most universal requirement across every lender in this guide.
  3. Open a U.S. bank account and maintain 2-3 months of documented statements. See our guide on opening a bank account with an ITIN if you have not yet done this.
  4. Assemble your alternative credit file in advance. Request 12-month payment histories in writing from your landlord, utility provider, and insurance company. Lenders will ask for these; having them ready prevents weeks of delay.
  5. Work with a mortgage broker who regularly closes ITIN loans. Brokers who specialize in non-QM lending have relationships with multiple ITIN lenders, which means they can match your specific documentation and down-payment profile to the program most likely to approve you. Because rates for ITIN loans can be higher, shopping around is a financial necessity, not an optional step.

Common questions

Can I get a mortgage with an ITIN number and no SSN?

Yes. No federal law prohibits mortgage lending to borrowers without a Social Security number. Dozens of non-QM lenders, portfolio banks, and credit unions offer ITIN mortgage programs. You will need two years of tax returns filed with your ITIN, verifiable income, a 10%-25% down payment, and proof of identity such as a passport or consular ID.

What are current ITIN mortgage interest rates in 2026?

As of mid-2026, verified ITIN mortgage rates fall between 6.87% and 8.50% for 30-year fixed non-QM loans, roughly 0.5%-2% above conventional 30-year rates. Your exact rate depends on down payment size, credit profile, loan amount, and income documentation type.

How much down payment do I need for an ITIN mortgage?

Most programs require 10%-25% down. The 15%-20% range is most common. Some community bank programs accept 10% with a strong credit profile. Quontic Bank's primary-residence program starts at 20% down with a 660 FICO. A larger down payment typically lowers your rate.

Do ITIN mortgage lenders check credit?

Many do, but credit requirements are flexible. If you have a U.S. credit score, most programs require a 620-660 minimum FICO. If you have no U.S. credit history, lenders build an alternative credit profile using 12 months of on-time rent, utility, and insurance payments, typically requiring 3-4 references.

Which states have the most ITIN mortgage lenders?

California, Texas, Florida, New York, and Illinois have the broadest selection of ITIN mortgage lenders. However, national non-QM lenders like First National Bank of America and Angel Oak Mortgage Solutions are licensed in 46-50 states, so options exist nationwide.

What documents do I need for an ITIN mortgage?

Standard requirements include your IRS-issued ITIN letter, two years of federal tax returns filed with your ITIN, government-issued photo ID (passport, consular card, or driver's license), proof of income (pay stubs, bank statements, or profit-and-loss for self-employed), two months of bank statements, and proof of residence such as a utility bill or lease.

Can an undocumented immigrant get a mortgage with an ITIN?

Yes. Neither citizenship nor a green card is required for an ITIN mortgage. Lenders care about tax-filing history, income stability, and down payment ability. Visa status is not a disqualifying factor with non-QM or portfolio lenders who specifically underwrite ITIN loans.

Can I use gift funds for the down payment on an ITIN mortgage?

Some lenders allow it. Quontic Bank explicitly accepts 100% gift funds for down payment and closing costs on its ITIN program. Angel Oak also permits gift funds for a portion of the down payment. Always confirm the specific gift-fund policy with your lender before applying.

How long does it take to close an ITIN mortgage?

Expect 30-60 days from application to closing. The manual underwriting required for non-QM ITIN loans adds time compared to automated conventional approvals. Gathering complete documentation upfront (tax returns, ID, income proof, alternative credit references) is the single biggest way to speed up the process.

What is the maximum loan amount for an ITIN mortgage?

Loan amounts typically range from $100,000 to $1 million through most ITIN programs. Quontic Bank goes up to $3 million with a 30% down payment and a 740 FICO score. First National Bank of America does not publish a hard cap, but amount eligibility depends on income documentation and LTV.
