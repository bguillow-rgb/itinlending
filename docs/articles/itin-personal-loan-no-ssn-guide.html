ITIN Personal Loans: Lenders, Rates & Docs

By Editorial Team · Loans, Mortgages & Credit · Published October 12, 2026

Advertiser disclosure:ITIN Lending is an independent resource. Some links on this page are from partners who compensate us, which may affect which products we feature and where. This does not change our editorial guidance and is never a cost to you. See our full disclosure.

Quick Answer

ITIN holders can get personal loans from credit unions (8%-18% APR), CDFIs like Mission Asset Fund (0% APR), and fintechs like Stilt and Oportun (up to 36% APR). No SSN is required. You need a valid ITIN, proof of income, a U.S. bank account, and a photo ID. Loan amounts range from $300 to $35,000.

Getting a personal loan without a Social Security number is not a workaround or a loophole. No federal law prohibits lenders from issuing personal loans to borrowers without Social Security numbers. Lenders need a way to identify borrowers and verify tax reporting obligations, and an ITIN satisfies that requirement. Whether a lender accepts an ITIN is a business decision, not a legal limitation.

The practical challenge is knowing which institutions have actually built ITIN workflows, what rates they charge, and what you need to walk in the door. This guide aggregates all of that into one reference.

Who actually qualifies for an ITIN personal loan, and what do lenders look at?

Approval for ITIN borrowers is usually based on income stability and bank account history, not U.S. credit score, which most ITIN holders do not have yet. APRs are often higher than for SSN holders because the lender is taking on more perceived risk with thinner credit data.

The profile that gets approved most often: a borrower with a valid, unexpired ITIN, 12-plus months of stable income shown through pay stubs or bank deposits, an active U.S. checking account with consistent transaction history, and at least one filed federal tax return. In practical terms, you typically need a valid ITIN, 1-2 years of filed tax returns, income verification through pay stubs or 3-6 months of bank statements, an active U.S. bank account, photo ID, and either a credit score around 580+ or 12 months of alternative credit history, with debt-to-income generally under 45%.

Research has shown that ITIN holders are less likely to default than consumers who use Social Security numbers, which is one reason community lenders and CDFIs have kept ITIN programs running even amid regulatory debate in 2026.

What APR should I realistically expect, and how does my credit profile affect it?

A question we hear often: “I have an ITIN but no U.S. credit score. Will any lender give me a reasonable rate?”

The honest answer: it depends on where you apply. Expect loan amounts of $500-$35,000, APRs between 15% and 36%, and terms of 12-60 months, with origination fees of 0%-5%. Credit unions with a Juntos Avanzamos designation often offer the best rates, around 8%-18% APR.

Rates improve with each loan as you build a credit history. Your second and third loans from the same lender will generally come at better terms than your first. That long-term relationship effect is a concrete reason to start with a credit union rather than a one-time online lender.

Legitimate ITIN lenders cap their rates at or below 36%. That 36% ceiling is your hard line. Walk away from any lender who quotes higher.

Which lender is the best starting point if I have zero U.S. credit?

This one comes up a lot, especially from recent arrivals who have been in the U.S. for less than a year.

Mission Asset Fund (MAF) Lending Circles are the clearest answer. Lending Circles are formed between 6-12 people, and the loan amount ranges from $300 to $2,400. Each month, a new member receives the loan until everyone in the group gets their turn. The loans carry 0% interest, and documentation needed to enroll includes proof of checking account ownership, an SSN or ITIN, a valid government-issued ID, and proof of income. No citizenship requirements apply.

The MAF model is based on the traditional Latin American “tanda” savings circle concept, formalized into a credit-building structure. MAF is a San Francisco-based nonprofit that facilitates zero-interest lending and simultaneous credit building. Their model links community members into rotating savings and credit associations, then reports participation to credit bureaus to help members establish or improve a credit score.

The limitation: MAF loans top out at $2,400 and require patience. If you need more than that today, Oportun is the next most accessible commercial option.

How does Oportun work for ITIN borrowers, and is the origination fee worth it?

Oportun has no minimum credit score and no credit history requirement. Loans run $300 to $10,000 with APRs up to 35.99%. Oportun accepts either a Social Security number or an ITIN, along with a government ID and proof of income and residence.

Oportun personal loans typically start at $300 and go up to $6,000 for new customers, $7,500 for returning customers, and $10,000 for secured loans. That tiered structure rewards repeat borrowers, which mirrors the credit-building logic of MAF Lending Circles.

The cost to weigh: Oportun charges an origination fee of up to 10% of the loan amount. On a $5,000 loan, that is up to $500 taken off the top before you receive funds. Factor the origination fee into your true cost of borrowing, not just the stated APR.

Oportun is available in AL, AK, AR, AZ, CA, DE, FL, GA, HI, ID, IL, IN, KS, KY, LA, MI, MN, MO, MS, MT, NC, ND, NE, NH, NJ, NM, NV, OH, OK, OR, PA, RI, SC, SD, TN, TX, UT, VA, VT, WI, and WY. That wide footprint makes Oportun one of the most accessible commercial options for ITIN borrowers across most of the country, including the key Spanish-search states of Texas, California, Florida, and Illinois.

What does Stilt offer, and who is it the right fit for?

Readers frequently ask whether Stilt is worth it compared to a credit union, especially when they have some employment history but no FICO score.

Stilt specializes in lending to immigrants, visa holders, and the underserved. H-1B visa holders and ITIN borrowers can check their rates on the company’s online platform without affecting their credit scores. The maximum loan term is 36 months, with loan amounts from $1,000 to $35,000.

Stilt considers education, employment, and visa status instead of a FICO score, and prequalification uses a soft pull only. That soft-pull prequalification matters: you can check your rate without touching any credit file you have started building. Interest rates run 8%-25% fixed.

Stilt lends in Arizona, California, Florida, Illinois, Massachusetts, Georgia, Pennsylvania, Michigan, Ohio, New York, New Jersey, Wisconsin, Virginia, Utah, and Texas, covering all three of the highest ITIN-density states (California, Texas, Florida) plus New York and Illinois.

The tradeoff: Stilt’s 36-month maximum term is shorter than what credit unions or Oportun offer, so monthly payments on larger loans will be higher. If you need $15,000-$35,000 with a 48-60 month term, a credit union is the better path.

What are Juntos Avanzamos credit unions, and how do I find one near me?

Juntos Avanzamos (“Together We Advance”) is a designation for credit unions committed to serving Hispanic consumers. It was developed by the Cornerstone Credit Union League as an immigrant outreach program in Texas.

The Juntos Avanzamos network includes 145 credit unions in 31 states, Puerto Rico, and the District of Columbia. Collectively, these credit unions serve 13+ million consumers through 1,250+ branches, holding $195+ million in assets.

To earn the designation, credit unions must meet specific standards: offering deposit and loan products for members with ITINs, providing bilingual services, employing culturally competent staff, and accepting alternative forms of identification. That means a Juntos Avanzamos credit union is, by definition, confirmed to accept ITINs for loan applications, not just accounts.

Credit unions with a Juntos Avanzamos designation often offer the best rates for ITIN borrowers, around 8%-18% APR. To find one near you, search the directory at juntosmovimiento.org or ask your local credit union directly whether they hold the designation.

What about Camino Financial for a larger personal loan?

A question we hear often: “I need $15,000-$35,000 for a business expense or major purchase. No credit union in my area will lend me that much. Is Camino Financial a real option?”

Camino Financial offers personal loans (and small business loans) for Latino borrowers, including ITIN holders. Personal loan amounts run $5,000-$35,000 at APRs typically between 18%-36%, with terms of 24-36 months. A bilingual application is available in Spanish, and funds typically arrive in 1-4 business days.

The cost is real. Business loan products run 32%-45% APR with a 5% origination fee, though no prepayment penalties apply. For personal loan products specifically, the 18%-36% range is more favorable than the business loan figure, but still meaningfully higher than credit union rates.

The 18%-36% range runs higher than credit unions or Stilt, so check those options first. That said, for borrowers who have been declined elsewhere and need more than Oportun’s $10,000 ceiling, Camino’s $35,000 limit and flexible underwriting can make it the right fit.

Camino’s underwriting uses a proprietary scoring model that weighs business revenue, time in the U.S., and banking activity more than traditional FICO. If you have a consistent income history even without a credit score, you have a real path to approval here.

Step-by-step: how to apply for an ITIN personal loan

This one comes up a lot, and the sequence matters because a hard credit pull at the wrong lender can hurt the thin file you are trying to build.

Step 1: Verify your ITIN is active. If your ITIN has not been used in federal declarations for several years, it may be expired, and that can complicate financial or tax matters. Renew through the IRS before applying anywhere. Check our guide on how to renew your ITIN for step-by-step instructions.

Step 2: Gather your documents. Collect your ITIN letter or card, your two most recent federal tax returns (filed with your ITIN), three to six months of bank statements showing regular deposits, a valid government-issued photo ID, and a utility bill or lease showing your U.S. address.

Step 3: Prequalify with a soft pull first. Stilt and Oportun both offer soft-pull prequalification. This lets you see rate estimates without touching your credit file. Check two or three lenders before committing to a hard pull.

Step 4: Apply at a credit union or CDFI first. Local credit unions and CDFIs tend to have the most flexible ITIN policies and the lowest rates. If you cannot qualify locally, fintech lenders like Stilt focus specifically on ITIN and immigrant borrowers.

Step 5: Compare total cost, not just APR. Two loans at the same rate can cost very different amounts once fees are counted. Factor in origination fees (Oportun’s 10% versus Stilt’s 0% versus a credit union’s 0%-2%) when calculating which offer is actually cheaper.

Step 6: Confirm bureau reporting before signing. Ask the lender directly: “Do you report to Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion?” Only a reported loan builds your U.S. credit score. MAF, Stilt, Oportun, and most credit unions all report to all three bureaus.

Does the 2026 CFPB guidance change anything for ITIN personal loan borrowers?

This question matters because of regulatory movement in mid-2026, and we want to give you a straight answer.

President Trump signed Executive Order 14406 on May 19, 2026. Rather than immediately creating a new nationwide banking rule, the Order directed the Treasury Department, CFPB, and federal banking regulators to take a series of actions over the following months. On June 8, 2026, the CFPB issued a Statement on Ability to Repay and Immigration Status. The CFPB explained that creditors may consider a borrower’s immigration status when evaluating whether the borrower will continue to have sufficient income to repay certain loans. Where information available to a creditor indicates that a person’s immigration circumstances could reasonably cause a future loss of U.S.-based employment income, the creditor may, in some situations, have to take that information into consideration when evaluating ability to repay.

Here is what that means in practice: the policy statement does not have the force of law. No major lender has publicly announced changes to its ITIN lending programs since the advisories were issued. Industry participants note that the guidance could raise questions about how lenders should weigh immigration-related risks while complying with fair lending laws, but so far the programs are running normally.

For borrowers, the most protective steps you can take right now are: keep your ITIN current, file your tax returns every year, maintain stable documented income, and keep bank account balances steady. These are exactly the factors lenders use to approve loans, and they also directly address any ability-to-repay question a lender might raise.

Can I refinance a payday loan or title loan with an ITIN personal loan?

Yes, and doing so can save you thousands of dollars. Stilt and Camino Financial both allow debt consolidation as a loan purpose. An ITIN personal loan at 18%-30% APR is dramatically cheaper than a payday loan at 300%-700% APR, so refinancing usually saves significant money.

Payday loans typically charge 300%-400% APR. Title loans put your vehicle at risk. Both products disproportionately target immigrant communities. Any loan with an APR above 36% should be a last resort; a legitimate ITIN personal loan should be well below that threshold.

If you currently have a payday or title loan, apply for a personal loan with Oportun, Stilt, or a local credit union and use the proceeds to pay off the high-rate debt immediately. Most ITIN personal loan lenders explicitly list debt consolidation as an approved loan purpose.

How does an ITIN personal loan build my U.S. credit score?

Readers frequently ask whether borrowing with an ITIN actually helps them build a credit file, especially if they eventually want to qualify for mainstream products.

The short answer: yes, if the lender reports to the major bureaus. The loan process, beyond providing financing, can build a credit history in the United States.

According to the CFPB, payment history is the single largest factor in most credit scoring models, so a loan paid on time every month for 12-24 months can substantially strengthen a thin credit file. A personal loan repaid on schedule for 12 months adds a positive trade line to your Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion reports. That record becomes the foundation for lower rates on your next loan. For more on the broader credit-building process, see our guide to building credit with an ITIN.

The fastest credit-building sequence for a new ITIN borrower: (1) MAF Lending Circle at 0% APR for 6-12 months, (2) a secured credit card at a Juntos Avanzamos credit union (see our ITIN credit card guide), (3) a small personal loan from Oportun or Stilt after 12 months of positive history. Each step adds a reported trade line and diversifies your credit mix.

What are the biggest red flags to avoid when shopping for an ITIN personal loan?

You can check lender complaints and verify licenses for free through the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau at consumerfinance.gov. According to the CFPB, immigrant and limited-English borrowers are disproportionately targeted by predatory loan products, so extra scrutiny is warranted.

Specific red flags to watch for:

APR above 36%. This is the clearest signal of a predatory product. Walk away.

Upfront fees before loan disbursement. Legitimate lenders deduct origination fees from loan proceeds. They never charge fees before you receive money.

No written loan agreement. Any offer communicated only by phone, text, or informal message is not a legitimate loan.

No state license. Every state requires consumer lenders to hold a license. Ask for the lender’s license number and verify it with your state’s banking regulator or at the NMLS Consumer Access database.

Pressure to use a prepaid debit card for repayment. Legitimate lenders accept ACH bank transfers or checks. Prepaid card demands are a common fraud signal.

8 questions ITIN borrowers ask most often

Can I get a personal loan with only an ITIN and no Social Security number? Yes. ITIN holders can get personal loans through credit unions, Community Development Financial Institutions (CDFIs), and some community banks. No federal law requires an SSN for a personal loan. Credit unions typically offer the lowest rates; CDFIs specialize in serving underbanked communities including ITIN holders.

What is the minimum income needed to qualify? Lenders do not publish a universal income floor, but most community lenders and CDFIs look for monthly income of at least $850-$1,200 to confirm you can make payments. Oportun does not disclose a minimum income figure. Stilt looks at the ratio of income to requested loan amount rather than a hard floor.

Can I apply online, or do I need to visit a branch? Stilt, Oportun, and Camino Financial are fully online. MAF Lending Circles require enrolling through a local partner organization, often in person. Credit unions vary: some accept online membership applications, others require an in-person visit to open an account and apply for a loan.

How long does it take to get funded? Oportun funds in 1-4 days post-approval. Camino Financial funds in 1-4 business days. Credit unions typically take 2-5 business days from application to deposit. MAF Lending Circles take several weeks because they require assembling a group of participants.

Will applying hurt my credit score? A prequalification (soft pull) at Stilt or Oportun does not affect your credit. A formal application triggers a hard pull, which typically drops a credit score by 2-10 points temporarily. If you have no U.S. credit file yet, there is nothing to pull, and the inquiry simply starts your credit record.

Can a co-signer improve my approval odds? Yes, at most lenders. A co-signer with a stronger credit profile can lower your rate and increase your approved amount. Credit unions are most likely to accept co-signers. Oportun does not offer a co-sign option. Check the specific lender’s policy before asking someone to co-sign.

What happens if my ITIN is expired? Most lenders will not approve the application until you renew your ITIN. Renewal takes 7-11 weeks through the IRS. Apply for renewal before you need the loan, not after you are declined.

Do ITIN personal loans work the same in Texas, California, and Florida? The federal framework is the same across all states, but state-level rate caps and lender availability differ. Texas has no personal loan rate cap, so Oportun’s full APR applies. California caps consumer loan rates at 36% for loans under $2,500, which aligns with Oportun’s cap. Florida also has strong consumer protection for smaller loans. All three states have dense Juntos Avanzamos credit union networks and are within Stilt’s and Oportun’s service areas. For state-specific detail, see our guides to ITIN personal loans in Texas, California, and Florida.

Common questions

Can I get a personal loan with only an ITIN and no Social Security number?

Yes. No federal law requires an SSN for a personal loan. Lenders including Oportun, Stilt, Mission Asset Fund, Self-Help Federal Credit Union, and Latino Community Credit Union all explicitly accept an ITIN as the primary identifier. Approval depends on your income, banking history, and any U.S. credit you have built.

What documents do I need to apply for an ITIN personal loan?

You typically need a valid ITIN, a government-issued photo ID (passport or consular ID), 1-2 years of filed federal tax returns, recent pay stubs or 3-6 months of bank statements, proof of a U.S. address (utility bill or lease), and an active U.S. checking account.

What APR should I expect on an ITIN personal loan?

APRs vary widely by lender type. Juntos Avanzamos credit unions typically offer 8%-18% APR, Stilt runs roughly 8%-25%, Oportun caps at 35.99%, and Camino Financial runs 18%-36% on personal loans. Mission Asset Fund Lending Circles offer 0% APR for loans up to $2,400. Avoid any lender charging above 36% APR.

What is the easiest ITIN personal loan to get approved for?

Mission Asset Fund (MAF) Lending Circles have among the highest approval rates because the program is peer-based, requires no U.S. credit score, and charges 0% APR. Oportun is the easiest commercial option: it sets no minimum credit score and accepts ITINs in most states.

Can I get an ITIN personal loan with no credit history?

Yes. Oportun requires no minimum credit score. Mission Asset Fund Lending Circles are designed specifically for borrowers with zero U.S. credit history. Stilt evaluates education, employment, and income in place of a FICO score. Expect APRs toward the higher end of each lender's range when your credit file is thin.

Does the 2026 CFPB immigration guidance affect my ability to get a personal loan with an ITIN?

The June 2026 CFPB statement on ability to repay and immigration status does not have the force of law, and no major personal loan lender has publicly changed its ITIN programs. Community lenders, CDFIs, and fintechs like Oportun and Stilt have continued operating normally. Staying current on your ITIN and having strong income documentation is more important than ever.

Which states have the most ITIN personal loan options?

Texas, California, Florida, Illinois, and New York have the densest networks of Juntos Avanzamos credit unions and CDFI lenders that accept ITINs. Oportun is available in roughly 41 states. Stilt covers about 16-19 states including CA, TX, FL, NY, IL, and AZ. Mission Asset Fund operates nationally through partner organizations.

Will an ITIN personal loan build my U.S. credit score?

It can, if the lender reports to the credit bureaus. Stilt, Oportun, Mission Asset Fund, and most credit unions report to all three major bureaus. Confirm bureau reporting before signing. A 12-24 month repayment record on a reported loan can significantly strengthen a thin credit file.

What is the maximum loan amount I can get with an ITIN personal loan?

Most ITIN personal loans top out at $10,000-$35,000. Stilt goes up to $35,000. Camino Financial reaches $35,000 on personal-style loans. Oportun is capped at $10,000 unsecured (up to $18,500 secured in select states). Mission Asset Fund is limited to $2,400. Credit unions vary but can reach $50,000 for well-qualified members.

Can I use an ITIN personal loan to consolidate or pay off payday loans?

Yes, and this is one of the best uses. Payday loans typically carry 300%-700% APR. An ITIN personal loan at 18%-30% APR can drastically cut your total cost. Both Stilt and Camino Financial allow debt consolidation as a stated loan purpose. Oportun also lists debt consolidation on its approved uses.
