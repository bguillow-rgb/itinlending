Illinois is one of the better states in the country for ITIN home lending, yet most ITIN holders in Chicago, Aurora, Cicero, and Elgin never find out these programs exist. This guide covers which Illinois lenders are active right now, what you will actually need to qualify, and how to keep your rate as low as possible without an SSN.

Who can get an ITIN mortgage in Illinois?

An ITIN mortgage is a home loan for buyers who use an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number instead of a Social Security Number. The IRS issues ITINs to people who need a U.S. taxpayer identification number but cannot get an SSN. That includes undocumented immigrants, certain visa holders, and foreign nationals who work and file taxes in the United States.

An estimated 1,988,000 immigrants live in Illinois, making up 16% of the total population. About 550,000 of those are unauthorized immigrants, representing 4.4% of the state population. A large share of those households pay taxes with an ITIN and are well-positioned to qualify for homeownership, yet most never hear about ITIN mortgage programs until they stumble across one.

ITIN mortgages are available nationwide, though availability varies by lender and region. Some states have more established ITIN lending markets than others, particularly those with larger immigrant populations. California, Texas, Florida, New York, and Illinois tend to offer a broader range of ITIN mortgage options.

What kind of loan is an ITIN mortgage?

A question we hear often: why can’t I just use an FHA loan?

ITIN mortgages fall under Non-Qualified Mortgages (Non-QM), meaning they do not follow the standard underwriting guidelines set by Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, or the Federal Housing Administration. Standard government-backed loans, FHA, VA, and USDA, all require a valid Social Security Number.

Because ITIN loans are non-QM, lenders keep them in their own portfolios rather than selling them on the secondary market. That gives portfolio banks more flexibility in how they underwrite income and credit. ITIN lenders use a manual underwriting process: instead of pulling a FICO score from a computer, a human underwriter looks at your full financial picture to assess risk. For borrowers with strong income and savings but a thin or nonexistent U.S. credit file, that is actually good news.

What are the requirements to qualify?

You will generally need two years of employment history, two years of tax returns, and a higher down payment than a conventional mortgage requires. Beyond that, most Illinois programs ask for:

  • A valid, unexpired ITIN issued by the IRS
  • Two government-issued IDs (passport, consular ID, state ID)
  • Bank statements covering at least 2-3 months of account history, proof of current residence such as utility bills or a lease, and employment documentation
  • Alternative credit references if you have no traditional credit file

On alternative credit: a standard credit report is not always required, but the lender may want proof that you pay bills on time, things like home utilities and cell phones. Many ITIN lenders accept references from rent, utility, and insurance payments in place of traditional credit scores. Generally, 3-4 alternative credit references with 12 months of on-time payment history are enough.

What down payment will I need in Illinois?

Readers frequently ask: is 10% really enough, or do lenders always push for more?

Most programs require at least 10%-20% down, and the amount you put down is tied closely to your credit profile. Borrowers with lower credit scores should expect to put down 15%-20% or more.

Here is why the Illinois housing market makes that math manageable for many buyers. The median home value in Illinois is $263,300, which is 21% below the national median of $332,700. At 10% down on a $263,300 home, you need roughly $26,330 plus closing costs. Compared to ITIN borrowers in California or New York facing median prices two to three times higher, Illinois buyers are in a much more accessible position.

One Illinois-specific advantage worth knowing: gift funds may be used for the down payment under several Illinois ITIN programs, including MidAmerica Bancorp’s product. Family contributions can count toward the required amount without disqualifying the application.

What ITIN mortgage rates should I expect in Illinois?

ITIN mortgages carry a rate premium over conventional loans because they are non-QM and stay on the lender’s books. Pricing on these programs runs about 1-3 percentage points above conventional, or roughly 8%-9% with Freddie Mac’s 30-year average at 6.55% in mid-July 2026. As of the week of September 15, 2026, the average 30-year fixed-rate mortgage in Illinois sat at 7% APR for conventional borrowers, which puts ITIN rates in approximately the 7.5%-10% range depending on your down payment and credit profile.

A few things that move your rate lower: a down payment above 20%, documented alternative credit with a clean 24-month history, and consistent income over two years, whether W-2 or self-employment. A credit score above 620 helps significantly. Shopping multiple lenders matters a lot here. Because ITIN programs are portfolio products, each lender sets its own pricing, and two quotes on the same file can differ by a full percentage point or more. For a broader comparison of rate ranges and program structures, see our guide to ITIN mortgage rates.

Step-by-step: how to apply for an ITIN mortgage in Illinois

  1. Confirm your ITIN is valid and unexpired. If it has expired, file Form W-7 with the IRS before starting the mortgage process. See our ITIN renewal guide for timing and tips.
  2. Gather two years of federal tax returns. You will need at least two years of filed federal tax returns showing consistent income. Tax returns are the primary income documentation, replacing the W-2 forms and pay stubs used in conventional applications.
  3. Pull three months of bank statements to document savings and down payment sourcing.
  4. Compile alternative credit references. Collect 12-24 months of payment history on rent, gas, electric, phone, or insurance accounts.
  5. Contact at least two Illinois-active ITIN lenders and request pre-qualification letters so you can compare terms before committing.
  6. Work with an Illinois real estate attorney at closing. Illinois is an attorney-state for real estate transactions, meaning a licensed attorney must review and oversee the closing. That adds a predictable cost (typically $500-$1,500) but also a layer of consumer protection.

Does my immigration status affect approval?

No. Some assume these programs are limited to undocumented immigrants. In reality, many ITIN holders have legal status but simply do not qualify for Social Security numbers. Others view ITIN loans as risky or illegitimate, even though regulated lenders have offered them for years. They are legal, mainstream non-QM products. Lenders evaluate your income, savings, and payment history. They do not report your file to immigration authorities, and holding a visa, DACA status, or no legal status at all does not automatically disqualify you.

With the 30-year conventional benchmark near 7%, ITIN borrowers typically see rates in the 7.5%-10% range depending on down payment, credit profile, and lender.
