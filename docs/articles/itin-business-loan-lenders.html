ITIN Business Loans: Best Lenders & SBA Rules (2026)

By Research Desk · Lenders & Loan Programs · Published July 27, 2026

Quick Answer

You can get a business loan with an ITIN through CDFIs like Accion Opportunity Fund ($5K-$250K), DreamSpring ($1K-$2M), and Kiva (0% up to $15K). As of March 1, 2026, SBA 7(a) and 504 loans require 100% U.S.-citizen ownership and are no longer available to ITIN holders or green card holders.

If you run a business and have an ITIN instead of a Social Security Number, 2026 brought a real shift you need to know about. New SBA guidance issued in February 2026 bars any small business not 100% owned by U.S. citizens from accessing agency-backed loan programs, including 7(a) and 504 loans, effective March 1, 2026. That one rule change closed the door on government-backed financing for millions of immigrant business owners who had previously qualified.

The SBA was never the only path, though, and the range of ITIN-friendly lenders is larger than most people realize. This guide names the real programs, their actual loan ranges, real rate data, and the exact requirements so you can stop searching and start applying.

Why did the SBA door close for ITIN holders, and when did it happen?

As of March 1, 2026, the SBA requires 100% U.S.-citizen ownership for 7(a) and 504 loans. This is a dramatic change from the previous policy, which allowed lawful permanent residents (green card holders) to qualify.

SBA loan participation is now limited to businesses owned entirely, directly and indirectly, by U.S. citizens or U.S. nationals who reside primarily within the United States or its territories. Any ownership structure that includes foreign nationals, green card holders, or qualifying individuals residing abroad makes the business ineligible.

The numbers put the impact in concrete terms. In fiscal year 2025, the SBA approved over 3,300 loans to businesses partially owned by lawful permanent residents. Those borrowers would no longer qualify under current rules. ITIN holders, who were already ineligible under older rules, are locked out the same way.

The SBA Microloan program is more complicated. Citizenship restrictions tightened in 2026, and the updated SOP 52 00 excludes green card holders, DACA recipients, and other groups previously eligible. That said, many SBA microloan intermediaries are also CDFIs that run their own non-SBA loan products, so contacting them directly is still worth doing even if the federal-backed product is off the table for you.

So what options actually work for an ITIN-only business owner in 2026?

A question we hear often: The answer is CDFIs (Community Development Financial Institutions), which are mission-driven nonprofit lenders certified by the U.S. Treasury, plus a handful of crowdfunding and fintech platforms. CDFIs are not backed by the SBA, so they set their own eligibility rules, and many explicitly accept ITINs.

Many CDFIs and nonprofit microlenders offer ITIN-only business loans specifically for undocumented or non-citizen immigrants. This is not a small or obscure corner of lending. CDFIs held over $25 billion in small business and microloans as of 2021, with annual loan originations more than doubling from $3.7 billion to $7.4 billion between 2018 and 2021, according to the Opportunity Finance Network.

The five lender categories that reliably serve ITIN business borrowers are:

  1. National CDFIs (Accion Opportunity Fund, DreamSpring, Kiva U.S.)
  2. Regional CDFIs matched to your state (Pursuit, LEDC, Accompany Capital, Working Solutions, Justine PETERSEN)
  3. State-chartered credit unions in high-immigrant-population areas
  4. Portfolio banks with ITIN programs (rare but they exist, see our ITIN business loan page)
  5. Crowdfunding platforms that carry no citizenship requirement

What is DreamSpring and does it really accept ITINs?

Readers frequently ask: DreamSpring is one of the most consistently ITIN-friendly CDFI lenders in the country, and it operates at real scale. Term loans run from $1,000 to $2,000,000, and they also offer lines of credit. You must have a Social Security Number or an ITIN.

Your business must be located in one of the states DreamSpring serves: Alabama, Arizona, California, Colorado, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Iowa, Kansas, Louisiana, Michigan, Mississippi, Missouri, Nebraska, Nevada, New Mexico, New York, North Carolina, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Washington, and Wyoming. That covers 27 states, including the three highest-density ITIN-holder states: Texas, California, and Florida.

On speed, DreamSpring can fund within 24 hours, charges no application or prepayment fees, and accepts ITIN-based applications. The main caution: some borrowers have reported interest rates far higher than those advertised, with a few experiencing rates as high as 66%. That is an outlier, not typical, but it means you need to read your specific loan offer carefully before signing anything.

How does Accion Opportunity Fund compare for ITIN borrowers?

Accion Opportunity Fund is a nonprofit lender focused on minority entrepreneurs, veterans, women-owned businesses, and ITIN-only borrowers.

Loans run from $5,000 to $250,000, with interest rates from 9.99% to 28.99%. That is meaningfully lower than many ITIN-accessible alternatives. The minimum credit score is around 620 according to NerdWallet’s data, which is achievable for ITIN holders who have been building credit in the U.S. (see our guide on how to build credit with an ITIN number).

Application review takes one to three business days, and once approved, funds are usually disbursed within one to four business days. That is a workable timeline for most business needs.

One notable limitation: Accion operates in most U.S. states, but loans are not available to businesses in Washington D.C., Montana, North Dakota, South Dakota, Tennessee, or Vermont.

Accion also provides mentoring, educational resources, and bilingual assistance in English and Spanish. Borrowers are not just handed a check and left to figure things out alone. For an immigrant entrepreneur navigating the U.S. financial system for the first time, that matters.

What is Kiva U.S. and is it really 0% interest for immigrant businesses?

This one comes up a lot: Kiva U.S. runs on a crowdfunding model, not a traditional lending model. It is a nonprofit that offers zero-interest microloans from $1,000 to $15,000 through crowdfunding, with no credit score requirement and no fees.

The tradeoff is time and effort. You create a loan profile and invite friends, family, and the Kiva community to fund it. It takes longer than traditional lending, but zero interest makes that wait worthwhile if your timeline is flexible. Kiva does not publish explicit citizenship or immigration status requirements, and in practice immigrant founders use it regularly.

Kiva works best for a business owner who has a 30-45 day runway, needs $15,000 or less, and can mobilize a small personal network to kick off the funding. If you need capital faster or in larger amounts, Accion or DreamSpring will serve you better.

What about regional CDFI lenders by state?

A question we hear often: For borrowers in specific states, regional CDFIs often have better terms and more flexibility than national programs because they understand local market conditions.

The CDFI landscape for immigrant founders includes Accompany Capital in NYC, LEDC in DC/MD/VA, Working Solutions in the Bay Area, Justine PETERSEN in MO/IL, and Pursuit across six Northeast states.

For New York-based refugee and immigrant founders specifically, Accompany Capital offers $1,000 to $350,000 with no minimum credit score and multilingual loan officers. The largest funding ceiling in the immigrant-focused CDFI space is Pursuit’s program at $500,000 across six Northeast states.

LEDC (Latino Economic Development Center) accepts ITIN-based applications for borrowers in MD, VA, DC, or Puerto Rico, with approval in roughly two weeks if the loan amount is in order. For Spanish-speaking entrepreneurs in those markets, LEDC also provides bilingual service.

Opening a dedicated business bank account is a step many ITIN holders skip, and it hurts their application. What if I have bad credit? Can I still get an ITIN business loan?

This one comes up a lot: Having bad credit or no U.S. credit history is common among immigrant entrepreneurs, and CDFIs are built for exactly this reality.

Accion focuses on underserved groups, including low-income households, people with disabilities, women, immigrants, people of color, and military veterans who often have trouble accessing traditional funding. Accion is willing to work with applicants who have little or no credit history.

A March 2026 Urban Institute study found that most CDFI microloan borrowers improved their business outcomes within five years of taking out their loan, including better consumer credit scores and greater business credit visibility. That means a CDFI business loan is not just short-term funding. It is a credit-building tool that opens better doors later.

If your credit score is very low (below 580), the practical path is:

  1. Start with Kiva (no minimum score required)
  2. Open a dedicated business checking account to build a transaction history
  3. After 6-12 months of clean repayment history on any loan, reapply to Accion or DreamSpring for a larger facility

Our guide on ITIN loans with bad credit covers this sequencing in more detail.

Do immigrant entrepreneurs actually represent a meaningful portion of U.S. small businesses?

The scale of immigrant entrepreneurship is often underestimated. Among new business owners in the Annual Business Surveys covering 2014 to 2019, the immigrant share is 24.2 percent, up from 18.7 percent in 2007 data. Nearly one in four new U.S. businesses is started by an immigrant.

In 2025, more than 46% of Fortune 500 companies (231 out of 500) were founded by immigrants or their children, according to the American Immigration Council. The capital pipeline that supports immigrant entrepreneurship at the small-business level, whether through CDFIs or alternative lenders, is not a niche concern. It is a mainstream economic issue.

Step-by-step: how to apply for an ITIN business loan in 2026

The process varies by lender, but this sequence applies to most CDFI applications:

Step 1: Confirm your ITIN is current. Log into IRS records or check your most recent tax return. If it has expired, file Form W-7 immediately. Processing takes 6-11 weeks.

Step 2: Obtain an EIN. Apply free at IRS.gov. You will receive your EIN immediately online. This is the number lenders use to pull your business credit profile.

Step 3: Open a dedicated business bank account. Take your ITIN, EIN, and business registration documents to a credit union or ITIN-friendly bank. See our guide on how to open a bank account with an ITIN number.

Step 4: Identify the right lender for your state and loan size. Use the table above. If you are in Texas, California, or Florida, DreamSpring covers all three. If you are in the New York corridor, try Accompany Capital or Pursuit. If you need under $15,000 and have time, start with Kiva.

Step 5: Gather your documents. At minimum: ITIN letter or tax return, passport, EIN letter, 6 months of business bank statements, business registration documents.

Step 6: Apply, then follow up. Accion reviews applications in one to three business days and disburses funds within one to four business days of approval. DreamSpring can fund within 24 hours in some cases. Kiva requires 30+ days of community funding.

Step 7: Use the loan to build your business credit file. Make every payment on time. CDFIs that report to business credit bureaus (Dun and Bradstreet, Equifax Business, Experian Business) will help you build the Paydex score that makes your next loan cheaper.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get a business loan with an ITIN and no credit history at all? Yes. Kiva U.S. has no credit score minimum and no revenue requirement. Accompany Capital in New York also has no stated credit score minimum. Both are designed for founders starting from scratch. The tradeoff is smaller loan amounts, closer scrutiny of your business plan, and sometimes a requirement for a personal reference or community trustee.

Is there a difference between using an ITIN vs. an EIN for a business loan application? Yes, and the distinction matters. Your ITIN identifies you personally. Your EIN identifies your business. Most lenders want both: the ITIN to underwrite you personally (checking your personal credit file, verifying tax compliance) and the EIN to verify the business entity. Applying with only an ITIN signals that your business is operating as an informal sole proprietorship, which is a red flag for many lenders. Incorporating as an LLC and obtaining an EIN before applying significantly improves your profile.

What industries do ITIN business lenders typically serve? CDFIs serve virtually every industry: food service, retail, construction, cleaning services, personal care, childcare, transportation (with some carve-outs), and professional services. Some lenders exclude cannabis, adult entertainment, financial services, and real estate investing. Always check the lender’s prohibited industry list before applying.

Can I get a business line of credit with an ITIN instead of a term loan?

DreamSpring offers term loans from $1,000 to $2,000,000 and lines of credit. The DreamSpring Power Line is a fixed-rate, up to 24-month revolving line of credit that business owners can draw on as needs arise. A line of credit is often more flexible than a term loan for businesses with variable cash flow, but it requires a stronger repayment track record to obtain.

Will taking a CDFI business loan affect my personal credit? It may. Many CDFI loans include a personal guarantee, meaning the lender can pursue your personal assets if the business defaults. A personal guarantee also means payment history may appear on your personal credit report. This can work in your favor if you pay consistently. See our page on how to check your credit score with an ITIN to monitor your profile as you repay.

If I am currently in DACA status, can I get a business loan?

Refugees still have access to dedicated federal microloans and training programs, nonprofit grants serve undocumented founders, and private lenders and crowdfunding platforms generally do not impose citizenship requirements. CDFI lenders generally do not ask about immigration status beyond confirming you have a valid tax identification number. DACA recipients with ITINs regularly receive CDFI loans. The SBA programs are now closed to DACA recipients as well as ITIN holders.

Common questions

Can I get a business loan with just an ITIN and no SSN?

Yes. CDFIs such as Accion Opportunity Fund, DreamSpring, Kiva U.S., and LEDC explicitly accept ITINs in place of a Social Security Number. Traditional banks and SBA-backed programs are generally not available to ITIN-only borrowers in 2026.

Are SBA loans available to ITIN holders in 2026?

No. Effective March 1, 2026, the SBA revised its rules to require 100% U.S.-citizen ownership for all 7(a), 504, and Express loan programs. ITIN holders, green card holders, and other non-citizens are now excluded from every SBA-guaranteed program.

What is the easiest business loan to get with an ITIN?

Kiva U.S. is often the lowest barrier: 0% interest, up to $15,000, no minimum credit score, and no collateral required. The tradeoff is a crowdfunding timeline of 30-45 days. For faster funding with larger amounts, Accion Opportunity Fund or DreamSpring are the next steps.

How much can I borrow for a business loan with an ITIN?

Ranges vary widely: Kiva U.S. goes up to $15,000 at 0%; Accion Opportunity Fund up to $250,000; DreamSpring up to $2,000,000 for established businesses; and Pursuit (Northeast corridor) up to $500,000. The amount you qualify for depends on revenue, time in business, and credit profile.

Do I need collateral for an ITIN business loan?

Not always. Kiva U.S. and Camino Financial (for SSN-holding minority owners) require no collateral. Accion Opportunity Fund places a blanket lien on loans of $50,000 and higher. DreamSpring may require collateral on larger loan amounts. Always confirm requirements before applying.

What documents do I need to apply for a business loan with an ITIN?

You typically need: your IRS ITIN assignment letter (CP565) or a recent tax return showing the ITIN, a valid passport or consular ID, business bank statements (6-12 months), business registration documents (EIN, articles of incorporation or LLC), and 1-2 years of business tax returns if available.

Can I get an SBA Microloan with an ITIN?

The SBA Microloan program was also impacted by the March 2026 rule change restricting SBA programs to U.S. citizens. However, many of the nonprofit intermediary lenders that distribute SBA microloans are also CDFIs that operate their own ITIN-friendly non-SBA loan products, so contact your local intermediary directly.

Does getting an EIN help me qualify for a business loan without an SSN?

Yes. An EIN (Employer Identification Number) is essential: it separates your business identity from your personal ITIN, allows you to open a business bank account, file business tax returns, and apply for permits. Most CDFI and alternative lenders require an EIN, not just your personal ITIN, when underwriting a business loan.

What interest rate will I pay on an ITIN business loan?

Rates vary by lender type: Kiva U.S. is 0%. CDFI lenders like Accion Opportunity Fund charge 9.99%-28.99%. DreamSpring rates vary and some borrowers have reported APRs exceeding 30%. Non-CDFI online lenders typically run 32%-45% APR. Shopping at least two to three CDFI options first is the single best way to minimize cost.

Can a sole proprietor with an ITIN get a business loan?

Yes, but requirements are stricter. CDFIs and microlenders are your best path. You will need to document business income separately from personal income, show consistent deposits in a dedicated business bank account, and in many cases provide 9-12 months of operating history. Incorporating as an LLC first often improves approval odds.
