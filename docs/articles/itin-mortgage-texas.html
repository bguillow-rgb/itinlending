Texas is home to one of the largest ITIN borrower populations in the United States, and the state’s mortgage market has responded with a real network of lenders, credit unions, and non-QM brokers who approve home loans without a Social Security number. If you have a valid ITIN, steady income, and savings for a down payment, buying a house in Texas is a concrete, achievable goal in 2026.

What exactly is an ITIN mortgage and is it legal in Texas?

An ITIN mortgage is a home loan where the borrower uses an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number instead of a Social Security number to establish identity and tax history. The IRS issues ITINs to individuals who are required to have a U.S. taxpayer identification number but cannot obtain an SSN.

These are completely legal products, established programs offered by regulated lenders, not fringe or predatory products. Because ITIN holders don’t fit the “qualified mortgage” (QM) boxes defined by federal rules, ITIN loans fall under a category called non-QM loans. That means the lenders keep these loans on their own books rather than selling them off, which gives them more flexibility. They focus on your ability to pay rather than a government checklist.

Some states have more developed ITIN lending markets than others, particularly those with larger immigrant populations. California, Texas, Florida, New York, and Illinois tend to offer a broader range of options. Texas consistently ranks near the top for ITIN loan volume, which means you have real lender competition working in your favor.

What are the actual requirements to qualify for an ITIN mortgage in Texas?

A question we hear often: borrowers want to know whether the bar is higher than for conventional loans. The honest answer is yes on the down payment, but the income and credit requirements are often more flexible.

These loans typically require a 10%-20% down payment, verifiable income through tax returns or bank statements, a minimum credit profile or alternative credit, and documented residency. Here is what each of those looks like in practice for Texas borrowers:

Down payment: most ITIN mortgages require between 10% and 20% of the property’s value, higher than the 3%-5% often available with conventional loans. Some lenders go lower on a case-by-case basis, especially if you have strong reserves.

Income documentation: W-2 employees with ITIN-filed tax returns typically have the most straightforward path. Self-employed borrowers can qualify with tax returns or a bank statement program, depending on which presents their income more accurately.

Credit: some form of credit proof is required, but a standard credit report is not always mandatory. Lenders may accept proof of paying utilities or cell phone bills as alternative credit history.

Employment history: you’ll generally need to show two years of employment and two years of tax returns.

Reserves: asset documentation confirms you have funds for the down payment and closing costs, plus enough to cover several months of mortgage payments if required. Lenders look for a clear paper trail showing where the money came from. Large unexplained deposits can delay approval.

What documents do I need to bring to a Texas ITIN lender?

This one comes up a lot. Getting your paperwork organized before you approach a lender is one of the most useful things you can do to speed up approval.

You will need proof of identification (a valid, unexpired passport or national ID card), proof of income (recent pay stubs, tax returns, W-9s, or other documentation verifying income and employment), and proof of residency (a lease agreement or utility bill showing your current address). You will also need the actual IRS letter or card showing your ITIN assignment.

If you are self-employed, bank statements can replace tax returns for income verification. Either way, bring at least two months of bank statements along with the rest of your file.

How much will my rate be compared to a conventional mortgage?

Readers frequently ask about the interest rate penalty for going non-QM. Expect a meaningful difference. ITIN mortgages are portfolio products: the lender cannot sell them to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, so they price in extra risk. In practice, rates on Texas ITIN mortgages tend to run roughly 1-2 percentage points above a comparable conventional 30-year fixed loan. If conventional 30-year rates are around 6.5% in your market, an ITIN program might price at 7.5%-8.5% depending on your credit profile, down payment, and loan size.

The spread narrows when you bring a larger down payment (20% or more) and documented credit history, even alternative credit. Borrowers with thin credit and the minimum 10% down typically see the widest spreads. The practical takeaway: improving your credit file and saving an extra 5% in down payment before applying can meaningfully cut your monthly payment over the life of a 30-year loan. Which lenders actually approve ITIN mortgages in Texas?

NQM Funding is a Texas-focused non-QM lender with a Foreign National Full Doc Program and specialized ITIN lending. AsertaLoans offers ITIN loans with a 10% down payment and a minimum credit score of 600 in Texas and Florida. Community credit unions and bilingual portfolio banks across the Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston, and San Antonio metros are also active in this space.

When comparing lenders, ask specifically about: minimum down payment, whether they accept alternative credit, their bank-statement income program terms, and whether they have Spanish-speaking loan officers.

Self-employed borrowers can qualify, but the lender will look closely at income stability, bank statements, tax returns, and overall file strength. Consistent deposits, solid reserves, and organized documents tend to make the process faster.

Step-by-step: how do I actually apply for an ITIN mortgage in Texas?

Here is the practical sequence our editorial team recommends based on how Texas non-QM lenders actually process these files:

  1. Confirm your ITIN is active. If your ITIN was issued before 2013 and you have not used it on a federal return for three consecutive years, it may have expired. Renew it before approaching any lender. See our ITIN renewal guide for the W-7 steps.
  2. Pull your credit report. Use AnnualCreditReport.com to check whether a credit file exists under your ITIN. If it does not, start building one with a secured card or credit-builder loan at least 6-12 months before you plan to buy.
  3. Gather income documentation. Organize at least two years of tax returns or 12-24 months of business bank statements if you are self-employed.
  4. Save your down payment. Target 15%-20% so you have negotiating leverage with multiple lenders rather than just the ones accepting 10%.
  5. Get pre-qualified with 2-3 lenders. Rate shopping within a short window (typically 14-45 days) counts as a single credit inquiry under FICO scoring rules.
  6. Submit your full application. Getting pre-approved helps you set your budget and shows sellers you are a serious buyer, which matters in competitive Texas markets.
  7. Review closing costs. Once underwriting begins, you will typically be asked to pre-pay for the appraisal, which can run $500-700. Budget for that plus title insurance, escrow fees, and origination charges.

Can I use an ITIN mortgage to buy a rental property in Texas?

A question we hear often from investors in Houston and Dallas. The answer is yes in some cases. Some lenders will review property cash flow, borrower income alternatives, or both when approving ITIN loans for rental properties. Most, though, treat investment properties as higher risk and will require a larger down payment, often 20%-25%, plus documented reserves equal to 6 months of payments.

If your plan is to start with a primary residence and later convert it to a rental, discuss that with your lender upfront. Occupancy requirements vary by program, and misrepresenting intended use is a form of mortgage fraud regardless of immigration status.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I buy a house in Texas with only an ITIN and no SSN? Yes. Texas has an active non-QM lending market. Portfolio lenders and specialty mortgage companies approve home loans using your ITIN as the primary identifier, no Social Security number required.

What is the minimum down payment for an ITIN mortgage in Texas? Most Texas ITIN lenders require 10%-20% down. Some programs start as low as 10% if your credit profile or bank statement history is strong, while lenders who require no credit score may ask for 20%-25%.

Do Texas ITIN mortgages have higher interest rates than conventional loans? Yes, typically 1-2 percentage points higher than a comparable conventional loan, because ITIN mortgages are non-QM products that lenders keep on their own books rather than selling to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac.

What documents do I need for an ITIN mortgage in Texas? You generally need your IRS ITIN letter or card, a valid passport or consular ID, the last two years of tax returns or 12-24 months of bank statements (for self-employed borrowers), recent pay stubs, and proof of Texas residency such as a utility bill or lease.

Can self-employed ITIN holders qualify for a Texas mortgage? Yes. Many Texas non-QM lenders offer bank statement programs where 12-24 months of deposits replace traditional W-2 income verification, which works well for self-employed or gig-economy ITIN borrowers.

Which Texas cities have the most ITIN mortgage lenders? Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston, San Antonio, and Austin have the highest concentration of bilingual ITIN lenders, reflecting the large immigrant populations in those metro areas.
