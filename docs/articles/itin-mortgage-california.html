ITIN Mortgage in California: Rates

By Editorial Staff · Research & Fact-Checking · Published September 4, 2026

Advertiser disclosure:ITIN Lending is an independent resource. Some links on this page are from partners who compensate us, which may affect which products we feature and where. This does not change our editorial guidance and is never a cost to you. See our full disclosure.

Quick Answer

Yes, you can get a mortgage in California with an ITIN and no Social Security number. ITIN mortgages are Non-QM portfolio loans requiring 10%-20% down, two years of tax returns filed under your ITIN, and a credit score of roughly 620 or alternative credit history. Rates run 1-3 points above conventional loans, and California has the largest ITIN lender market in the country.

California has more ITIN mortgage lenders than any other state, which means real competition and, for borrowers who know where to look, genuinely accessible rates. If you have been filing U.S. taxes with your ITIN and building a financial track record, buying a home in California is closer than you might think, even without a Social Security number.

Is it actually legal to buy a home in California with an ITIN?

A question we hear often:

Non-citizens, including undocumented individuals, can legally purchase property in the U.S. At the state level, California Civil Code protects the right of any person, regardless of immigration status, to own real property. No law restricts lenders from offering mortgages to non-citizens.

The practical barrier is not legal. It is lender preference. Most institutions only want to lend to borrowers with a Social Security number, and neither Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, nor the FHA backs these loans, which creates problems in the secondary mortgage market. That is why finding the right type of lender matters, and we cover that below.

One thing worth stating clearly: owning a home does not change your immigration status or grant residency. It is a financial decision, full stop.

What kind of mortgage can an ITIN holder get in California?

An ITIN mortgage is a home loan issued to a borrower who uses an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number instead of a Social Security Number. These loans fall under the Non-Qualified Mortgage (Non-QM) category, meaning they do not follow the standard underwriting guidelines set by Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, or the FHA.

The lenders who offer them are called portfolio lenders. They keep your loan on their own books rather than selling it to the secondary market, which gives them the flexibility to set their own underwriting rules. Some credit unions and community banks offer ITIN loans with more flexible terms than large lenders.

California is by far the most active state for this product. Community banks, credit unions, and Non-QM specialists serving Latino, Asian, and other immigrant communities are well established in Los Angeles, San Francisco, San Diego, and the Central Valley. That density of competition works in your favor when you shop for rates.

What are the requirements to qualify?

This one comes up a lot:

The core requirements most California ITIN mortgage lenders share in 2026:

RequirementTypical Range
Down payment10%-20% (some programs: 3.5%-10%)
Credit score620-660 minimum (or alternative credit)
Tax returns under ITIN2 years
Bank statements12-24 months
Cash reserves after closing2-6 months of PITI
Debt-to-income ratio43%-50% maximum
Property types coveredSingle-family, condo, townhouse

A minimum down payment of 10%-20% is typical, though the exact number shifts based on your credit score. You need at least two years of tax returns filed under your ITIN, demonstrating a stable income history.

On reserves: conventional and FHA loans often ask for 0-2 months, but ITIN programs want 2-6 months. Budget for that cushion on top of your down payment and closing costs.

On credit: no traditional score is not automatically disqualifying. Many lenders accept alternative credit histories, a 12-24 month record of on-time rent payments, for instance, or consistent utility bills. That kind of paper trail is often enough to get you in the door.

What interest rates should I expect in California?

Rates on ITIN mortgages are higher than conventional loans because lenders carry the risk themselves rather than offloading it to the secondary market. The gap is typically 1-3 percentage points above conventional rates in 2026, though some lenders quote a narrower spread of 0.5%-2%. Where you land depends on your down payment, credit profile, and the specific lender.

A few things that move your rate lower: a larger down payment, ideally 20%, signals lower risk to the lender. A credit score above 620 helps, and the higher the better. Showing 6 months of reserves after closing reassures manual underwriters. And shopping around matters more than almost anything else.

Individual rates depend on your credit score, down payment, loan amount, property type, and the lender. Get at least three competing quotes. In a state with as many ITIN lenders as California, there is no reason to accept the first offer you receive.

What documents do I need to apply?

Readers frequently ask:

Gather these before you contact any lender so the process moves quickly:

  • Your IRS ITIN assignment letter, plus a valid government-issued photo ID (passport, consular ID, or state-issued document)
  • Two years of federal tax returns filed under your ITIN (W-2, 1099, or self-employed returns)
  • 12-24 months of bank statements, with written explanations for any large cash deposits
  • Proof of income: pay stubs, employer letters, or profit-and-loss statements if self-employed
  • Alternative credit documentation: landlord letters, 12-24 months of utility payment history, or cell phone records if you have a thin credit file
  • Reserves documentation: bank or investment account statements showing 2-6 months of future mortgage payments

Lenders will compare your monthly debt payments (car, credit cards, loans, plus the future mortgage) against your gross monthly income to calculate your debt-to-income (DTI) ratio. The typical ceiling for ITIN programs is 43%-50% of gross income. Paying down existing balances before you apply can make a real difference here.

Are there any California state programs for ITIN borrowers?

CalHFA offers a 30-year fixed-rate FHA loan program that some first-time homebuyers can access using an ITIN, including Latino borrowers. These loans can be paired with down payment and closing cost assistance programs. CalHFA does have credit score requirements, though: a minimum of 660-680 depending on the loan type, plus completion of a CalHFA-approved homebuyer education course.

Program availability and funding change frequently. Confirm current eligibility directly with a CalHFA-approved lender before building any plans around a specific program.

For ITIN holders who do not qualify for CalHFA, private Non-QM lenders are the main path. Our guide to ITIN home loan lenders covers vetted options across the country, and the ITIN mortgage loan programs article walks through how each program type is structured.

How does the application process work, step by step?

The California ITIN mortgage process follows a familiar arc, with a few key differences from conventional lending:

  1. Check your credit. Pull your credit report from Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion. ITIN holders can do this for free at AnnualCreditReport.com. If you have no score yet, review our guide on building credit with an ITIN before applying.
  2. Assemble your documents. Use the checklist in the section above. Gaps here are the most common cause of delays.
  3. Get pre-approved with 2-3 lenders at the same time. Multiple pre-approvals within a short window count as one hard inquiry under FICO scoring rules. Compare loan amounts, rates, and fees side by side.
  4. Make an offer. A pre-approval letter from a reputable lender strengthens your position in California’s competitive market.
  5. Complete manual underwriting. ITIN loans go through manual underwriting rather than automated approval systems, which takes longer. Expect 30-45 days from application to close, sometimes more.
  6. Close. Most portfolio ITIN loans carry no prepayment penalty, so once you qualify for better terms, you can refinance without a penalty.

If you later obtain an SSN and build a conventional credit history, an ITIN mortgage refinance can bring your rate down considerably.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy a house in California without a Social Security number? Yes. There is no citizenship or residency requirement to purchase property in California, and no federal law bars lenders from issuing mortgages to ITIN holders. You use your ITIN in place of an SSN on the application.

How much down payment do I need for an ITIN mortgage in California? Most lenders require 10%-20% down. Some specialized Non-QM lenders advertise programs with as little as 3.5%-10% down, but those typically require a stronger credit profile or additional reserves.

What credit score do I need for an ITIN mortgage in California? A score of 620-660 is the common minimum. If you have little or no U.S. credit history, many lenders accept alternative credit: 12-24 months of on-time rent payments, utility bills, and similar recurring expenses.

Are ITIN mortgage rates higher than regular mortgage rates in California? Yes. ITIN mortgages are Non-QM loans and typically carry rates 1-3 percentage points above comparable conventional loans in 2026, because lenders hold them in their own portfolio rather than selling them to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac.

Does CalHFA offer programs for ITIN holders in California? Some CalHFA programs are accessible to first-time buyers who qualify via ITIN. Requirements include a minimum credit score of 660-680, completion of a homebuyer education course, and income within county limits. Availability changes, so confirm directly with a CalHFA-approved lender.

What documents do I need for a California ITIN mortgage? Typically: your IRS ITIN assignment letter, a valid government-issued photo ID (passport or consular ID), two years of federal tax returns filed under your ITIN, 12-24 months of bank statements, proof of income or alternative income documentation, and 2-6 months of cash reserves after closing.
