ITIN Mortgage in Texas With Bad Credit (2026)

By Editorial Staff · Research & Fact-Checking · Published October 2, 2026

Quick Answer

Yes, you can get an ITIN mortgage in Texas with bad credit. Non-QM lenders and community CDFIs approve ITIN borrowers with credit scores as low as 580, or with no traditional credit score at all, using alternative documentation like rent history and utility payments. Expect a 15%-20% down payment and rates roughly 1-3 points above conventional.

A bad credit score is not an automatic disqualification when you are pursuing a home loan in Texas with an ITIN. Non-QM lenders, portfolio lenders, and community CDFIs have built entire programs around this borrower profile, and Texas, with its large immigrant population and strong housing demand in Houston, Dallas, San Antonio, and Austin, is one of the better-served states for ITIN mortgage products.

What does “bad credit” actually mean for an ITIN mortgage applicant in Texas?

For most non-QM lenders operating in Texas, “bad credit” means a FICO score below 620. Some lenders draw the line at 580. Below 580, or when no U.S. credit file exists at all, lenders typically shift to alternative credit underwriting: rent payment history, utility bills, cell phone records, and insurance premiums paid on time.

One nuance worth understanding: many ITIN holders in Texas do not have a bad credit score. They have no credit score. Those are different problems with slightly different solutions. A thin or absent file is often easier to work around than a file full of collections or missed payments, because lenders can build a manual underwriting case from scratch using 12-24 months of alternative documentation.

What documents do I need to apply in Texas?

This one comes up a lot: applicants assume they cannot qualify because they lack the standard paperwork. The document list is different from a conventional loan, not necessarily harder.

Texas ITIN loan requirements typically include two years of steady employment, two years of tax returns (W-2 or 1099), a down payment of at least 10%-20% depending on the lender, and proof of paying recurring bills such as utilities or a cell phone. You will also need your ITIN card plus a state ID, driver’s license, or passport, and recent bank statements (some lenders want the last two months, others require up to six).

For borrowers with bad credit specifically, bring a written explanation of any derogatory items on your credit report. Lenders doing manual underwriting want to see that past problems had a specific cause, whether job loss or a medical emergency, and that your payment behavior has improved in the 12-24 months before you apply.

How much down payment do I actually need with a low score?

Readers frequently ask: whether a larger down payment can make up for a poor credit score when seeking an ITIN mortgage in Texas.

It can, and it is one of the most actionable levers you have. ITIN loans in Texas often come with higher interest rates and tighter down payment requirements, reflecting the perceived risk of lending to borrowers without a traditional credit history or permanent residency status.

Here is how it typically works out for ITIN borrowers in Texas in 2026:

  • Score 620 or higher: many lenders start at 10%-15% down.
  • Score 580-619: expect 15%-20% as a floor.
  • Score below 580 or no score: some lenders require 20%-30%, and manual underwriting is standard.
  • No traditional credit file at all: some lenders in Texas and Florida offer ITIN loans with a 10% down payment and a minimum credit score of 600, but those terms assume at least some verifiable credit. Borrowers with no score at all usually need more.

A larger down payment also reduces your loan-to-value ratio immediately, which often triggers a better rate even within the same non-QM program. On a $300,000 home in San Antonio or Houston, the difference between 10% and 20% down can translate to a real reduction in both rate and monthly payment.

What interest rates should I expect on an ITIN mortgage in Texas with bad credit?

ITIN mortgages for Texas borrowers with low scores carry rates above what a conventional borrower with strong credit would see, though the spread varies by lender type and score band.

In 2026, non-QM rates for ITIN borrowers in Texas generally run 1-3 percentage points above the 30-year conventional rate benchmark, depending on credit profile, LTV, and loan amount. Borrowers with scores near 580-600 tend to land at the higher end of that range. Those with alternative credit and solid income documentation tend to land lower.

One important piece of context: a 2024 report from the nonpartisan Urban Institute found that although there are roughly 5.25 million ITIN holders currently residing in the U.S., fewer than 6,000 ITIN mortgages were closed in 2023. That gap between demand and supply means competition among ITIN-friendly lenders is still relatively low, and rates have not seen the same downward pressure that conventional products do. Shopping at least three lenders matters more here than in almost any other loan category.

Can I use alternative credit references instead of a FICO score?

A question we hear often: whether a lender will count a spotless rent-payment history as a substitute for a formal U.S. credit score.

Yes, and it is one of the most practical paths for ITIN holders who simply have not had enough time in the U.S. financial system to build a file. Portfolio lenders and CDFIs accept manual underwriting based on non-traditional credit references. What qualifies typically includes:

  • Rent payment history: a landlord letter or 12-24 months of cancelled checks and bank statements showing consistent on-time payments.
  • Utility bills: electricity, gas, water, and internet accounts paid on time.
  • Insurance premiums: auto or health insurance paid monthly over a sustained period.
  • Remittances: regular international wire transfers documented through a service like Western Union or Remitly are sometimes accepted as evidence of financial discipline.

A standard credit report is usually not required, but lenders want to see proof of paying recurring bills. Compile 12-24 months of statements for every account you plan to reference before you start the application.

What steps should I take to improve my odds before applying?

Getting approved for an ITIN mortgage in Texas with bad credit is achievable, but timing the application thoughtfully makes a real difference. A practical pre-application checklist:

  1. Check your ITIN expiration date. An expired ITIN can stall or kill an application. See our guide to ITIN renewal if yours is expiring soon.
  2. Pull any existing U.S. credit report. If you have open accounts, verify they are reporting correctly. Disputing errors before you apply prevents surprises during underwriting.
  3. Open a U.S. bank account if you do not already have one. Two years of statements showing consistent deposits and no overdrafts is one of the strongest signals a manual underwriter can see. Read more on opening a bank account with an ITIN.
  4. Pay down existing debts to reduce your debt-to-income ratio (DTI). Most non-QM lenders cap DTI at 43%-50%. The lower yours is, the more loan you can qualify for.
  5. Save for a larger down payment. As noted above, 20% down is the single biggest approval lever when your credit score is below 620.
  6. Consider a credit-builder loan first. Even a six-month credit-builder loan with a credit union can add a positive tradeline that shows up on your report before your mortgage application.

Once you are ready to apply, get quotes from at least three lenders. ITIN mortgage programs are not standardized, and the difference in rate and fee structure between lenders can be significant, sometimes more than a full percentage point on the same borrower profile. FAQs

What is the minimum credit score for an ITIN mortgage in Texas? Most non-QM lenders that serve Texas ITIN borrowers set a floor of 580-620. A handful of portfolio lenders and CDFIs waive the minimum score entirely and instead verify 12-24 months of alternative credit, such as rent receipts and utility bills. No government-backed FHA or conventional loan is available to ITIN holders without an SSN.

How much of a down payment do I need for an ITIN home loan in Texas with bad credit? Plan on 15%-20% for a credit score in the 580-619 range. If your score is below 580 or you have no U.S. credit file at all, some lenders require 20%-30%. A larger down payment directly lowers lender risk and often unlocks better rates, so saving more before applying is one of the highest-return moves available to ITIN borrowers.

Can I get an ITIN mortgage in Texas with no credit history at all? Yes. Portfolio lenders and CDFIs in Texas accept non-traditional credit references: 12-24 months of on-time rent payments (verified by landlord letters or bank statements), utility bills, insurance premiums, and remittances. Lenders like Golden Lenders and ACC Mortgage advertise ITIN programs that do not require a traditional FICO score.

Do Texas ITIN mortgage lenders report payments to the credit bureaus? Not all do, so ask before you sign. Bureau-reporting lenders report to Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. Every on-time mortgage payment reported to a bureau helps build the U.S. credit file you will need for future refinancing at a lower rate. Once you have 24 months of positive mortgage history, you may qualify to refinance your mortgage with an ITIN at a meaningfully lower rate.

Does a 2026 executive order affect my ability to get an ITIN mortgage in Texas? On May 19, 2026, the President signed an executive order titled “Restoring Integrity to America’s Financial System,” which directs banks and their federal regulators to begin treating a customer’s immigration status as a factor when opening accounts, extending credit, and reporting suspicious activity. In practice, ITIN mortgages were already handled by specialized non-QM and portfolio lenders, not mainstream banks. The Urban Institute estimated that only roughly 5,000 to 6,000 mortgages a year were issued to ITIN customers, and those specialized lenders have continued to offer ITIN programs. Confirm current availability directly with any lender before applying.
