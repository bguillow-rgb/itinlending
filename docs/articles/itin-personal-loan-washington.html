ITIN holders in Washington state have more options for personal loans than in most other states, thanks to a dense network of community credit unions and CDFIs that explicitly accept an ITIN in place of a Social Security Number. Express Credit Union, a not-for-profit financial institution serving Washington state, has built a dedicated ITIN lending program, and it is far from the only one. This guide covers every realistic lender, what rates to expect, and exactly how to apply, whether you are in Seattle, Yakima, or the Olympic Peninsula.

Can I really get a personal loan in Washington if I only have an ITIN?

Yes, and Washington is one of the better states for it. ITIN holders can get personal loans through credit unions, Community Development Financial Institutions (CDFIs), and some community banks, because no federal law requires an SSN for a personal loan. What lenders in Washington care about is documented income, a verifiable address, and your ITIN letter from the IRS. Immigration status is not a disqualifying factor at the lenders listed below.

The ITIN itself, formally called the Individual Taxpayer Identification Number, is issued by the IRS to people who have U.S. tax obligations but do not qualify for a Social Security Number. It is nine digits and begins with the number 9. Holding one signals to lenders that you file taxes and have a verifiable financial identity, which is exactly what they need to underwrite a loan.

Which lenders in Washington state actually accept an ITIN for personal loans?

A question we hear often: Which specific institutions serve ITIN borrowers in Washington, and how do their terms compare?

Express Credit Union (Seattle area) accepts an ITIN and offers checking, savings, personal loans, a Visa credit card, auto loan refinancing, and credit-builder loans to Washington residents. It is one of the most immigrant-friendly credit unions in the Pacific Northwest.

NW Plus CU offers auto loans and personal loans to ITIN holders and carries Juntos Avanzamos accreditation, a national designation for credit unions that meet specific standards for immigrant financial inclusion. That accreditation is a meaningful quality signal, not just a marketing label.

WSECU (Washington State Employees Credit Union) accepts an SSN or ITIN for personal loans. Applicants need to provide their date of birth, address, employment information and income, and monthly expenses. WSECU personal loans run 10.24%-27.99% APR with a maximum loan amount of $40,000 and terms up to 84 months, with rates current as of November 1, 2026.

Peninsula Credit Union in Washington makes all its loans available to ITIN holders, serving people who live, work, worship, or attend school in Kitsap, Mason, Jefferson, Clallam, or Grays Harbor counties, plus family members of current members.

For borrowers outside these specific service areas, OneMain Financial accepts ITIN borrowers and has close to 1,400 branches across 44 states, while Embold Credit Union in the Pacific Northwest explicitly markets its ITIN loan program.

What APR and loan size should I realistically expect?

This one comes up a lot: Borrowers often want a simple number, so here it is. ITIN borrowers in Washington state with established income and at least some credit history should expect APRs in the 12%-22% range at credit unions. Borrowers with thin or no U.S. credit history typically land in the 18%-28% range, and specialty CDFIs like Capital Good Fund cap their programs at around 18%.

Credit unions and CDFIs generally offer the best rates (8%-18% APR). Fintech lenders approve borrowers with thin or no credit at 12%-36% APR, with loan amounts from $300 to $35,000 and terms of 6-60 months. Washington credit unions tend to cluster toward the lower end of that range because they are member-owned and not-for-profit.

One practical note: rates improve with each loan as you build a credit history with the same lender. Starting with a smaller loan at Express CU or NW Plus CU and repaying it cleanly is the fastest way to get better terms on the next application.

What documents do I need to apply?

Readers frequently ask: what exactly goes in the folder before they walk into a branch or start an online application.

Most Washington lenders ask for the same core set. Bring your ITIN letter or card from the IRS, a valid government-issued photo ID (passport, consulate ID, or state ID), two to three months of pay stubs or bank statements showing income, and a U.S. address. The official IRS letter (CP-565 or W-7 approval notice) is the primary tax-identification document. Some lenders also accept a Matricula Consular or state ID alongside it.

For credit unions, you will typically also need to open a share (savings) account and pay a small membership fee, often $5-$25, before submitting a loan application. This is standard across the industry and not specific to ITIN borrowers.

What if I have no U.S. credit history at all?

A question we hear often: Many ITIN holders arrived recently and have zero entries on a U.S. credit report. That does not automatically disqualify you in Washington.

Oportun has no minimum credit score requirement and accepts applicants with limited or no credit history. It operates in Washington and offers loans between $300 and $10,000, making it a practical starting point. CDFIs like Capital Good Fund similarly use alternative underwriting that weighs rental payment history, utility bills, and cash-flow patterns from bank statements rather than a FICO score alone.

If you want to build credit before applying for a larger personal loan, consider pairing a small ITIN personal loan with a credit builder loan or a secured credit card. Both report monthly to the credit bureaus and can generate a scorable credit file within 6-12 months. That file then opens better rates on your next personal loan application. For a broader look at the full credit-building path, see our guide to building credit with an ITIN.

How do I actually apply, step by step?

Start with a credit union or CDFI. These lenders have the most favorable terms for ITIN borrowers. Here is the sequence that works in Washington:

  1. Choose a lender whose service area covers you. Express CU focuses on the Seattle and King County area. Peninsula CU covers the Olympic Peninsula counties. NW Plus CU and WSECU serve Washington statewide and accept online applications.
  2. Join the credit union. Open a share savings account (usually $5-$25 to establish membership). Some credit unions require you to hold the account for 30-90 days before applying for a loan.
  3. Gather your documents. ITIN letter, photo ID, two to three months of pay stubs or bank statements, and proof of your Washington address (utility bill or lease).
  4. Submit the loan application. Most credit unions offer online pre-qualification that uses a soft credit pull and does not affect your score.
  5. Review and sign. If approved, funds typically land in your account within one to three business days at credit unions, and sometimes the same day at larger institutions.

According to the CFPB, borrowers who compare at least three lenders before accepting a personal loan save meaningfully on total interest costs. Submit applications to two or three ITIN-friendly institutions even if the first one approves you.

Does the loan build U.S. credit?

Yes, in most cases. WSECU, NW Plus CU, and Express CU all participate in standard credit-bureau reporting. Each on-time payment adds a positive entry to your Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion files, which over time creates the credit score that opens bank-rate personal loans, auto financing, and eventually home ownership. If credit reporting matters to you, confirm it with the specific lender before signing. A small number of community programs do not report.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a personal loan in Washington state with only an ITIN and no SSN? Yes. Several Washington credit unions, including Express Credit Union, NW Plus CU, WSECU, and Peninsula CU, explicitly accept an ITIN in place of a Social Security Number. No federal law prohibits lenders from issuing personal loans to ITIN holders.

What is the minimum credit score for an ITIN personal loan in Washington? It depends on the lender. WSECU and NW Plus CU set rates based on credit score but do not publish a hard minimum. Express Credit Union and CDFIs like Capital Good Fund are more flexible and accept borrowers with thin or no U.S. credit history.

What documents do I need for an ITIN personal loan in Washington? Most lenders ask for your ITIN letter or IRS CP-565 notice, a valid government photo ID (passport, matrícula consular, or state ID), recent pay stubs or bank statements showing income, and proof of a Washington address.

How much can I borrow with an ITIN personal loan in Washington state? Typical loan amounts range from about $500 (small CDFIs) up to $40,000 (WSECU). The amount you qualify for depends on your income, credit history, and the specific lender’s program limits.

Do ITIN personal loans in Washington report to the credit bureaus? Most credit unions and CDFIs report to Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion, so repaying on time helps build your U.S. credit score. Confirm reporting policy before you apply, especially with smaller community lenders.
