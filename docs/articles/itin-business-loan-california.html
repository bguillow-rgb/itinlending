Quick Answer

ITIN holders in California can get business loans through CDFIs and nonprofit microlenders including Accion Opportunity Fund, DreamSpring, Working Solutions CDFI, and Kiva. SBA-guaranteed loans are no longer available to ITIN holders as of March 2026, but these lenders offer $1,000 to $250,000 with no SSN required.

Why does California have so many ITIN business loan options?

A question we hear often:

California is not just the largest state by population. It has the most developed infrastructure for immigrant business lending in the country. Nearly one-in-five businesses nationwide are owned by immigrants, but that share rises to roughly one-third in California. With that density of immigrant entrepreneurs comes a corresponding density of mission-driven lenders, Community Development Financial Institutions (CDFIs), and credit unions that have built underwriting systems around ITIN documentation rather than SSNs.

More than half of all firm founders in Silicon Valley are immigrants, a fact that pushed Bay Area nonprofit lenders to build ITIN-capable programs years before the rest of the country caught up. Working Solutions CDFI, for instance, grew out of San Francisco specifically to serve small business owners who could not get traditional bank financing.

ITIN holders can legally form LLCs, obtain EINs, and operate businesses. The primary barriers are banking access and credit history, because most banks require SSNs and credit bureaus index by SSN. California’s CDFI ecosystem exists partly to fix that structural gap. The result is that an ITIN holder in Los Angeles, San Jose, Fresno, or San Diego has more real lender options than in almost any other state.

What happened to SBA loans for ITIN holders in 2026?

This one comes up a lot:

The short answer: SBA-guaranteed loans are gone for ITIN holders. Under SBA Policy Notice 5000-876441, effective March 1, 2026, SBA rules require that 100% of all direct and indirect owners of a small business applicant be U.S. citizens or U.S. nationals maintaining their principal residence in the United States. ITIN holders and green card holders are both excluded.

This is a sharp change from the prior landscape. Previously, SBA microloan intermediaries (many of them CDFIs) could set their own eligibility rules and often accepted ITIN applicants. Now, even those intermediaries cannot deploy SBA funds to ITIN-only borrowers. ITIN holders were never the primary SBA audience, but the complete closure of the program to any non-citizen owner eliminates even workaround structures.

The practical impact in California: the SBA Microloan Program, which historically channeled funds through CDFI intermediaries at rates of 8% to 13%, is no longer accessible. Many of those same CDFI intermediaries still lend their own capital and impact investment funds to ITIN holders, though. The loan comes from the CDFI’s own balance sheet rather than the SBA guarantee facility, so rates and terms may differ slightly, but the door is still open.

Our editorial stance: Do not waste time applying to SBA programs. Focus your energy on the private ITIN-friendly market documented in this guide.

Tell me about Kiva: the 0% option for California entrepreneurs

Kiva is a genuinely free microloan up to $15,000, but you will wait up to 60 days and need your personal network to help fund it. Loan amounts run from $1,000 to $15,000 with a 0% APR and no minimum credit score or time in business required.

You apply online, get approved based on character-based social underwriting rather than credit score, then enter a 15-day private funding period where you must recruit a minimum number of people from your own network to contribute at least $25 each. After clearing this phase, your loan goes public on Kiva’s platform, where the organization’s network of over 2 million lenders can fund the remainder.

For ITIN holders in California, Kiva has a meaningful advantage beyond the 0% rate: optional business credit reporting to Experian Business and Dun & Bradstreet through the Credit Builders Alliance, which can help you qualify for larger loans later. If your business has no U.S. credit history yet, this is a genuine credit-building tool packaged as a loan.

The catch: the mandatory private funding period forces you to recruit friends, family, or contacts to contribute at least $25 each before your loan goes public. If you cannot mobilize your network, the loan will not fund. End-to-end funding takes 30 to 60 days, which rules out Kiva for any urgent capital need.

Tip for California applicants: Kiva works with a network of field partners and trustees. If you are connected to a CDFI, a small business development center, or a microenterprise organization in your area, ask whether they are a Kiva trustee. A trustee endorsement can meaningfully strengthen your application and in some cases accelerates the approval timeline. California has a large number of Kiva trustees concentrated in Los Angeles, San Francisco, and the Central Valley.

How does Accion Opportunity Fund work for California ITIN holders?

Readers frequently ask:

Accion Opportunity Fund (AOF) is a nonprofit Community Development Financial Institution headquartered in San Jose, California, formed in 2020 when Accion U.S. Network and Opportunity Fund merged. This matters for California applicants because AOF originated here, which means the organization’s staff, field officers, and California-specific knowledge run deep.

Loans carry interest rates between 9.99% and 28.99%, which may include processing or closing costs. Borrowing amounts run from $5,000 to $250,000, with terms of 12 to 36 months and an origination fee of 3% to 5%.

The minimum credit score is 600, but Accion’s underwriting is notably holistic. AOF accepts credit scores as low as 600 and weighs cash flow over credit history alone. It also offers free one-on-one business coaching, mentorship, and educational resources in English and Spanish included with every loan. For California’s large Spanish-speaking immigrant entrepreneur community, the bilingual support is a real differentiator.

Standard eligibility for Accion in California:

  • At least 12 months in business (some programs require 24 months and $300,000 in annual revenue for working capital loans)
  • ITIN accepted in place of SSN
  • Government-issued ID (passport, consular ID, or driver’s license)
  • 3 months of business bank statements
  • 20% or more ownership stake in the business
  • Accion Opportunity Fund Community Development loans are available in 45 states, which includes California

Average funding speed is about six days once the application is complete, which makes Accion one of the faster CDFI options in the market.

What is DreamSpring, and can it help me in California?

DreamSpring is a nationally recognized nonprofit CDFI serving entrepreneurs across 27 U.S. states, with a focus on underserved communities including women, immigrants, people of color, and rural founders. California is within its service territory, alongside Texas, Florida, New York, and 23 other states.

You must have a Social Security Number (SSN) or Individual Tax Identification Number (ITIN) to qualify, and ITIN is explicitly accepted across all of DreamSpring’s products. This is a verified policy, not a case-by-case decision.

DreamSpring’s product menu for California ITIN holders:

  • Small Business Term Loan: $1,000 to $250,000+, terms of 24 to 72 months, credit score 600+, interest rate 9.99% to 18.99%, community benefit fee 2.5% to 10%

  • Power Line of Credit: $1,000 to $100,000, 9.99% to 18.74% APR, 24-month revolving term, 600+ credit score

A key feature for newer businesses: DreamSpring’s flagship loan requires no collateral under $20,000 for borrowers with credit scores above 650, and accepts ITIN in lieu of SSN. No minimum time in business is specified for the SBA Microloan product, meaning startups can apply.

DreamSpring’s required documents for California applicants include: 3 to 6 months of business bank statements, most recent year’s business federal tax return (or year-to-date P&L for newer businesses), government-issued ID (ITIN accepted), and a brief description of how the loan will be used.

Who is Working Solutions CDFI, and why do Bay Area entrepreneurs use them?

This one comes up a lot:

Working Solutions is a San Francisco-based CDFI that has served California small business owners since 2005. Unlike national CDFIs that added California coverage as a geographic expansion, Working Solutions was built for the Bay Area immigrant entrepreneur market from the ground up. The organization accepts ITIN holders and focuses specifically on businesses that cannot get conventional bank financing.

Their loan range typically runs from $5,000 to $100,000, and the underwriting is explicitly holistic, meaning they look at your business plan, your community ties, and your cash flow, not just your FICO score. They offer bilingual staff, in-person support in San Francisco and Oakland, and connections to the broader Bay Area small business support ecosystem.

For ITIN holders in Los Angeles or Southern California, Accion Opportunity Fund is generally the stronger option given AOF’s statewide reach and faster funding timelines. For Bay Area founders, Working Solutions deserves a first call.

What if I have bad credit or no U.S. credit history?

The obstacle for many ITIN holders is usually procedural rather than a policy against ITIN filers: most lenders’ automated underwriting pulls a consumer credit file keyed to an SSN, and without one the application fails a technical check rather than an actual credit decision.

CDFIs in California solve this in several ways:

No credit minimum (Kiva): Kiva charges a genuinely 0% APR with no origination, maintenance, draw, wire, or prepayment fees. There is no minimum credit score, no revenue requirement, no time-in-business threshold, and no collateral needed, making it accessible to brand-new businesses.

Alternative credit review (Accion, DreamSpring): Both lenders can consider rent payment history, utility bills, and other non-traditional credit signals if you lack a FICO score. Bring 12 months of documentation showing consistent payments.

Cash-flow focus: Asset-based and revenue-based products bypass the credit-score barrier entirely. If your business generates consistent revenue, CDFIs will often lend based on that cash flow track record, even without a strong credit profile.

For more strategies on building a credit file that ITIN-friendly lenders can use, see our guide on building credit with an ITIN. Even a credit builder loan or secured card started today can meaningfully improve your CDFI approval odds within 12 months.

What other funding options exist in California beyond CDFIs?

Readers frequently ask:

CDFIs are the right first move for most ITIN holders, but they are not the only move. Two additional products can work well for ITIN holders in California who need faster capital or who have B2B invoices:

Invoice factoring: Invoice factoring works like this: you deliver goods or services to a customer and issue an invoice. Instead of waiting 30 to 90 days for the customer to pay, you sell that invoice to a factoring company. They advance you approximately 70% to 90% of the invoice value immediately, typically within 24 hours. When your customer pays the invoice, the factor retains a fee (typically 1.5% to 5% of the invoice amount) and sends you the remaining balance. Qualification depends on your customer’s creditworthiness, not yours, which makes it one of the most accessible products for ITIN holders with limited U.S. credit history. Specialty lenders including Conserv Capital and Cultiva Financial accept ITIN for equipment financing and invoice factoring products.

Merchant cash advances (MCAs): MCAs provide a lump-sum advance repaid through a percentage of daily sales. They accept ITIN holders and have very low credit requirements, but the cost is steep. A merchant cash advance is the most expensive form of business financing widely available, with effective APRs of 80% to 350% being common. Most MCAs for businesses with poor credit come with factor rates of 1.30 to 1.50 and repayment in 6 to 12 months, putting the effective APR at 70% to 150%+. Use an MCA only as a last resort, and only if the profit generated from that capital will clearly exceed the cost.

Our editorial stance: If you have B2B invoices, explore factoring before considering an MCA. If you have neither and need under $15,000, Kiva at 0% beats an MCA at 100%+ APR every time.

What is the step-by-step process to apply for an ITIN business loan in California?

Here is a practical sequence that minimizes wasted time:

Step 1: Get your ITIN and EIN in order. Your ITIN must be current. The IRS began expiring unused ITINs in 2017, so borrowers who have not filed taxes recently may need to renew them. Apply for an EIN from the IRS (free at IRS.gov) if you do not already have one. Most CDFIs will want the EIN on your application even if the loan is in your personal name.

Step 2: Open a dedicated business bank account. This is not just paperwork preparation. Having a separate business account for 3 to 6 months demonstrates that your business operates as a real entity, not just a personal side income. Many California credit unions, including those covered in our ITIN bank account guide, accept ITIN to open business checking accounts.

Step 3: Gather your documents. Work through the document table in the section above. Pull 6 months of bank statements, prepare a simple P&L, and scan your ITIN letter and government ID.

Step 4: Pre-qualify without a hard pull. Both Accion and DreamSpring allow prequalification without a hard credit inquiry. AOF offers customizable financing: see if you prequalify and get three different term options in just 15 minutes, without impacting your credit score.

Step 5: Apply to your best-fit lender. Use the table at the top of this article to match your situation. If you are pre-revenue, start with Kiva. If you need $5,000 to $250,000 and have 12+ months of history, start with Accion. If you are in the Bay Area, contact Working Solutions directly.

Step 6: Be transparent in your application. CDFIs use “character-based” underwriting. A clear explanation of your business, how you will use the funds, and your plan to repay carries real weight. Thin or incomplete applications get slower decisions.

Are there ITIN business loan options specific to Los Angeles, San Francisco, or other California metros?

This one comes up a lot:

California’s CDFI landscape is genuinely statewide, but concentration varies:

Los Angeles: Accion Opportunity Fund is active throughout the Los Angeles metro and has historically focused resources on the area’s large immigrant business community. Local CDFIs including Inclusive Action for the City specifically target South LA and East LA microbusinesses and are worth exploring directly.

San Francisco and Bay Area: Working Solutions CDFI is headquartered in San Francisco and maintains strong relationships with ITIN-holder entrepreneurs specifically. Accion also operates in the Bay Area. The Bay Area SBDC network (Small Business Development Center) can connect you with ITIN-accepting lenders as a free service.

Central Valley (Fresno, Bakersfield, Stockton): The agricultural economy of the Central Valley means many ITIN business owners are in food production, transportation, or equipment-dependent industries. LiftFund and Accion both serve this region. Equipment financing from ITIN-accepting specialty lenders may be especially relevant here.

San Diego: Accion’s Southern California reach extends to San Diego. The Jacobs Center for Neighborhood Innovation and the City of San Diego’s CDFI partners also serve ITIN-holder entrepreneurs in the South Bay and Logan Heights neighborhoods.

For any of these regions, the California Governor’s Office of Business and Economic Development (GO-Biz) maintains a free directory of CDFIs, small business lenders, and technical assistance providers. Filtering by your county and loan size is a practical starting point alongside the lenders named in this guide.
