## Who actually qualifies for an ITIN personal loan, and what do lenders look at?

Approval for ITIN borrowers is usually based on income stability and bank account history, not U.S. credit score, which most ITIN holders do not have yet. APRs are often higher than for SSN holders because the lender is taking on more perceived risk with thinner credit data.

The profile that gets approved most often: a borrower with a valid, unexpired ITIN, 12-plus months of stable income shown through pay stubs or bank deposits, an active U.S. checking account with consistent transaction history, and at least one filed federal tax return. In practical terms, you typically need a valid ITIN, 1-2 years of filed tax returns, income verification through pay stubs or 3-6 months of bank statements, an active U.S. bank account, photo ID, and either a credit score around 580+ or 12 months of alternative credit history, with debt-to-income generally under 45%.

Research has shown that ITIN holders are less likely to default than consumers who use Social Security numbers, which is one reason community lenders and CDFIs have kept ITIN programs running even amid regulatory debate in 2026.

## Which lender is the best starting point if I have zero U.S. credit?

*This one comes up a lot,* especially from recent arrivals who have been in the U.S. for less than a year.

Mission Asset Fund (MAF) Lending Circles are the clearest answer. Lending Circles are formed between 6-12 people, and the loan amount ranges from $300 to $2,400. Each month, a new member receives the loan until everyone in the group gets their turn. The loans carry 0% interest, and documentation needed to enroll includes proof of checking account ownership, an SSN or ITIN, a valid government-issued ID, and proof of income. No citizenship requirements apply.

The MAF model is based on the traditional Latin American "tanda" savings circle concept, formalized into a credit-building structure. MAF is a San Francisco-based nonprofit that facilitates zero-interest lending and simultaneous credit building. Their model links community members into rotating savings and credit associations, then reports participation to credit bureaus to help members establish or improve a credit score.

The limitation: MAF loans top out at $2,400 and require patience. If you need more than that today, Oportun is the next most accessible commercial option.

Oportun has no minimum credit score and no credit history requirement. Loans run $300 to $10,000 with APRs up to 35.99%. Oportun accepts either a Social Security number or an ITIN, along with a government ID and proof of income and residence.

Oportun personal loans typically start at $300 and go up to $6,000 for new customers, $7,500 for returning customers, and $10,000 for secured loans. That tiered structure rewards repeat borrowers, which mirrors the credit-building logic of MAF Lending Circles.

The cost to weigh: Oportun charges an origination fee of up to 10% of the loan amount. On a $5,000 loan, that is up to $500 taken off the top before you receive funds. Factor the origination fee into your true cost of borrowing, not just the stated APR.

## What are Juntos Avanzamos credit unions, and how do I find one near me?

Juntos Avanzamos ("Together We Advance") is a designation for credit unions committed to serving Hispanic consumers. It was developed by the Cornerstone Credit Union League as an immigrant outreach program in Texas.

The Juntos Avanzamos network includes 145 credit unions in 31 states, Puerto Rico, and the District of Columbia. Collectively, these credit unions serve 13+ million consumers through 1,250+ branches, holding $195+ million in assets.

To earn the designation, credit unions must meet specific standards: offering deposit and loan products for members with ITINs, providing bilingual services, employing culturally competent staff, and accepting alternative forms of identification. That means a Juntos Avanzamos credit union is, by definition, confirmed to accept ITINs for loan applications, not just accounts.

Credit unions with a Juntos Avanzamos designation often offer the best rates for ITIN borrowers, around 8%-18% APR. To find one near you, search the directory at juntosmovimiento.org or ask your local credit union directly whether they hold the designation.

## Step-by-step: how to apply for an ITIN personal loan

*This one comes up a lot,* and the sequence matters because a hard credit pull at the wrong lender can hurt the thin file you are trying to build.

**Step 1: Verify your ITIN is active.** If your ITIN has not been used in federal declarations for several years, it may be expired, and that can complicate financial or tax matters. Renew through the IRS before applying anywhere. Check our guide on [how to renew your ITIN](/how-to-get-an-itin) for step-by-step instructions.

**Step 2: Gather your documents.** Collect your ITIN letter or card, your two most recent federal tax returns (filed with your ITIN), three to six months of bank statements showing regular deposits, a valid government-issued photo ID, and a utility bill or lease showing your U.S. address.

**Step 3: Prequalify with a soft pull first.** Stilt and Oportun both offer soft-pull prequalification. This lets you see rate estimates without touching your credit file. Check two or three lenders before committing to a hard pull.

**Step 4: Apply at a credit union or CDFI first.** Local credit unions and CDFIs tend to have the most flexible ITIN policies and the lowest rates. If you cannot qualify locally, fintech lenders like Stilt focus specifically on ITIN and immigrant borrowers.

**Step 5: Compare total cost, not just APR.** Two loans at the same rate can cost very different amounts once fees are counted. Factor in origination fees (Oportun's 10% versus Stilt's 0% versus a credit union's 0%-2%) when calculating which offer is actually cheaper.

**Step 6: Confirm bureau reporting before signing.** Ask the lender directly: "Do you report to Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion?" Only a reported loan builds your U.S. credit score. MAF, Stilt, Oportun, and most credit unions all report to all three bureaus.

## Does the 2026 CFPB guidance change anything for ITIN personal loan borrowers?

This question matters because of regulatory movement in mid-2026, and we want to give you a straight answer.

President Trump signed Executive Order 14406 on May 19, 2026. Rather than immediately creating a new nationwide banking rule, the Order directed the Treasury Department, CFPB, and federal banking regulators to take a series of actions over the following months. On June 8, 2026, the CFPB issued a Statement on Ability to Repay and Immigration Status. The CFPB explained that creditors may consider a borrower's immigration status when evaluating whether the borrower will continue to have sufficient income to repay certain loans. Where information available to a creditor indicates that a person's immigration circumstances could reasonably cause a future loss of U.S.-based employment income, the creditor may, in some situations, have to take that information into consideration when evaluating ability to repay.

Here is what that means in practice: the policy statement does not have the force of law. No major lender has publicly announced changes to its ITIN lending programs since the advisories were issued. Industry participants note that the guidance could raise questions about how lenders should weigh immigration-related risks while complying with fair lending laws, but so far the programs are running normally.

For borrowers, the most protective steps you can take right now are: keep your ITIN current, file your tax returns every year, maintain stable documented income, and keep bank account balances steady. These are exactly the factors lenders use to approve loans, and they also directly address any ability-to-repay question a lender might raise.

## What are the biggest red flags to avoid when shopping for an ITIN personal loan?

You can check lender complaints and verify licenses for free through the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau at consumerfinance.gov. According to the CFPB, immigrant and limited-English borrowers are disproportionately targeted by predatory loan products, so extra scrutiny is warranted.

Specific red flags to watch for:

**APR above 36%.** This is the clearest signal of a predatory product. Walk away.

**Upfront fees before loan disbursement.** Legitimate lenders deduct origination fees from loan proceeds. They never charge fees before you receive money.

**No written loan agreement.** Any offer communicated only by phone, text, or informal message is not a legitimate loan.

**No state license.** Every state requires consumer lenders to hold a license. Ask for the lender's license number and verify it with your state's banking regulator or at the NMLS Consumer Access database.

**Pressure to use a prepaid debit card for repayment.** Legitimate lenders accept ACH bank transfers or checks. Prepaid card demands are a common fraud signal.

## 8 questions ITIN borrowers ask most often

**Can I get a personal loan with only an ITIN and no Social Security number?**
Yes. ITIN holders can get personal loans through credit unions, Community Development Financial Institutions (CDFIs), and some community banks. No federal law requires an SSN for a personal loan. Credit unions typically offer the lowest rates; CDFIs specialize in serving underbanked communities including ITIN holders.

**What is the minimum income needed to qualify?**
Lenders do not publish a universal income floor, but most community lenders and CDFIs look for monthly income of at least $850-$1,200 to confirm you can make payments. Oportun does not disclose a minimum income figure. Stilt looks at the ratio of income to requested loan amount rather than a hard floor.

**Can I apply online, or do I need to visit a branch?**
Stilt, Oportun, and Camino Financial are fully online. MAF Lending Circles require enrolling through a local partner organization, often in person. Credit unions vary: some accept online membership applications, others require an in-person visit to open an account and apply for a loan.

**How long does it take to get funded?**
Oportun funds in 1-4 days post-approval. Camino Financial funds in 1-4 business days. Credit unions typically take 2-5 business days from application to deposit. MAF Lending Circles take several weeks because they require assembling a group of participants.

**Will applying hurt my credit score?**
A prequalification (soft pull) at Stilt or Oportun does not affect your credit. A formal application triggers a hard pull, which typically drops a credit score by 2-10 points temporarily. If you have no U.S. credit file yet, there is nothing to pull, and the inquiry simply starts your credit record.

**Can a co-signer improve my approval odds?**
Yes, at most lenders. A co-signer with a stronger credit profile can lower your rate and increase your approved amount. Credit unions are most likely to accept co-signers. Oportun does not offer a co-sign option. Check the specific lender's policy before asking someone to co-sign.

**What happens if my ITIN is expired?**
Most lenders will not approve the application until you renew your ITIN. Renewal takes 7-11 weeks through the IRS. Apply for renewal before you need the loan, not after you are declined.

**Do ITIN personal loans work the same in Texas, California, and Florida?**
The federal framework is the same across all states, but state-level rate caps and lender availability differ. Texas has no personal loan rate cap, so Oportun's full APR applies. California caps consumer loan rates at 36% for loans under $2,500, which aligns with Oportun's cap. Florida also has strong consumer protection for smaller loans. All three states have dense Juntos Avanzamos credit union networks and are within Stilt's and Oportun's service areas. For state-specific detail, see our guides to [ITIN personal loans in Texas](/articles/itin-personal-loan-texas), [California](/articles/itin-personal-loan-california), and [Florida](/articles/itin-personal-loan-florida).
