New York is home to one of the largest immigrant populations in the country, and ITIN holders here have more personal-loan options than most people realize. No federal law prohibits lenders from issuing personal loans to borrowers without Social Security numbers. The catch is finding the right lenders, because availability, rates, and requirements differ sharply by institution, and some nationally known options don't even operate in New York.

## What lenders actually offer ITIN personal loans in New York?

Credit unions, CDFIs, and some community banks offer personal loans to ITIN holders using your ITIN, tax returns, and bank statements as verification. In New York specifically, a few names stand out.

TD Bank (TD Fit Loan) is the single strongest bank option. TD Bank explicitly lists ITIN as an acceptable form of identification on its application, alongside the usual personal info, employment history, income, and expenses. The fixed APR runs 7.99%-23.99% (as of June 2026) with no origination, application, or prepayment fees. Loan amounts go from $2,000 to $50,000 with repayment terms of 36-60 months. That's a competitive package for a bank-level lender.

One important New York gap to know: Oportun does not offer personal loans to New York residents, even though it accepts ITINs and lends in 41 states. If you've seen Oportun recommended broadly for ITIN borrowers, it's not an option here.

Credit unions and CDFIs fill the gap at the lower end of the rate spectrum. You can apply at credit unions with a Juntos Avanzamos designation, CDFIs making small loans of $500-$10,000, community and regional banks in immigrant neighborhoods, and a handful of online lenders that use alternative data. Capital Good Fund, a CDFI licensed in New York, and Mission Asset Fund's Lending Circles (available in most states via nonprofit partners) are solid choices if you have no credit history. CDFIs are nonprofit lenders certified by the U.S. Treasury that focus on underbanked communities; they typically accept ITINs and carry lower rates than online lenders.

## How do lenders decide whether to approve me?

*A question we hear often:* what exactly does a lender look at if I don't have a U.S. credit score?

Loan approval is usually based on income stability and bank account history, not a U.S. credit score, which most ITIN holders don't have yet. Lenders want evidence that you earn enough to repay and that you manage your finances responsibly. Filed tax returns are useful proof of both: they confirm your income and show that you participate in the U.S. tax system.

A history of filed tax returns and at least one active bank account dramatically improves your chances. Lenders use these to verify income and financial stability in place of a credit bureau pull. Beyond that, lenders look at income stability, credit history where it exists, and repayment capacity rather than visa category. Your immigration status is not what's being evaluated.

For borrowers who do have some U.S. credit history, the picture improves further. According to the CFPB's research on credit access for immigrant communities, even a thin credit file of 6-12 months can move a borrower from the highest-rate tier to a mid-range one.

## What documents do I need to apply?

*This one comes up a lot:* preparing your paperwork before you apply is the single fastest way to cut your timeline from weeks to days.

| Document | What lenders typically accept |
|---|---|
| Tax ID | Valid ITIN (IRS-issued, not expired) |
| Photo ID | Passport, matrícula consular, or state-issued ID |
| Proof of address | Utility bill, lease, or bank statement dated within 60 days |
| Income verification | Pay stubs, 1099s, employer letter, or 3-6 months of bank statements |
| Tax returns | 1-2 years of federal returns filed with your ITIN |
| Bank account | Active U.S. checking or savings account |

To qualify for an ITIN personal loan you typically need a valid ITIN, 1-2 years of filed tax returns, income verification through pay stubs or 3-6 months of bank statements, an active U.S. bank account, photo ID, and either a credit score around 580 or 12 months of alternative credit, with a debt-to-income ratio generally under 45%.

If you're self-employed or paid in cash, lenders will lean heavily on bank statements. Acceptable documentation includes 3-6 months of U.S. bank statements showing regular deposits, 1099 contractor payment logs, cash-deposit affidavits, or your last two years of federal tax returns filed with your ITIN.

## What interest rate should I realistically expect?

*Readers frequently ask:* how much more will I pay compared to someone with an SSN and good credit?

APRs are often higher than for SSN holders because the lender is working with thinner credit data. That said, the range is wide and depends mostly on which type of lender you choose.

| Lender type | Typical APR range | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| CDFI / nonprofit (MAF Lending Circles) | 0% | First-time borrowers, credit building |
| Credit union (ITIN-friendly) | 8%-18% | Members with steady income |
| TD Bank (TD Fit Loan) | 7.99%-23.99% | ITIN holders with established banking |
| ITIN fintech (Stilt, Camino Financial) | 15%-36% | No membership needed |
| Storefront / payday alternatives | Up to 36% | Last resort only |

Avoid payday and title loans, which often charge 300%-400% APR. Those products target ITIN holders specifically and cost far more than any of the lenders listed above.

## What if I have bad credit or no U.S. credit history at all?

New York ITIN borrowers with no U.S. credit history still have real options. Mission Asset Fund's Lending Circles are a zero-interest loan program that reports to all three credit bureaus: you borrow, repay on a schedule, and exit with a credit file. For a long-term financial plan, that credit file is worth more than the loan itself.

Instead of a credit score, these lenders review income, employment stability, and bank account activity. Rates typically run 15%-30% APR without a credit file, but drop significantly once you build even a thin credit history. Pairing one of these programs with an [ITIN credit builder loan](/itin-personal-loans) or a [secured credit card](/itin-credit-cards) speeds that process up considerably.

If you have some credit but it's below 620, community credit unions with a Juntos Avanzamos designation are your best first call. They're structured specifically to serve immigrant borrowers and evaluate applications with more flexibility than automated bank systems.

## How do I actually apply, step by step?

*Readers frequently ask:* what is the actual sequence, start to finish?

1. Gather documents first. Use the table above. Having everything ready before you open an application prevents delays.
2. Check your ITIN is current. ITINs not used on a federal tax return for three consecutive years expire. Confirm yours is active before applying. You can [renew your ITIN](/how-to-get-an-itin) through the IRS if needed.
3. Prequalify with TD Bank or a CDFI. Prequalification typically uses a soft credit pull that doesn't affect your score.
4. Compare at least two offers. Even a 2%-3% difference in APR on a $10,000 loan over 48 months means roughly $500-$800 in extra interest paid.
5. Submit your full application and respond quickly to document requests. Most lenders fund within 1-2 business days once all documents are verified.
6. Make on-time payments every month. Lenders that report to all three credit bureaus help you build U.S. credit, which opens lower-rate options within 12-24 months.

Once you have an established credit file, revisit your [options for personal loans as an ITIN holder](/itin-personal-loans) to refinance into a lower rate. If your goal also includes building business credit, a [business loan with your ITIN](/itin-business-loans) may run parallel to your personal borrowing plan.

## Frequently asked questions

Can I get a personal loan in New York with only an ITIN, no Social Security number?
Yes. No federal law requires an SSN for a personal loan. In New York, TD Bank's TD Fit Loan explicitly accepts ITINs, as do several credit unions and CDFIs. Lenders verify identity using your ITIN plus a government-issued photo ID such as a passport or consular card.

Which lender is the best option for an ITIN personal loan in New York?
TD Bank (TD Fit Loan) is the strongest single-lender option in New York because it accepts ITINs, charges 7.99%-23.99% APR with no origination or prepayment fees, and lends up to $50,000. CDFIs like Capital Good Fund and Mission Asset Fund are better for borrowers with no credit history.

Does Oportun offer personal loans in New York?
No. Oportun does not currently lend to New York residents. New York ITIN borrowers who want a no-minimum-credit-score lender should look at Capital Good Fund or a local Juntos Avanzamos credit union instead.

What documents do I need for an ITIN personal loan in New York?
Most lenders require a valid ITIN, a government-issued photo ID (passport, matrícula consular, or state ID), proof of a New York address (utility bill or lease), proof of income (pay stubs, 1099s, or 3-6 months of bank statements), and an active U.S. bank account. One to two years of filed tax returns strengthen your file.

What credit score do I need for an ITIN personal loan in New York?
It depends on the lender. TD Bank typically favors scores of 660 or above. Credit unions and CDFIs may accept scores as low as 580 or use alternative credit such as rent and utility payment history. Mission Asset Fund's Lending Circles require no credit score at all.

How fast can I get funded after approval?
TD Bank and most online CDFI lenders fund within 1-2 business days of approval. Credit unions tend to take 2-5 business days because of manual underwriting. Having all documents ready before you apply cuts the timeline significantly.
