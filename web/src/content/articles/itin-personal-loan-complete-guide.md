## Is it actually legal to borrow money with just an ITIN?

*A question we hear often:*

Completely legal. No federal law prohibits personal loans to borrowers without an SSN. An ITIN satisfies lenders' identity verification and tax-reporting requirements.

The Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA) prohibits lenders from discriminating against applicants based on national origin or race. Lenders can verify residency and legal capacity to sign contracts, but they are not legally required to demand an SSN.

Whether to accept an ITIN is a business decision each lender makes internally, not a legal barrier. The reason most mainstream banks require an SSN has nothing to do with the law: most underwriting technology stacks pull credit reports keyed to an SSN and have not yet integrated ITIN flows, and some lenders worry about FinCEN and KYC rules for non-SSN borrowers even though ITIN is explicitly accepted by federal regulators.

That is changing. The lenders in the table above have built real ITIN approval flows, and the market continues to expand.

## Which lenders actually confirm they accept ITINs, and what are the real requirements?

The list below goes deeper than the overview table, covering specific underwriting details that determine whether you will actually be approved.

### Mission Asset Fund (MAF) Lending Circles

Lending Circles are formed between 6, 12 people, with loan amounts ranging from $300 to $2,400. Each month, one member receives the loan until everyone in the group gets their turn.

When the circle begins, each participant makes the same monthly payment ($50 to $200), which MAF reports to all three credit bureaus. The APR is 0% and there are no credit score requirements. Documentation needed to enroll: proof of checking account ownership, SSN or ITIN, a valid government-issued ID, and proof of income.

This is the strongest starting option for someone with zero U.S. credit history. The drawback is the amount cap and the group-based structure, which means you may need to wait several months before it is your turn to receive the funds.

### Self-Help Federal Credit Union

Self-Help FCU is a community development credit union that accepts ITIN for membership and lending, with personal loans of $500 to $25,000 and APRs often in the 10%, 18% range depending on credit profile. Self-Help Credit Union and Self-Help FCU are both certified CDFIs. The credit union operates branches in North Carolina, South Carolina, Florida, Georgia, and Virginia, and also accepts members from elsewhere in the U.S. through its online platform. Manual underwriting means thin-credit applicants get a real review rather than an automated denial.

### Latino Community Credit Union (LCCU)

Latino Community Credit Union accepts ITIN for checking, savings, auto loans, personal loans, mortgages, and credit cards. It is also an IRS-authorized ITIN Certified Acceptance Agent, meaning it can help you apply or renew your ITIN.

Based in North Carolina with 15 branches statewide and online services, LCCU offers loan amounts from $500 to $15,000 with APRs typically in the 10%, 18% range depending on credit profile. It is designed specifically for Spanish speakers and Latino immigrant communities. Membership requires joining the Latino Community Development Center for a one-time $8 fee. At least one year of credit history is required for loans over $500.

### Stilt

Stilt provides loans typically ranging from $1,000 to $35,000, with fixed interest rates generally between 8% and 25% APR. It does not require a Social Security number or a traditional credit score, which removes a major barrier for its target market.

Instead of a FICO score, Stilt looks at education, employment, and visa status. It uses a soft pull only at prequalification. Loans typically fund within 2-4 business days of approval and document verification.

### Oportun

Oportun requires a valid photo ID and a Social Security number or ITIN. There is no minimum credit score requirement. The application takes minutes, and funding can arrive as fast as the next business day.

APRs start at 20.05% and loan amounts run from $300 to $10,000. In California, Florida, and Texas, Oportun offers a secured loan option where you can put up your car as collateral for a larger amount and a lower rate. Oportun is the most accessible single lender for a borrower with no U.S. credit history who needs funds quickly, but its rates are high. Read the origination fee disclosure for your state before signing.

### TD Bank (TD Fit Loan)

TD Bank explicitly lists ITIN as an acceptable form of identification on its application. The fixed APR runs 7.99%, 23.99% (as of June 2026) with no origination, application, or prepayment fees. TD Bank is primarily an East Coast institution with branches concentrated from Maine to Florida. It is a good option for established borrowers who can demonstrate a solid credit profile.

### Capital Good Fund

Capital Good Fund focuses on people with limited access to traditional financing, accepts ITINs and other documentation in lieu of an SSN, and offers loans for home energy efficiency, emergency expenses, and immigration costs, with rates accessible even without a strong credit score. Every Capital Good Fund loan product reports on-time payments to all three major credit bureaus. It is one of the few CDFIs nationally that specializes in small-dollar personal loans.

## What documents do I need, and how do I prepare a strong application?

*Readers frequently ask:*

Gather these before you apply anywhere. Missing documents are the most common reason applications stall or get rejected.

**Core documents (required by almost every ITIN lender):**

1. **ITIN letter:** Your IRS-issued ITIN assignment letter (Form CP565) or W-7 approval notice. The IRS began expiring ITINs in 2017 for numbers not used on a tax return for three consecutive years. If your ITIN has expired, you will generally need to renew it before a lender can move forward. See our [ITIN renewal guide](/how-to-get-an-itin) if yours has lapsed.

2. **Government-issued photo ID:** An unexpired foreign passport, consular identification card (such as a Mexican Matricula Consular or Guatemalan Consular ID), or a state-issued driver's license.

3. **Proof of income:** Recent pay stubs covering the last 30, 60 days; the previous two years of federal tax returns (Form 1040) filed with your ITIN; and bank statements from the last 3-6 months showing consistent direct deposits.

4. **Active U.S. bank account:** Required by virtually every ITIN lender to receive funds and set up repayment.

5. **Proof of address:** Utility bills showing your name and current address, or a signed residential lease agreement.

**For self-employed borrowers:** Two full years of Schedule C tax forms filed under your ITIN can verify consistent revenue. Some lenders also accept profit-and-loss statements prepared by a CPA.

**Key underwriting thresholds to know:** Most lenders prefer a DTI ratio below 36%, 40%. A DTI above 50% is a hard rejection trigger at most traditional lenders. Calculate yours before applying: add up all monthly debt payments and divide by gross monthly income.

## How do I actually apply, step by step?

*A question we hear often:*

**Step 1: Confirm your ITIN is active.** Check your IRS letter. If your ITIN has not been used on a tax return in three consecutive years, it may be expired. Renew it before applying. A lender cannot process an application against an expired ITIN.

**Step 2: Pull your credit report.** Go to AnnualCreditReport.com and request reports from all three bureaus using your ITIN. Even a thin file tells you what a lender will see. If you have no file at all, build one first through a credit-builder loan or secured card before applying for an unsecured personal loan. Our guide on [building credit with an ITIN](/articles/how-to-build-credit-with-itin) walks through the fastest path.

**Step 3: Calculate your DTI.** Total all monthly debt obligations (credit cards, car payments, rent if counted, existing loans) and divide by gross monthly income. If DTI exceeds 45%, pay down a revolving balance or wait for a salary increase before applying.

**Step 4: Gather all five documents listed in the previous section.** Incomplete applications are the number-one cause of delays.

**Step 5: Apply at a credit union or CDFI first.** Local credit unions and CDFIs tend to have the most flexible ITIN policies and the lowest rates. If you need to build membership at a credit union first, start that process now, as some require 30, 90 days of active membership before you can borrow.

**Step 6: Use prequalification before formal applications.** Stilt and Oportun both offer a soft credit pull at the prequalification stage. Use this to compare likely rate offers without affecting your credit score. Only submit a formal application where you have a realistic chance of approval.

**Step 7: If a credit union declines, go to Stilt or Oportun.** If you cannot qualify locally, fintech lenders like Stilt and EzLoan focus specifically on ITIN and immigrant borrowers. Oportun is the best fallback for borrowers with no score. Stilt is better for borrowers with some U.S. banking history and visa documentation.

## How do ITIN personal loan rates compare to SSN-based loans, and why?

The rate premium for ITIN borrowers is real but not permanent. It exists primarily because of data, not status.

Interest rates on ITIN personal loans run higher than comparable SSN-based loans for two structural reasons. First, most ITIN borrowers arrive with thin or no U.S. credit file, which means lenders have less repayment history to price the risk accurately. Second, ITIN-accepting lenders are a smaller pool, so there is less competitive pressure on rates.

The premium shrinks as you build history. Credit unions with a Juntos Avanzamos designation often offer the best rates, around 8%, 18% APR, and those rates improve with each loan as you build a credit history with the same lender.

Here is a direct comparison of the ITIN rate landscape against SSN borrower benchmarks:

| Borrower Type | Typical APR Range | Source |
|---------------|------------------|--------|
| SSN borrower, 740+ FICO (benchmark) | 9.6%, 12.4% | Credible, Nov 2026 |
| ITIN borrower, 660+ score, credit union | 10%, 18% | Self-Help FCU, LCCU data |
| ITIN borrower, 580, 659, fintech | 15%, 25% | Stilt verified |
| ITIN borrower, no score, Oportun | 20.05%, 35.99% | Oportun disclosures |
| Payday / title loan (avoid) | 300%, 700%+ | CFPB data |

Stilt and Camino Financial both allow debt consolidation as a loan purpose. An ITIN personal loan at 18%, 30% APR is dramatically cheaper than a payday loan at 300%, 700% APR, so refinancing usually saves significant money.

Yes, but only if your lender reports to the credit bureaus. This is a critical question to ask before signing.

Every Capital Good Fund loan product reports on-time payments to all three major credit bureaus.

## What red flags should I watch for in ITIN lending?

*Readers frequently ask:*

Some payday lenders advertise "No SSN Required" loans but charge interest rates from 400% to 700% APR. A two-week $500 loan can trap you in hundreds of dollars of continuous renewal fees fast.

Specific warning signs that a lender is predatory:

- **APR above 36%.** Legitimate ITIN lenders cap their rates at or below 36%. Any offer above that threshold is a strong signal to walk away.
- **Upfront fees before loan disbursement.** Legitimate lenders deduct origination fees from the loan proceeds; they do not ask for cash before funding.
- **"Guaranteed approval" language.** Legitimate lenders always verify income. Guaranteed approval with no income check is a predatory signal.
- **No bureau reporting.** A lender that does not report payments to any credit bureau cannot help you build credit and may be operating outside standard compliance norms.
- **Unlicensed lender.** Verify the lender is registered with your state's financial regulator before proceeding. Each state maintains a licensed lender lookup tool.
