The funding landscape for immigrant entrepreneurs shifted sharply in early 2026. On February 2 and March 6, 2026, the U.S. Small Business Administration issued policy notices banning non-U.S. citizens, foreign nationals, and U.S. citizens or nationals living outside the United States from accessing certain SBA-guaranteed small business loans. The practical result: if you hold an ITIN and not a U.S. passport, the SBA's flagship programs are now off the table. That is the bad news.

The good news is that a parallel lending world, built around CDFIs, nonprofit microlenders, and immigrant-friendly credit unions, was never dependent on SBA rules. Those lenders have not changed their programs. This guide maps exactly who lends, how much, at what rates, and what documents they need from an ITIN business owner in 2026.

## Why does every guide I find talk about SBA loans, if I can't even apply for them?

*A question we hear often:*

Most lending guides were written before March 2026 and have not been updated. Here is the current reality in plain language.

Under the SBA's new policies, only small business owners who are U.S. citizens or nationals with their principal residence in the United States are eligible for SBA 7(a) and 504 loan programs and its Microloan and Surety Bond Guarantee programs. Previously, foreign nationals, permanent residents (green card holders), and U.S. citizens with a principal residence outside the United States could hold majority ownership in businesses receiving SBA loans.

That word "all" does a lot of work here. A single co-owner who is a lawful permanent resident can now make an otherwise qualified business ineligible for an SBA loan. In fiscal year 2025, the SBA approved over 3,300 loans to businesses partially owned by lawful permanent residents. Every one of those business owners would be ineligible under current rules.

There is organized opposition. House Small Business Committee Ranking Member Nydia Velázquez and a bipartisan coalition sent a letter to SBA Administrator Kelly Loeffler condemning the policy and urging the SBA to return to lending standards that had been in place for a quarter century. As of April 2026, legal challenges were under review, but no court order had blocked or stayed the citizenship requirement. The rule remains in full effect.

The bottom line for ITIN holders: stop reading SBA 7(a) guides and focus on the lenders below.

## What is Kiva U.S. and is it actually free money?

*This one comes up a lot:*

Kiva is not a grant. It is a nonprofit that runs a peer-to-peer crowdfunding model where lenders from around the world fund portions of your loan, and you repay them. There is only one product: 0% APR with no origination fees, no maintenance fees, and no prepayment penalties. Loan amounts run from $1,000 to $15,000 with repayment terms between 12 and 36 months.

No SSN required, no minimum credit score, no collateral. The trade-off is the process: Kiva requires borrowers to first demonstrate creditworthiness by gathering investment from friends and family before the loan goes public to the broader Kiva lender community. That takes 30 to 60 days and requires active participation from your network. For a first business loan with no U.S. credit history, though, Kiva is often the best place to start.

One underappreciated feature: Kiva borrowers can opt into business credit reporting through a partnership with the Credit Builders Alliance, which reports loan activity to Experian Business and Dun & Bradstreet. A $10,000 Kiva loan at 0% can fund your business and start building the credit file you will need to qualify for larger loans later.

## How does Accion Opportunity Fund work for ITIN borrowers?

Accion Opportunity Fund is a CDFI focused on small business owners who face barriers to traditional financing. Loans run from $5,000 to $250,000, and the organization explicitly works with ITIN holders. Applications are online. Underwriting is holistic: Accion considers personal and business financial history, the strength of the business plan, and the owner's track record.

Rates start at 5.99%, which is competitive with conventional bank rates, not the punishing double-digit territory many immigrant borrowers expect. Accion has a strong record with immigrant entrepreneurs across California, Texas, Illinois, New York, and Florida. The minimum credit score is approximately 620, and for loans above $50,000, Accion looks for at least $50,000 in annual revenue. Below that threshold, the revenue bar is lower.

One practical perk worth knowing: Accion partners with American Express, The Boston Beer Company, LegalZoom, and FedEx, giving borrowers access to networking, grants, and free services. For an immigrant entrepreneur still building a professional network in the U.S., that access can matter as much as the loan itself.

## What is DreamSpring and who qualifies?

*Readers frequently ask:*

DreamSpring is a nonprofit CDFI operating across 27 states with one of the most explicit ITIN-friendly policies of any lender on this list. You must have a Social Security Number or an ITIN to qualify, and DreamSpring lends to both operating businesses and aspiring entrepreneurs who are just starting out.

As part of a May 2026 product overhaul backed by a $500,000 Wells Fargo grant, DreamSpring capped all loan interest rates at 18.99%, with certain products starting as low as 7%. Loan sizes run from $1,000 to $2,000,000, which makes DreamSpring the single ITIN-friendly lender with the widest range on this list. Funding within 24 hours is possible on some products, and there are no application or prepayment fees.

Eligible states include Alabama, Arizona, California, Colorado, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Iowa, Kansas, Louisiana, Michigan, Mississippi, Missouri, Nebraska, Nevada, New Mexico, New York, North Carolina, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Washington, and Wyoming. That covers the major ITIN-holder population centers, including the three states with the highest concentration of ITIN borrowers: Texas, California, and Florida.

One caveat worth flagging: some borrowers have reported rates from DreamSpring that were higher than those advertised, with some experiencing rates as high as 66% before the 2026 rate cap. The new 18.99% ceiling should prevent that, but always ask for the full APR and the total cost of the loan in writing before you sign.

## Why do CDFIs say yes when banks say no?

Community Development Financial Institutions are certified by the U.S. Treasury Department and exist specifically to serve borrowers who cannot access traditional bank financing. That mandate matters in practice: CDFIs receive federal funding tied to serving underserved communities, so lending to immigrants with thin credit histories is not a risk detour for them. It is the core mission.

CDFIs underwrite for cash flow and character rather than FICO score alone. That is why they dominate this space: 10 of 13 immigrant-specific programs tracked by GrantCompass in 2026 are CDFI or CDFI-style loans.

The difference in underwriting shows up clearly in credit floors. Working Solutions CDFI has no minimum credit score. Kiva has no minimum credit score. DreamSpring requires 600+. Accion Opportunity Fund requires approximately 620. Conventional bank small business loans typically start at 680-720 and require years of tax returns and collateral.

If you have filed U.S. taxes using an ITIN, those returns demonstrate financial responsibility even without a long credit history. Two or three years of ITIN tax returns showing consistent self-employment or business income can substitute for much of the credit history a traditional bank would demand.

## What rate and loan size should I realistically expect?

*A question we hear often:*

Expectations matter here, because CDFI rates are not as low as the SBA benchmark you may have seen in older guides.

The average SBA microloan carries interest rates between 8% and 13%, with repayment terms up to six years. CDFI rates sit in a similar but wider range. Ascendus lends to borrowers with credit scores as low as 575 at 7.5%-15.99% APR. DreamSpring's new rate cap runs 7%-18.99%. Accion starts at 5.99% but goes higher for riskier profiles.

For most first-time ITIN business borrowers, a realistic range is 8%-18% APR on a loan between $5,000 and $50,000. Repay one or two loans on time and you build the credit file to access larger amounts at better rates. The credit-building ladder looks like this in practice:

1. **Months 1-3:** Kiva microloan up to $15,000 at 0%. Opt into Credit Builders Alliance reporting. Open a business checking account at an ITIN-friendly bank.
2. **Months 3-12:** Repay Kiva. Business bank statements now show 6-12 months of clean history. Apply to Accion or DreamSpring for $25,000-$75,000.
3. **Year 2+:** With a CDFI loan repaid and growing business credit, access $100,000-$500,000 from DreamSpring, Pursuit, or a CDFI in your state.

SBA microloan intermediaries vary, but many will consider businesses with as little as $2,000 per month in revenue for their smallest products, making entry possible even before your business has significant scale.

## Does my immigration status affect whether I can be approved?

No, at least not through private lenders and CDFIs. Most CDFIs, microlenders, and nonprofit lenders do not require citizenship or permanent residency. Some programs ask that you be a U.S. resident, meaning you live here, not that you are a citizen.

No federal law prohibits lenders from issuing loans to borrowers without Social Security numbers. Lenders need a way to identify borrowers and verify tax reporting obligations, and an ITIN satisfies that requirement. The SBA restriction is a policy choice for government-backed programs, not a legal prohibition on private lending.

Immigrants make up 23.6% of all entrepreneurs nationwide, even though they account for just 14.3% of the U.S. population. CDFIs know these numbers and build their lending around them. Accion focuses specifically on underserved groups, including immigrants who have trouble accessing traditional funding sources.

If your ITIN is expired, renew it before applying. An expired ITIN on a loan application will stall or kill the approval. See our guide to [renewing your ITIN](/articles/itin-renewal) before you start the loan process.

## What mistakes get ITIN business loan applications rejected?

*Readers frequently ask:*

Lenders who specialize in ITIN borrowers are experienced at working with thin credit files, but certain application errors still cause rejections.

**Applying without an EIN.** Your ITIN identifies you personally. Your EIN identifies the business. Most CDFI lenders will not process a business loan application without both. The EIN application is free at irs.gov and takes about 15 minutes online.

**Mixing personal and business bank accounts.** If all of your business income flows through your personal checking account, lenders cannot cleanly verify business revenue. Open a dedicated business account at an ITIN-friendly institution before you apply. Check our guide to [opening a bank account with an ITIN](/articles/itin-bank-account) for lenders that accept ITIN for business banking.

**No tax returns.** If you have filed U.S. taxes using an ITIN, those returns demonstrate financial responsibility even without a long credit history. Two years of filed returns is the minimum for most CDFI applications above $25,000.

**Applying to the wrong lender for your state.** DreamSpring covers 27 states but not all 50. Pursuit covers six Northeast states. Accompany Capital is New York City only. Confirm geographic coverage before you fill out the application.

**No business plan for larger amounts.** For loans above $25,000, a one-page written plan describing what the business does, who the customers are, and how the loan will be used can be the deciding factor. Your business plan, income, and identity need to tell a coherent story, even if you don't have U.S. credit.

If you need to build your personal credit profile before applying for a business loan, our guide to [building credit with an ITIN](/articles/how-to-build-credit-with-itin) covers the fastest paths to an established credit file.

## Is there anything that works like an SBA loan for immigrants now?

The closest remaining option is the SBA Microloan program, accessed through local nonprofit intermediaries rather than banks. Because the funds come through a nonprofit, underwriting tends to focus on business viability rather than immigration status. The SBA Microloan's citizenship bar applies to the SBA's own guarantee, but intermediary nonprofits retain discretion in who they serve, and many explicitly welcome ITIN holders.

The SBA Microloan program provides loans up to $50,000 through these intermediaries, and many of them explicitly accept ITIN holders.

## 8+ Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I get a business loan with just an ITIN and no SSN?**
Yes. CDFIs, microlenders, and some credit unions accept an ITIN as your personal identifier. You will also need an EIN for the business itself, which the IRS issues free of charge using your ITIN.

**Can ITIN holders get SBA loans in 2026?**
No. Effective March 1, 2026, the SBA requires that businesses seeking SBA-guaranteed financing be 100% owned by U.S. citizens or U.S. nationals. ITIN-only borrowers no longer qualify for SBA 7(a), 504, or Microloan programs.

**What is the best first loan for an ITIN business owner with no U.S. credit?**
Kiva U.S. It is a crowdfunded microloan at 0% interest with no fees and no minimum credit score. The trade-off is a 30-60 day crowdfunding period before funds are released.

**What documents do I need to apply for an ITIN business loan?**
Expect to provide your ITIN, business EIN, two years of personal and business tax returns, three to six months of business bank statements, a business plan or written description, and a government-issued photo ID such as a passport or consular card.

**Does DreamSpring accept ITIN borrowers?**
Yes. DreamSpring accepts applications from borrowers with either a Social Security Number or an ITIN. It operates in 27 states and caps rates at 18.99%, with loan sizes from $1,000 to $2,000,000.

**What is the minimum credit score for an ITIN business loan?**
It varies. Kiva and Working Solutions CDFI have no minimum. DreamSpring requires 600+ and Accion Opportunity Fund requires approximately 620.

**Can I use an ITIN business loan to buy equipment or real estate?**
CDFI loans can be used for most business purposes: working capital, equipment, inventory, tenant improvements, marketing, hiring, business acquisition, and refinancing business debt. Some CDFIs also offer commercial real estate financing. SBA Microloans cannot be used to buy real estate.

**How long does it take to get approved?**
DreamSpring advertises funding within 24 hours for some products. Accion typically funds within about one week after a complete application. Kiva takes 30-60 days because of its crowdfunding model.

**Is it legal for undocumented immigrants to borrow?**
Yes. The SBA policy restricts access to SBA-backed financing. It does not prohibit immigrant entrepreneurship or private lending. CDFIs and microlenders are free to lend to ITIN holders regardless of immigration status.

**Do I need a business bank account to apply?**
Most lenders strongly prefer or require one. A dedicated business checking account gives lenders a clean, verifiable revenue stream and is the single easiest way to strengthen an application before you submit it. Many ITIN-friendly banks and credit unions open business accounts with an ITIN and EIN, no SSN required.
