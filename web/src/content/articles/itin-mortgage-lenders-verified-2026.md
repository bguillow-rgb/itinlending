## What rates and terms should I expect on an ITIN mortgage right now?

In mid-2026, ITIN mortgage rates are running between **6.87% and 8.50%**. That range reflects the added risk and manual underwriting involved in non-QM files that do not meet government-backed standards.

To put that in context: ITIN loans carry a yield premium of 0.5%-2% over conventional 30-year fixed rates. If a conventional borrower is quoted 6.5% today, an ITIN borrower with a solid profile might pay 7.0%-7.5%. Someone with no U.S. credit history or a smaller down payment could land closer to 8.5%.

A few variables move your rate in either direction:

- Down payment size: more equity at closing signals lower lender risk and earns a better rate.
- Credit profile: if you have a FICO score, most programs require a minimum between 620 and 660 to qualify for competitive pricing.
- Income documentation type: full-doc (tax returns) typically prices better than bank-statement-only loans, which in turn price better than profit-and-loss-only submissions.
- Loan term and amount: loan amounts typically range from $100,000 to $1 million, with fixed-rate and adjustable-rate options available, and 15- and 30-year terms being most common.

There is a structural reason ITIN rates stay elevated. Conventional and government-backed loans require Social Security numbers and can be sold to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, or insured by federal agencies. ITIN mortgages do not meet those criteria, so they fall into the non-QM category and are typically held by lenders or sold to private investors. Because lenders cannot easily offload that risk to the secondary market, they price for it upfront.

FNBA's non-QM loan programs allow ITIN or SSN borrowers a path to homeownership when conventional mortgage guidelines do not fit their situation. ## What makes Quontic Bank different from other ITIN lenders?

*Readers frequently ask* why Quontic keeps appearing on best-of lists for immigrant borrowers. There are two concrete reasons.

First, structure: as a federally chartered digital bank and U.S. Treasury designated Community Development Financial Institution (CDFI), Quontic is one of very few mortgage lenders that can accept alternative income documentation from borrowers who have strong credit and a substantial down payment.

Second, documentation flexibility: Quontic's ITIN loan program does not require tax returns, W-2s, or extensive bank statements to qualify. It also accepts 100% gift funds for down payment and closing costs.

The tradeoff is the down payment floor. Depending on which program you qualify for, the minimum is 20% down. For loans above $1.5 million, the minimum rises further. A loan up to $3 million requires at least 30% down and a minimum FICO of 740. Quontic is best suited for borrowers who have the equity but lack traditional income documentation.

## What documents do I actually need to apply?

*A question we hear often.* The document list varies by lender, but these items appear on nearly every ITIN mortgage checklist:

### Standard ITIN mortgage document checklist

| Document | Details |
|---|---|
| IRS ITIN letter | Issued by the IRS confirming your ITIN number |
| Federal tax returns | 2 years filed using your ITIN |
| Government-issued photo ID | Passport, Matricula Consular, or driver's license |
| Income proof | Pay stubs, W-2/1099, bank statements (12-24 months), or P&L for self-employed |
| Bank statements | Typically 2-3 months; up to 24 months for bank-statement programs |
| Proof of residence | Utility bill, lease agreement, or government correspondence |
| Alternative credit references | 3-4 references with 12 months of on-time payment history if no U.S. credit score |

On the alternative credit front, acceptable references include on-time rent payments verified by your landlord, utility payment history from electric, gas, water, or phone companies, car insurance payment history, and other recurring obligations. Most lenders require at least 3-4 references covering 12 months of on-time payments.

For self-employed borrowers: if you have been operating your business for at least one year, many lenders will accept a profit-and-loss statement prepared by an accountant, or 12-24 months of business bank statements, in place of tax returns. New American Funding's Texas program, for example, accepts full doc, one-year tax returns, one-year W-2 or 1099, one-year P&L (no bank statements required), or 12 months of bank statements.

## Does my down payment amount really change what I can borrow?

*This one comes up a lot.* Yes, meaningfully. The down payment on an ITIN mortgage does three things at once: it determines your maximum LTV, it signals creditworthiness to the underwriter, and it directly affects your interest rate tier.

The minimum down payment for an ITIN mortgage in 2026 typically runs 10%-25%. While 20% is the standard for most non-QM products, some programs accept 15% with strong alternative credit.

Property type and loan-to-value ratios will ultimately set your specific cash-to-close requirement. A two-unit or three-unit property will generally require more down than a single-family home, and investment properties typically require more than primary residences.

A practical illustration: on a $350,000 home purchase, the difference between 10% and 20% down is $35,000. That $35,000 also buys you a lower rate. If a 10% down scenario prices at 8.25% and a 20% down scenario prices at 7.50%, the rate difference on a $315,000 loan versus a $280,000 loan compounds into tens of thousands of dollars over 30 years. Running both scenarios through an amortization calculator before you decide how much to put down is worth the 15 minutes it takes.

Do not overlook closing costs. They typically run 3%-5% of the loan amount, so your true cash-to-close on a $350,000 purchase with 20% down can reach $80,500 to $87,500 total. Plan for that number, not just the down payment.

## I have bad credit. Is an ITIN mortgage still possible?

Yes, though it narrows your lender pool and raises your rate. The key distinction is between having *low* U.S. credit and having *no* U.S. credit history at all.

**No U.S. credit history:** This is actually the more manageable scenario. A traditional U.S. credit score is not always required for approval. Lenders build an alternative credit profile using 12 months of on-time payments for rent, utilities, and insurance. FNBA has no published minimum FICO and accepts alternative credit references. Quontic's flexible documentation program does not require W-2s or tax returns for income qualification either.

**Low U.S. credit score (below 620):** Options narrow. Most non-QM programs floor at 620-660. Some portfolio lenders and community banks will go lower with compensating factors, such as a 25%+ down payment, 6-12 months of mortgage payments sitting in reserves, or a co-borrower with stronger credit.

Building credit in parallel is worth doing regardless of where you stand today. ## What is the fastest path to getting approved?

The most common reason ITIN mortgage applications stall is documentation gaps discovered mid-process, not initial eligibility.

ITIN loan approvals often fall apart for reasons that never came up in the first conversation with a lender. Down payment thresholds, how income is documented, and whether your credit references are considered acceptable can all change the outcome, even when you qualify in principle.

Five things that move your application forward fastest:

1. **Confirm your ITIN is active.** An expired or soon-expiring ITIN must be renewed before you apply. See our [ITIN renewal guide](/articles/itin-renewal) if yours is approaching expiration.
2. **File two years of tax returns with your ITIN before applying.** This is the single most universal requirement across every lender in this guide.
3. **Open a U.S. bank account and maintain 2-3 months of documented statements.** See our guide on [opening a bank account with an ITIN](/articles/itin-bank-account) if you have not yet done this.
4. **Assemble your alternative credit file in advance.** Request 12-month payment histories in writing from your landlord, utility provider, and insurance company. Lenders will ask for these; having them ready prevents weeks of delay.
5. **Work with a mortgage broker who regularly closes ITIN loans.** Brokers who specialize in non-QM lending have relationships with multiple ITIN lenders, which means they can match your specific documentation and down-payment profile to the program most likely to approve you. Because rates for ITIN loans can be higher, shopping around is a financial necessity, not an optional step.
