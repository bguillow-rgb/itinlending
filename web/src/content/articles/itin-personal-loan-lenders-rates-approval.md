## Is it actually legal to get a personal loan with an ITIN?

No federal law prohibits lenders from issuing personal loans to borrowers without Social Security numbers. Lenders need a way to identify borrowers and verify tax reporting obligations, and an ITIN satisfies that requirement. Whether a lender accepts an ITIN is a business decision, not a legal limitation.

This matters because many ITIN holders assume the door is legally shut. It isn't. Approval is most common through credit unions, online lenders, and specialty finance companies, and many ITIN personal loans are unsecured, though secured options may offer lower interest rates.

Immigration status and ITIN eligibility are also two separate things. ITINs are issued to certain visa holders, non-resident aliens with U.S. income, and dependents who must file U.S. tax returns but don't qualify for Social Security numbers. Lenders assess income stability, credit history, and repayment capacity rather than visa category.

## What documents do I need to apply?

*Readers frequently ask:* whether a foreign passport is enough, or whether a U.S. driver's license is required.

A foreign passport works fine. Most ITIN-accepting lenders ask for a combination of the following:

- **ITIN documentation:** The official IRS letter (CP-565 or W-7 approval notice) assigning your ITIN number.
- **Government-issued photo ID:** A foreign passport is universally accepted. Some lenders also accept a Matricula Consular or state ID.
- **Proof of income:** Pay stubs for employees, or 12-24 months of bank statements for self-employed borrowers. Some lenders also accept IRS tax transcripts.
- **U.S. bank account:** Needed for fund disbursement and automatic repayments. Most lenders require a U.S.-based checking account, though some also accept savings accounts.
- **Proof of address:** A utility bill, lease agreement, or bank statement with your current U.S. address.

If your ITIN has expired, most lenders will put the file on hold. See our guide to [renewing your ITIN](/how-to-get-an-itin) before you apply.

## What credit score do I actually need?

Credit requirements vary widely. Some ITIN personal loan programs require a traditional credit score, often in the 580-680 range or higher. Others place less emphasis on credit history and rely more on income and banking activity.

Here is the honest breakdown by lender tier:

- **660 or above:** Opens Wells Fargo, Alliant CU, PNC, and TD Bank. These are your best rates.
- **580-659:** Stilt and Security Service FCU remain accessible. Expect rates of 15%-25%.
- **No U.S. score or thin file:** Oportun and Stilt evaluate income and employment history instead. MAF has no score requirement at all.

Credit can be evaluated using traditional or alternative methods. Traditional credit relies on a score from a major credit bureau, often with a minimum of around 620. Alternative credit may include documented payment history for rent, utilities, phone bills, insurance, or other recurring obligations.

If you've been paying rent and utilities consistently but have no formal credit score, ask specifically about alternative credit underwriting when you call a credit union. Many will accept a letter from your landlord or 12 months of on-time utility records. For practical steps on building a score from scratch, see our guide to [building credit with an ITIN](/articles/how-to-build-credit-with-itin).

## How do credit unions compare to online lenders for ITIN borrowers?

*A question we hear often:* "Is it worth the extra steps to join a credit union, or should I just apply online?"

The answer depends on your timeline and credit profile. Credit unions like Alliant, Security Service FCU, and Strata CU (Kern County, CA) offer meaningfully lower rates and longer terms, but they require membership steps that can add 30-90 days before you're eligible to borrow. Many local and regional credit unions accept ITINs, especially in states with large immigrant populations, including Self-Help Federal Credit Union, Latino Community Credit Union, and Mission Asset Fund. Membership is usually based on geography or community ties.

Online lenders like Stilt and Oportun move faster. Online ITIN loan lenders often fund within 1-2 business days once approved. Credit unions and banks may take 3-5 business days.

Our editorial stance: if you need money in the next 2-4 weeks and have a thin credit file, Stilt or Oportun are your most realistic options. If you have 3-4 months to plan and a credit score above 640, join Alliant CU or find a community credit union in your area first. The rate savings over a 3-5 year loan term are substantial.

Community Development Financial Institutions (CDFIs) are nonprofit lenders certified by the U.S. Treasury that focus on underbanked communities. They typically accept ITINs and carry lower interest rates than online lenders. CDFIs are an underused resource for ITIN borrowers, especially in California, Texas, and Florida.

## What are my actual approval odds?

Approval odds for ITIN personal loans are harder to pin down than for conventional loans because ITIN-accepting lenders are a smaller universe and don't always publish approval rates. Based on aggregated lender criteria, here is a realistic framework:

| Borrower Profile | Approval Odds | Recommended Lenders |
|---|---|---|

You'll generally have the best odds of approval if you have a good credit score, steady income, and a low debt-to-income ratio. For ITIN borrowers specifically, income documentation is the single most influential factor after credit score.

Existing debt also plays a role. Lenders assess your debt-to-income ratio to determine whether you can reasonably take on another monthly payment. High existing debt relative to income can limit approval amounts or result in a denial. Many lenders follow guidelines around keeping total debt payments under 36% of gross monthly income, according to standard underwriting guidance from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB).

## Should I get a secured or unsecured ITIN personal loan?

Most ITIN personal loans are unsecured, meaning no collateral is required. Approval is based on income, credit profile, and repayment capacity rather than pledged assets. Because there's no collateral backing the loan, unsecured options generally carry higher interest rates and stricter approval standards.

Secured options do exist and can improve your odds. Oportun offers secured personal loans with a paid-off vehicle as collateral, with a maximum loan amount of $18,500. Secured personal loans through Oportun are available in AZ, CA, FL, IL, NV, NJ, TX, and UT. If you own a car free and clear and need more than the $10,000 unsecured cap, a secured Oportun loan is worth exploring, particularly for borrowers in California, Texas, or Florida.

## How do I avoid predatory lenders?

ITIN borrowers are frequently targeted by predatory lenders. Red flags to watch for:

- **APRs above 36%:** The CFPB and most consumer advocates use 36% as the upper limit of an affordable loan. Anything higher is high-cost lending.
- **Upfront fees before funding:** A legitimate lender never asks for cash before releasing loan proceeds.
- **No NMLS number or state license:** Verify any lender at the NMLS Consumer Access portal (nmlsconsumeraccess.org). If you have doubts about a lender's reliability, confirm it is registered in the state where you reside.
- **Pressure to sign quickly:** Legitimate lenders give you time to review all terms before signing.
- **Balloon payments or vague repayment terms:** All terms, including total repayment cost, should be disclosed in writing before you sign.

The lenders on our verified table are registered institutions with confirmed ITIN programs. Any lender not on a recognized comparison platform or CFPB database warrants extra caution.

## Step-by-step: how to apply for an ITIN personal loan

1. **Check your ITIN status first.** If your ITIN expired (ITINs with middle digits 70-88, 90-92, or 94-99 required renewal after 2018), most lenders will pause your file. Renew via IRS Form W-7 before applying.
2. **Check your credit.** Pull your free report at AnnualCreditReport.com. ITIN holders who have existing U.S. accounts should have a file. If not, review our guide to [checking your credit score with an ITIN](/articles/itin-credit-score-check).
3. **Gather documents upfront.** ITIN letter, passport, 2-3 months of pay stubs or 12 months of bank statements, and proof of address.
4. **Pre-qualify without a hard pull.** Oportun lets you check your approval odds and potential rates online before you apply, and this won't affect your credit score. Alliant, PNC, and TD Bank also offer soft-pull pre-qualification.
5. **Compare at least two offers.** Pricing and requirements vary widely between lenders, and choosing the wrong option can be costly.
6. **Submit a complete file.** The most preventable cause of denial is incomplete documentation. Missing or inconsistent paperwork forces underwriters to pause or decline files that might otherwise qualify. Confirm documentation requirements with your lender and submit everything at once.
7. **Enroll in autopay after approval.** Many lenders offer a 0.25% rate discount for autopay. Wells Fargo offers exactly that. It also protects your credit score by eliminating the risk of missed payments.

Once approved, online ITIN loan lenders often fund within 1-2 business days.

## FAQs

**Can I get a personal loan with an ITIN and no credit history?**
Yes. Lenders like Oportun and Mission Asset Fund accept borrowers with little or no U.S. credit history. They evaluate income, banking activity, and alternative credit data such as rent and utility payments instead of a traditional score.

**What credit score do I need for an ITIN personal loan?**
It varies widely. Prime lenders like Wells Fargo and Alliant Credit Union look for 660 or higher. Mid-tier lenders like PNC and Stilt work with scores around 580-660. Oportun and CDFIs may approve borrowers with no score at all, though rates will be higher.

**How much can I borrow with an ITIN personal loan?**
Loan amounts range from $300 (Oportun, new customers) to $100,000 (Wells Fargo and Alliant Credit Union for well-qualified borrowers). Most ITIN borrowers without an established U.S. credit file find approvals in the $1,000-$15,000 range.

**What documents do I need to apply for a personal loan with an ITIN?**
Typically: your ITIN letter or card from the IRS, a valid government-issued photo ID (passport accepted), proof of income (pay stubs, tax returns, or 12-24 months of bank statements), and a U.S. bank account for fund deposit and repayment.

**Do ITIN personal loans report to the credit bureaus?**
Most do. Wells Fargo, Alliant, PNC, TD Bank, and Stilt all report to the major bureaus. Making on-time payments builds your U.S. credit file, which improves your odds and rates on future borrowing.

**How long does it take to get funded after approval?**
Online lenders and credit unions with ITIN programs typically fund within 1-2 business days. Some, like Alliant Credit Union and Wells Fargo, offer same-day or next-day funding. Traditional bank branches may take 3-5 business days.

**Can I get an ITIN personal loan with bad credit?**
Yes, though your options narrow and rates rise. Oportun caps APRs at 35.99% and has no minimum credit score. Stilt works with thin files. CDFIs like Mission Asset Fund offer 0% interest immigration loans that accept ITINs with no credit requirement.

**Is it legal for a lender to issue a personal loan to someone without an SSN?**
Completely legal. No federal law prohibits personal loans to borrowers without an SSN. An ITIN satisfies lenders' identity verification and tax-reporting requirements. Whether to accept an ITIN is each lender's own business decision, not a legal barrier.

**Which states have the best access to ITIN personal loans?**
California, Texas, and Florida have the widest lender coverage because of large immigrant populations. Credit unions like Strata CU (CA), Security Service FCU (TX), and many community lenders in FL maintain explicit ITIN programs. Online lenders like Oportun and Stilt serve most states.

**What is the difference between an ITIN personal loan and a credit-builder loan?**
A personal loan gives you a lump sum upfront to spend. A credit-builder loan holds the funds in a locked account while you make payments, then releases the money at the end. Credit-builder loans are designed purely to build credit history; personal loans serve immediate cash needs. See our guide to [ITIN credit-builder loans](/articles/itin-credit-builder-loan) for a full comparison.
