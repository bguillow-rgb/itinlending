ITIN holders can finance income-producing and commercial property in 2026 without a Social Security number. The key is choosing the right loan structure: DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio) loans, portfolio commercial programs, and private capital all accept an ITIN as your primary identifier. The property's cash flow does most of the heavy lifting, which is a real advantage for immigrant entrepreneurs who show modest personal income on paper.

## What exactly is a commercial real estate loan for ITIN holders, and how does it differ from a business loan?

*A question we hear often:* many readers confuse commercial property financing with the operating business loans covered in our [ITIN business loans guide](/itin-business-loans). The distinction matters.

A commercial real estate loan is secured by a property, say a rental apartment building, a retail strip, a small office, or an industrial unit. The lender's primary underwriting concern is whether that property generates enough rent to cover the mortgage payment. An operating business loan funds payroll, inventory, or working capital and is repaid from business revenue rather than rental income.

For ITIN borrowers, this is actually good news. A DSCR loan qualifies you based on the property's income, not your personal income, W-2s, or tax returns. If the property's net operating income covers the mortgage payment by a sufficient ratio (typically 1.20x-1.25x), the loan qualifies. A self-employed immigrant with a lean tax return can still compete for investment property financing as long as the deal itself pencils out.

## What types of lenders actually approve these loans with an ITIN?

Commercial lenders range from banks and mutual companies to private lending institutions and hard-money shops. Standards vary widely, and private-market lenders tend to be more flexible on financial qualifications than banks. For ITIN holders specifically, three categories work consistently in 2026:

Portfolio lenders and non-QM specialty shops keep loans on their own books rather than selling them to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac. One verified example: a Brazilian investor in Boca Raton obtained financing through a portfolio lender, structured as an ITIN-based loan, with a $2.24M loan at 70% LTV, an 8.5% five-year fixed rate on a 25-year amortization, and a 1.35x DSCR.

Several non-QM lenders also maintain explicit foreign national or ITIN tracks that skip personal income verification entirely. Products labeled for non-U.S. borrowers, ITIN, and no U.S. credit appear in the DSCR market, with foreign national DSCR rates running 7.50%-9.50% on 30-year fixed terms for loan sizes of $200K-$5M.

Private and hard-money lenders are the fastest path to approval but carry the highest rates, often 10%-13%+. They lend primarily on collateral value and work best as bridge financing while you stabilize a property, then refinance into a DSCR product.

*This one comes up a lot:* readers want real numbers before they call a lender.

As of mid-2026, DSCR loan rates for residential investment properties generally run 6.5%-8.75%, while commercial DSCR loans typically run higher, from about 7.25%-10.75%, depending on the deal. Where you land depends heavily on your credit score, your loan-to-value, and how comfortably the property's income clears the required DSCR threshold.

## How does the DSCR calculation work, and what happens if my property falls short?

*Readers frequently ask:* "my numbers are close, can I still qualify?"

DSCR measures a property's ability to pay its debt service. The formula is net operating income divided by annual debt service. A DSCR of 1.25 means the property produces 25% more income than is needed to cover annual principal and interest. Most commercial mortgage lenders in 2026 require a minimum DSCR between 1.20 and 1.35, depending on property type, occupancy, loan program, sponsor strength, and market conditions. Retail and office properties face stricter scrutiny; multifamily typically gets the most favorable coverage requirements.

If your DSCR comes in under the minimum, you have three levers: raise the rent estimate with a fresh appraiser rent survey, buy down the rate with points to lower the payment, or increase your down payment to shrink the loan amount and improve the ratio. Adding a co-borrower with verifiable U.S. income is a fourth option that some lenders allow.

## Do I need a U.S. LLC or EIN before applying, and what documents should I prepare?

Many lenders require you to form a U.S. LLC before closing, typically through a U.S. attorney or online formation service at a cost of $1,000-$3,000. Holding the property in an LLC also protects your personal assets and can simplify future financing.

Your ITIN identifies you as an individual taxpayer. An EIN (Employer Identification Number, free from IRS.gov) identifies your business entity. Applying with both strengthens your application and is required by most CDFI and credit union lenders.

Typical document checklist for an ITIN commercial DSCR loan:

- Valid ITIN (IRS assignment letter or most recent tax return showing the number)
- Passport or consular ID for identity verification
- Two years of tax returns filed under your ITIN (some DSCR programs waive this for income qualification)
- Signed lease agreements or a market rent appraisal for the subject property
- Three to six months of personal and business bank statements
- LLC formation documents and EIN confirmation
- Proof of funds for the down payment (sourced and seasoned, ideally 60+ days)

A large deposit that lands in your account two weeks before closing without a paper trail will get flagged. Sourced and seasoned funds matter.

## Are SBA 504 loans available to buy commercial real estate with an ITIN?

No, and this is a hard stop. Under SBA Policy Notice 5000-876441, effective March 1, 2026, SBA rules require that 100% of all direct and indirect owners of a small business applicant be U.S. citizens or U.S. nationals maintaining their principal residence in the United States. ITIN holders and green card holders are both excluded. That means both the SBA 504 program (used for owner-occupied commercial real estate) and the 7(a) program are off the table for ITIN borrowers.

The private DSCR and portfolio market has stepped into this gap. ## What states have the most ITIN-friendly commercial lenders?

Some states have more developed ITIN lending markets than others, particularly those with larger immigrant populations. California, Texas, Florida, New York, and Illinois tend to offer the broadest range of options.

California is the largest market, with numerous non-QM and portfolio lenders running bilingual programs. Texas is especially strong for multifamily and mixed-use deals in Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, and Austin. Florida has an active market in South Florida centered on Miami-Dade and Broward counties, with several lenders specifically experienced with foreign national and ITIN borrowers.

One line item worth watching regardless of state: most ITIN and DSCR programs carry a prepayment penalty of 1 to 5 years, often stepping down each year (5-4-3-2-1). If you plan to refinance or sell within 24 months, that structure matters more than the headline rate. Negotiate it before signing, especially in markets where refinancing opportunities can appear quickly.

## What practical steps should I take before applying?

1. **Get or verify your ITIN.** If yours is expired, see our [ITIN renewal guide](/how-to-get-an-itin) before starting any loan application.
2. **Form a U.S. LLC and get an EIN.** Most commercial lenders expect a clean entity on title.
3. **Open a U.S. business bank account.** Some banks allow ITIN-based accounts, including Bank of America, Wells Fargo, and TD Bank.
4. **Run your DSCR before talking to a lender.** Divide the property's projected annual net operating income by the annual mortgage payment. If the result is below 1.20x, adjust your offer price or down payment before you apply.
5. **Budget for 25%-30% down plus reserves.** Plan on 20%-25% down for a standard purchase (75%-80% loan-to-value). Commercial programs typically require an additional 5% and six to twelve months of payment reserves.
6. **Work with a bilingual broker.** ITIN commercial loans are a niche within a niche. A broker who specializes in non-QM or foreign national lending will know which lenders are actively closing these deals in your target market.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I get a commercial real estate loan with an ITIN and no SSN?**
Yes. DSCR loans, portfolio commercial lenders, and private/hard-money lenders all accept ITINs in place of an SSN. Qualification turns on the property's income, so your immigration status is not the deciding factor.

**What DSCR ratio do lenders require for ITIN borrowers?**
Most commercial DSCR lenders in 2026 require a minimum ratio of 1.20x-1.35x, meaning the property's net operating income must exceed the annual debt payment by at least 20%-35%. Some programs accept 1.0x with a larger down payment.

**How much do I need to put down on a commercial loan with an ITIN?**
Plan on 25%-30% down for most ITIN commercial DSCR programs. Residential DSCR (1-4 units) often requires only 20%-25%. A larger down payment can offset a lower DSCR or a thin credit file.

**Do I need a U.S. LLC to get an ITIN commercial loan?**
Many portfolio and DSCR lenders prefer or require that the property be held in a U.S. LLC. Forming one creates a clean entity for title and simplifies underwriting. It is not legally mandatory in all programs, but it materially improves approval odds.

**What credit score do lenders want for a commercial DSCR loan with an ITIN?**
Commercial DSCR lenders typically want a 680+ FICO. Some ITIN-specific programs accept 620-640 with compensating factors such as a larger down payment, a strong DSCR above 1.35x, or 12 months of alternative credit history.

**Are SBA 504 loans available to ITIN holders for commercial property?**
No. As of March 1, 2026, SBA Policy Notice 5000-876441 requires all business owners to be U.S. citizens or nationals. ITIN holders are excluded from all SBA-guaranteed programs, including 504 loans used for owner-occupied commercial real estate.
