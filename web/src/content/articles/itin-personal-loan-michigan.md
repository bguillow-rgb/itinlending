Michigan has one of the Midwest's fastest-growing immigrant communities, yet practical guidance on personal loans for ITIN holders here is thin. Several lenders actively serve Michigan residents who hold an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number, and at least one Michigan-based credit union explicitly lists ITIN acceptance on its application page. This guide cuts through that with verified lenders, current APR ranges, required documents, and practical steps to get approved in 2026.

## Is it even legal to get a personal loan in Michigan with an ITIN instead of an SSN?

U.S. federal lending laws do not prohibit financial institutions from extending credit to ITIN holders. Michigan follows the same federal framework, so lenders in the state are free to accept an ITIN as the primary borrower identifier. Lenders need a way to identify borrowers and verify tax reporting obligations, and an ITIN satisfies that requirement.

The practical challenge is finding which lenders actually welcome ITIN holders rather than just technically permitting them. The sections below name those lenders specifically.

## Which lenders accept ITIN for personal loans in Michigan?

*A question we hear often:* finding a lender that explicitly says "yes" to ITIN in Michigan rather than leaving you guessing.

Consumers Credit Union, a Michigan-based institution, accepts applications from both ITIN and SSN holders, with no application fees for Michigan residents. That makes it one of the few home-state options with an explicit public commitment to ITIN borrowers.

Beyond Consumers CU, several national lenders operate in Michigan:

- Stilt specializes in immigrants and international students, offering loans of $1,000 to $35,000 with APRs typically 7%-36%. It considers education, employment, and visa status instead of a FICO score, uses a soft pull only at prequalification, and reports to all three bureaus. It operates in 16 states, Michigan included.

- Oportun lends $500 to $10,000, caps APRs at 35.99%, requires no credit history, and actively markets to ITIN holders while reporting monthly payment history to the major credit bureaus.

- OneMain Financial reviews applicants with fair or poor credit and accepts ITIN documentation. If your credit history is limited, you can secure the loan using collateral (a vehicle title, for example), which can lower your interest rate.

- Alliant Credit Union offers quick decisions and same-day funding on approvals. Loan amounts run $1,000 to $100,000 with repayment terms of 12-60 months, and the minimum APR of 8.74% is competitive.

- PNC Bank offers personal loans to ITIN holders with amounts of $1,000 to $35,000 and an APR as low as 8.14%, depending on creditworthiness.

## What documents do I actually need to apply?

*This one comes up a lot:* borrowers often assume the document list will be impossible. It is not, but you need to gather everything before applying.

Most ITIN lenders accept an unexpired foreign passport, a Consular ID (Matrícula Consular), a U.S. state driver's license or state ID, or a foreign national identity card.

For income, lenders accept 3-6 months of U.S. bank statements showing regular deposits, 1099 contractor payment logs, cash-deposit affidavits, or your last two years of federal tax returns (Form 1040) filed with your ITIN.

The standard checklist for Michigan ITIN personal loan applications:

1. ITIN letter (IRS Form CP565 or the original ITIN assignment letter)
2. Valid photo ID: passport, Matrícula Consular, or Michigan state ID
3. Proof of income: pay stubs (2-3 recent), bank statements (3-6 months), or two years of tax returns
4. Michigan address verification: utility bill, lease agreement, or bank statement with your address
5. Secondary ID if the lender requires it (some credit unions ask for two forms)

Having all five items scanned and ready before you start an application can cut processing time from days to hours.

## What if I have bad credit or no U.S. credit history at all?

*Readers frequently ask:* whether a thin credit file or past delinquencies close every door.

They narrow your options, but they do not close them. Several lenders now approve scores well below 600 using factors beyond your FICO number. For Michigan ITIN borrowers with thin or troubled credit, two paths are the most accessible:

Community Development Financial Institutions (CDFIs): Local credit unions designated as CDFIs are often the most affordable ITIN lenders around. In Michigan, look for CDFIs affiliated with the Michigan CDFI Coalition, which supports organizations serving underbanked immigrant communities. Mission Asset Fund's Lending Circles program offers 0% APR loans of $300-$2,400 specifically to help borrowers build credit with zero interest.

Secured personal loans: If unsecured approval looks unlikely, adding collateral changes the math. Consumers Credit Union offers secured personal loans to Michigan residents, and OneMain Financial allows a vehicle title as collateral to lower your rate. Even a share-secured loan at a local credit union (where your own savings back the loan) reports to bureaus and builds your profile within 12-18 months.

One important note: avoid high-cost payday or title-loan products marketed to ITIN holders in Michigan. An ITIN personal loan at 18%-30% APR is dramatically cheaper than a payday loan at 300%-700% APR, so refinancing payday debt with a personal loan usually saves significant money.

## How do I actually apply, step by step?

1. Check your credit file first. Use Experian's free ITIN credit check or pull your report at AnnualCreditReport.com. Even a thin file tells you where you stand. See our guide on [checking your credit score with an ITIN](/articles/itin-credit-score-check).
2. Prequalify with at least two lenders. Stilt and Oportun both use soft pulls that do not affect your score.
3. Gather your documents (see list above) before submitting any formal application.
4. Apply to your top choice. The formal application triggers a hard pull. Submit to your backup within 14 days if needed: bureaus typically count multiple inquiries in a 14-day window as one.
5. Review the offer carefully. Confirm the APR (not just the monthly payment), loan term, prepayment penalties, and whether the lender reports to all three bureaus.
6. Accept and receive funds. Stilt and Alliant can fund same-day or next business day. CDFIs may take 3-7 business days.

## FAQs

Can I get a personal loan in Michigan with only an ITIN and no SSN?
Yes. No federal law prevents lenders from issuing personal loans to ITIN holders. Consumers Credit Union, Stilt, Oportun, and OneMain Financial all accept an ITIN in place of an SSN for Michigan borrowers.

What credit score do I need for an ITIN personal loan in Michigan?
It depends on the lender. Stilt and Oportun work with thin or no U.S. credit files. Consumers Credit Union and PNC typically look for a score of 580 or above, while Alliant Credit Union and Wells Fargo prefer 660 or higher.

What documents do I need to apply for a personal loan with an ITIN in Michigan?
Most Michigan lenders ask for your ITIN letter or IRS CP565, a valid government photo ID (passport, Matrícula Consular, or state ID), 2-3 recent pay stubs or 3-6 months of bank statements, proof of a Michigan address, and your last two years of federal tax returns.

How much can I borrow with an ITIN personal loan in Michigan?
Loan amounts vary by lender. Oportun starts at $500 and caps at $10,000. Consumers Credit Union and Security Service FCU go up to $50,000. Alliant Credit Union can reach $100,000 for well-qualified borrowers.

Does an ITIN personal loan in Michigan help build my U.S. credit score?
Yes, as long as the lender reports to the three major bureaus: Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. Stilt and Oportun both report monthly payment history, so on-time payments actively build your U.S. credit profile.

Can I get an ITIN personal loan in Michigan with bad credit?
Yes. Oportun caps APRs at 35.99% and has no minimum credit score requirement. OneMain Financial also reviews fair and poor credit applicants and allows you to add collateral, like a vehicle title, to improve approval odds.
