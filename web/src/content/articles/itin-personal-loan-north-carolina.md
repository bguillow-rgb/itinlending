North Carolina is home to two of the most ITIN-friendly credit unions in the country: Latino Community Credit Union (LCCU) and Self-Help Credit Union. If you live in the state and hold an ITIN instead of a Social Security Number, your borrowing options are genuinely strong compared with what most other states offer. This guide covers every real lender, rate range, and document you will need.

## Can I actually get a personal loan in North Carolina with just an ITIN?

Yes. ITIN holders can get personal loans through credit unions, Community Development Financial Institutions (CDFIs), and some community banks. No federal law requires an SSN for a personal loan.

North Carolina is a particularly good state to be in because two mission-driven credit unions with ITIN programs are headquartered here, giving residents local access that most states simply do not have.

Latino Community Credit Union (LCCU) is based in Durham. Founded in 2000, it was the first Latino credit union in North Carolina and remains the only one in the state. It now has over 90,000 members and 15 locations. That branch footprint, combined with bilingual staff at every location, makes it the logical first stop for most ITIN borrowers in NC.

## Which lenders in North Carolina accept an ITIN for personal loans?

*A question we hear often:* there are three realistic options for North Carolina residents, each with different loan limits and membership rules.

| Self-Help Credit Union / FCU | CDFI Credit Union | ~8%-18% | $500-$25,000 | Yes | Flexible (alt. data OK) |
| TD Bank (TD Fit Loan) | Bank | 7.99%-23.99% | $2,000-$50,000 | Yes | ~660 score preferred |

**Latino Community Credit Union (LCCU):**
LCCU accepts ITIN for personal loans ranging from $500 to $15,000, with APRs typically between 10% and 18% depending on your credit profile. It was built specifically for Spanish speakers and Latino immigrant communities. Published consumer loan terms require proof of income, six months of personal account statements (or one year of tax returns if self-employed), and at least one year of credit history for loans above $500. The maximum is $20,000 for individual or joint applicants. Terms run six to 48 months, with no prepayment penalty.

**Self-Help Credit Union / FCU:**
Self-Help FCU is a community development credit union that accepts ITIN for both membership and lending. Personal loans run $500 to $25,000, with APRs often between 10% and 18% depending on credit profile. It operates branches in California, North Carolina, Florida, Illinois, and Wisconsin. Anyone in the U.S. can join by paying a one-time $5 fee to the Center for Community Self-Help nonprofit, so you do not need to live near a branch.

**TD Bank (TD Fit Loan):**
TD Bank explicitly lists ITIN as an acceptable form of identification on its application. The fixed APR runs 7.99%-23.99% (as of June 2026), with no origination, application, or prepayment fees. North Carolina is one of the states where TD Fit Loan is available, making it a solid bank-channel option for borrowers with an established credit score around 660 or above.

## What credit score do I need, and what if I have no U.S. credit history?

*This one comes up a lot:* the good news is that both NC-based credit unions use flexible, income-first underwriting designed for immigrants building credit from scratch.

Self-Help FCU and Latino Community Credit Union are among the CDFIs most consistently cited for ITIN-holder service. Membership is typically based on geography or community ties. LCCU, based in North Carolina but serving members across several southeastern states, is particularly well known for Spanish-language service and immigrant-inclusive underwriting.

If you have no U.S. credit file at all, start at LCCU, which lends up to $500 with no formal credit history requirement. After repaying that loan on time, your file will be substantial enough to qualify for LCCU's larger consumer loan tier (up to $20,000). According to the CFPB, lenders may accept an ITIN in place of an SSN, but it is a per-lender policy rather than a legal requirement, so confirm in writing before you apply. That way a denial does not leave a hard-inquiry footprint on your report.

Credit unions are nonprofits that often carry rates below what banks charge. The National Credit Union Administration (NCUA) caps APRs at 18% for most federal credit unions.

## What documents do I need to gather before I apply?

*Readers frequently ask:* lenders in NC generally want the same core set of documents, though each institution has slight variations.

Here is a practical checklist:

- **Valid ITIN** (your IRS CP565 assignment letter confirms the number is active)
- **Government-issued photo ID** such as a passport, Matrícula Consular, or foreign national ID
- **Proof of NC residency** such as a utility bill, lease agreement, or bank statement with your address
- **Income verification** such as the most recent two to three months of pay stubs, or one to two years of tax returns if you are self-employed
- **Six months of bank statements** to show cash-flow stability
- **Membership fee** for LCCU ($8 one-time, paid to the Latino Community Development Center) or Self-Help ($5 one-time)

Make sure your ITIN is current. The IRS began expiring unused ITINs in 2017, so if yours has not appeared on a tax return in the last three years, renew it before you apply. Step-by-step renewal instructions are in our [ITIN renewal guide](/how-to-get-an-itin).

## How do rates and amounts compare to other ITIN personal loan options?

*A question we hear often:* North Carolina borrowers are in a better position than ITIN holders in most other states.

ITIN-focused credit unions like Self-Help FCU and Latino Community CU typically run 10%-18% APR. Mission Asset Fund Lending Circles charge 0% APR, Stilt ranges from roughly 8%-25%, Camino Financial runs 18%-36%, and Oportun caps at 35.99%. Your actual rate depends on credit profile, income, and loan amount.

For context, most NC personal loans from mainstream lenders sit around a 13.35% average APR according to recent market data. That puts LCCU and Self-Help FCU rates right in the competitive band, without requiring an SSN. That is a noticeably better outcome than going to an online subprime lender, where ITIN borrowers with thin credit often see rates above 30%.

If you need more than $25,000 or have a credit score above 660, the TD Fit Loan is worth comparing. It goes up to $50,000, carries no fees, and North Carolina is one of the 15 states where the product is available.

## Will the loan build my U.S. credit history?

Yes, provided the lender reports to the credit bureaus. Stilt, Self-Help FCU, Latino Community Credit Union, and Mission Asset Fund all report to all three major bureaus. TD Bank reports repayment history to all three as well. Every on-time payment builds the U.S. credit file you need for future borrowing. Pair the loan with a [secured credit card](/itin-credit-cards) or a [credit builder loan](/articles/itin-credit-builder-loan) and your score can reach a qualifying tier for mainstream lenders within 12-24 months.

## Step-by-step: how to apply for an ITIN personal loan in North Carolina

1. **Confirm your ITIN is active.** Check your most recent IRS correspondence or call the IRS ITIN helpline.
2. **Join a credit union first.** For LCCU, pay the $8 membership fee to the Latino Community Development Center. For Self-Help, pay the $5 nonprofit fee online.
3. **Open a checking or savings account with the credit union.** Even a small balance demonstrates a banking relationship, which nudges approval odds in your favor.
4. **Gather your documents** using the checklist above.
5. **Pre-qualify before submitting a full application.** LCCU and Self-Help both offer pre-qualification conversations that do not trigger a hard credit inquiry.
6. **Compare at least two offers.** If LCCU offers you 15% APR, ask Self-Help if they can do better, or check TD Bank if your score qualifies.
7. **Review the repayment schedule carefully.** LCCU caps terms at 48 months; Self-Help and TD Bank offer longer windows that lower the monthly payment.
