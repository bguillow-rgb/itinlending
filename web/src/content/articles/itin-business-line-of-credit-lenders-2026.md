## Why are business lines of credit harder to find for ITIN holders than term loans?

*A question we hear often:* many ITIN holders have already gotten through a term loan with a CDFI or credit union, then hit a wall when they need revolving credit.

Revolving lines require lenders to commit capital on demand, so they lean harder on the borrower's ongoing creditworthiness than a one-time disbursement does. Most banks tie their underwriting systems to Social Security Number lookups, automated credit pulls, and existing customer relationships, all of which put ITIN-only borrowers at a disadvantage. Most business lending runs off the company's EIN rather than your personal ITIN, but banks still typically require an SSN for the personal guarantee component.

The result: mainstream online lenders like Bluevine and Fundbox are not on this list because, while they accept low credit scores, their applications still require an SSN on the personal guarantee. Every lender in this guide has confirmed ITIN acceptance in their actual application forms or policy pages.

## What is the difference between my ITIN and my EIN, and which one matters more for a business line of credit?

This distinction trips up a lot of applicants.

Your **ITIN** (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number) is your personal tax ID, issued by the IRS to individuals who need to file U.S. taxes but cannot get an SSN. It starts with the number 9 and is formatted like an SSN.

Your **EIN** (Employer Identification Number) is your business's federal tax ID, completely separate from your personal identity. The SBA describes the EIN as the nine-digit number a company uses for tax returns, a business bank account, licenses and permits, and applying for business credit. When a commercial lender underwrites your business, they pull your business credit file using the EIN, not your ITIN.

If your business has its own EIN and has built a credit profile with Dun & Bradstreet, Experian Business, or Equifax Business, lenders will look at that profile first. A strong business credit score shows that the entity itself is creditworthy, which can reduce the weight placed on the owner's immigration status.

For the personal guarantee (the document that makes you personally liable if the business defaults), your ITIN replaces your SSN. So you need both: the EIN for the business file and your ITIN for the personal guarantee. The IRS online EIN tool requires the SSN or ITIN of the responsible party, so ITIN holders can and should obtain an EIN using their ITIN before applying for any business financing.

## Which lenders actually offer a revolving ITIN business line of credit?

*Readers frequently ask:* "I keep seeing term loans listed but I need a line I can draw and repay repeatedly. Who does that with an ITIN?"

Only a small number of lenders in the ITIN-friendly space offer a true revolving product. Here is what each one actually looks like in practice:

### DreamSpring Power Line of Credit

DreamSpring requires a Social Security Number or Individual Tax Identification Number.

The DreamSpring Power Line is a fixed-rate revolving line of credit with terms up to 24 months, ranging from $1,000 to $100,000 at 9.99%-18.74%, with a minimum credit score of 600 and a community benefit fee of 4%-10% due at origination.

DreamSpring offers one of the broadest CDFI product lineups available, including one of the only CDFI-issued revolving lines of credit, and explicitly accepts ITIN across 27 states. The community benefit fee (origination fee) is on top of the stated interest rate and effectively raises the all-in cost, so factor that into comparisons. For amounts under $20,000, DreamSpring offers uncollateralized options with a 650+ credit score.

### Accion Opportunity Fund working capital line

Accion Opportunity Fund (AOF) is a nonprofit CDFI headquartered in San Jose, California, formed in 2020 when Accion U.S. Network and Opportunity Fund merged.

AOF focuses on minority entrepreneurs, veterans, women-owned businesses, and ITIN-only borrowers. It offers loans from $5,000 to $250,000 with terms up to 36 months, and interest rates start at 8.49%. It is a nonprofit lender that focuses on addressing racial and gender inequalities by providing financing to minority business owners who have had trouble getting loans from traditional lenders. Origination fees run up to 3.99% per their published disclosures. A minimum personal FICO score of 600 applies for the standard business term loan, and AOF also weighs cash flow, revenue, and overall business health rather than relying on credit score alone.

## What about ITIN borrowers who need equipment rather than revolving credit?

*This one comes up a lot:* construction contractors, food truck operators, landscapers, and manufacturing startups often need a specific piece of equipment, not a draw-and-repay line.

Equipment financing is one of the more accessible paths for ITIN holders because the equipment itself secures the loan, reducing the lender's reliance on personal credit history.

### How equipment financing rates work in 2026

Equipment financing is a loan or lease used to purchase or access business equipment, with the equipment as collateral. Rates currently range from 5.5%-18% APR for qualified borrowers. Lenders advance 80%-100% of the equipment's purchase price, and repayment terms run 24-84 months.

As of November 2026, rates break down as follows: 6%-14% APR at 700+ FICO, 12%-18% APR at 650-699 FICO, 12%-20% APR at 620-649 FICO, and 18%-22% APR at 580-619 FICO. ITIN borrowers with thin U.S. credit histories and strong business cash flow typically land in the 12%-20% range.

There is also a meaningful tax benefit worth noting: the Section 179 deduction lets businesses deduct the full purchase price of equipment in Year 1, with a 2026 limit of $1,220,000, which substantially reduces net financing cost.

### Conserv Capital: equipment financing for ITIN holders

Conserv Capital accepts ITIN numbers for equipment financing and will need your ITIN, business documentation, and proof of your ability to repay. No SSN required. They finance construction companies from $10,000 to $2 million, with approval in 24-48 hours.

### Cultiva Financial: lease-to-own for contractors

Cultiva Financial's services include international factoring, asset-based loans, equipment leasing, materials purchasing, and invoice factoring. Their lease-to-own program gives contractors of all credit levels access to financing for new or used business equipment between $20,000 and $500,000, with terms from 24-60 months.

Equipment leases differ from traditional financing in that they do not carry an interest rate. Payments are fixed throughout the term and must be made in full to complete the lease. It operates more like a long-term rental, except that if you make all payments, you own the equipment at the end of the term.

Cultiva calculates monthly payments based on a combination of the owner's credit history, business strength and credit, and cash flow. Lessees provide a security deposit of 10% of the equipment's value, applied to the end of the lease. Equipment leases run two to five years, with an average of three years.

Cultiva is based in San Antonio, Texas, and has particular depth with Spanish-speaking clients and Latino-owned businesses in the construction and industrial sectors.

## Can I use a Kiva microloan as a stepping stone to a larger line of credit?

Yes, and this is one of the more underused strategies for ITIN-holding business owners with no U.S. credit history.

A Kiva business loan is a 0% interest, no-fee microloan of $1,000 to $15,000 for U.S. small business owners. Kiva US is generally the most accessible option because it has no credit score requirement, no SSN requirement, no collateral requirement, and charges 0% interest. The tradeoff is timeline: Kiva requires a public crowdfunding phase that typically takes 15-30 days after internal approval.

Kiva can report repayments to Experian Business and Dun & Bradstreet, which creates a business tradeline under your EIN. After 12-18 months of on-time Kiva payments, you have a business credit file that DreamSpring and Accion will consider favorably, which improves your approval odds and rate for a larger revolving line.

The practical ladder looks like this:

1. **Months 0-6:** Apply for Kiva (up to $15,000, 0% interest). Repay on time.
2. **Months 6-18:** Open vendor net-30 accounts with suppliers that report to Dun & Bradstreet.
3. **Month 12-18:** Apply to DreamSpring Power Line of Credit (up to $100,000) or Accion Opportunity Fund using your established business credit file.
4. **Year 2+:** Equipment financing with Conserv Capital or Cultiva Financial using documented business revenue and a clean EIN credit profile.

## What documents do I need to apply for an ITIN business line of credit?

*Readers frequently ask* for a single consolidated checklist, because every lender guide lists slightly different items.

Based on verified lender requirements across the programs in this guide:

**Identity documents (personal)**
- ITIN assignment letter from the IRS (Form CP565)
- Valid government-issued photo ID: passport, Matricula Consular, or state-issued ID

**Business identity documents**
- EIN confirmation letter (IRS Form CP-575 or 147C)
- Business registration documents: LLC articles of organization, DBA certificate, or corporation formation documents
- Certificate of Good Standing from your state (some lenders)

**Financial documents**
- 3-6 months of business bank statements showing revenue and cash flow
- Most recent year of business tax returns (or year-to-date profit and loss statement for newer businesses)
- For DreamSpring specifically: government-issued ID (ITIN accepted) and a brief description of how the loan will be used

**For equipment financing specifically**
- Invoice or purchase quote from the equipment vendor
- Equipment description: make, model, year, and estimated resale value
- 10% security deposit (Cultiva Financial lease structure)

The document set is similar to what you would need for any ITIN business loan. If you have already filed two years of federal business tax returns using your ITIN and EIN, you are in the strongest possible position.

## What has changed for ITIN business borrowers in 2026?

The most significant development is the SBA rule change. Since March 1, 2026, SBA programs require 100% of a business's direct and indirect owners to be U.S. citizens or U.S. nationals whose principal residence is in the U.S. Lawful permanent residents and ITIN filers can no longer hold any ownership stake in an SBA borrower, so a business with an ITIN-filing owner needs a non-SBA option.

This directly affects SBA microloans, which had previously been accessible through CDFI intermediaries. The practical consequence is that the CDFI revolving line products from DreamSpring and the term loans from Accion become even more important as the primary institutional alternative. DreamSpring accepts ITIN across all of its loan products in the 27 states it serves and offers a no-collateral option under $20,000, which is a rare combination among CDFI lenders nationally.

For equipment financing, the SBA rule change has less impact because equipment lenders are predominantly private commercial lenders who were never relying on SBA guarantees in the first place.

## How do I build business credit with an ITIN so future borrowing gets cheaper?

Business credit is completely separate from your personal credit file and is not affected by whether you have an SSN. It is tied to your EIN and tracked by Dun & Bradstreet, Experian Business, and Equifax Business, which are entirely separate from the personal bureaus.

You can create your business file with Dun & Bradstreet by registering for a D-U-N-S Number. Once you have one, your business has a credit file, though it will not have a score until there is payment information on that file. A D-U-N-S number is free from Dun & Bradstreet and usually arrives within 30 business days.

Practical credit-building steps for ITIN-holding business owners:

1. **Register for a D-U-N-S Number.** Free at Dun & Bradstreet. This opens your business credit file.
2. **Repay your Kiva loan on schedule.** Kiva can report repayment to Experian Business and Dun & Bradstreet, creating your first tradeline.
3. **Open vendor net-30 accounts.** Vendor net-30 credit lets you pay for goods 30 days later. Pay on time and they report it to business credit bureaus like Dun & Bradstreet, building your business's reputation independently of your immigration status.
4. **Consider an EIN-only corporate card.** EIN-only corporate cards bypass personal credit evaluation entirely because they look at business cash or revenue, metrics where immigrant entrepreneurs often perform better than the personal credit system gives them credit for.
5. **Report payments from CDFI loans.** CDFIs such as DreamSpring report to business credit bureaus, creating a tradeline that strengthens your EIN file for future, larger applications.

After 12-18 months of consistent, on-time payment history across these sources, you will have a Paydex score (Dun & Bradstreet's 0-100 business credit score) and an Experian Business file that commercial equipment lenders and larger line-of-credit programs will recognize without requiring exceptional personal credit.
