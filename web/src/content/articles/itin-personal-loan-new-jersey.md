New Jersey is home to one of the largest immigrant communities on the East Coast, and many residents hold an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number instead of a Social Security Number. If you need funds for an emergency, home repair, debt consolidation, or anything else, you have real borrowing options here, even without an SSN.

## Is it actually legal to get a personal loan in New Jersey without an SSN?

No federal law requires an SSN for a personal loan. Credit unions, CDFIs, and some community banks will lend to ITIN holders using your ITIN, tax returns, and bank statements as verification. New Jersey state lending laws add no citizenship requirement on top of federal rules, so the only real barrier is finding the right lender, not the law itself.

Approval is most common through credit unions, online lenders, and specialty finance companies. Many ITIN personal loans are unsecured, though secured options tend to come with lower rates. Expect higher APRs than SSN-based loans, particularly with limited credit history. Income verification and repayment ability carry more weight than citizenship status.

## Which lenders specifically serve New Jersey ITIN borrowers?

*A question we hear often:* where do I actually walk in, or log on, and get approved with an ITIN in New Jersey? The list is shorter than for SSN holders, but it is real.

TD Bank's TD Fit Loan explicitly lists ITIN as an acceptable form of identification. The application asks for basic personal info, your SSN or ITIN, employment history, income, and expenses. The fixed APR runs 7.99%-23.99% (as of June 2026) with no origination, application, or prepayment fees. TD Fit Loan is available in 15 states plus Washington, D.C., including New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Vermont, and Virginia, which makes it one of the stronger bank options for Garden State residents.

Capital Good Fund runs an Impact Loan program in New Jersey built specifically for underserved borrowers. It accepts applicants with a Social Security number or ITIN who live in New Jersey and earn active, recurring income, ideally verifiable through bank account history. The program covers emergency expenses, home energy efficiency, immigration costs, and similar needs. ITINs and other documentation are accepted in lieu of an SSN.

Princeton Federal Credit Union is a New Jersey-based credit union with a dedicated Spanish-language ITIN personal loan page. Their loans are designed to cover important expenses, personal projects, or debt consolidation without requiring a Social Security number. APRs start around 9.75% for qualified members.

Oportun is a fintech lender that actively markets to ITIN holders across the country, including New Jersey. Loans run $500 to $10,000 with APRs capped at 35.99%. They report monthly payment history to major credit bureaus. Requirements include an ITIN or passport, proof of steady income, and a verified residential address.

OneMain Financial rounds out the in-person options. They accept ITIN borrowers, have close to 1,400 branches across 44 states, and will review applicants with fair or poor credit. If your credit history is thin, you can secure the loan with a vehicle title, which shifts some risk to the lender and can bring the rate down.

## What do I actually need to qualify? (The honest checklist)

*This one comes up a lot:* readers often assume ITIN lending requires exotic paperwork. In reality the list looks a lot like any personal loan application, with one substitution.

Your official IRS ITIN assignment letter (Form CP565) or an unexpired ITIN card is the core document. Beyond that, you generally need:

- Government photo ID: a passport, consular ID (Matrícula Consular), or New Jersey driver's license.
- Proof of income: recent pay stubs (last 2-3 months), 1-2 years of tax returns filed with your ITIN, or 3-6 months of bank statements if you are self-employed.
- U.S. bank account: most lenders require an active checking or savings account to deposit funds. Prepaid or government benefit cards are not accepted by lenders like Capital Good Fund.
- New Jersey address: a utility bill, lease agreement, or bank statement showing your NJ address confirms residency.

For most personal loans, lenders want two years of filed returns. That is because ITIN borrowers frequently have self-employment or cash income that a pay stub alone will not cover.

If you do not yet have a U.S. credit file, consider starting the process of [building credit with an ITIN](/articles/how-to-build-credit-with-itin) at the same time, since even a thin but growing credit file will improve your rate on the next loan.

## What if I have bad credit or no U.S. credit history at all?

*Readers frequently ask:* "I just arrived. I have an ITIN but zero U.S. credit. Am I stuck?"

Not entirely. Approval at most of these lenders turns on income stability and bank account history, not a U.S. credit score, which most ITIN holders do not have yet. Capital Good Fund and Oportun set no minimum credit score, which makes them the strongest starting points for someone with no U.S. credit file.

For borrowers who do have some credit history but it is damaged, OneMain Financial's secured loan option is worth a look. Putting up a paid-off vehicle as collateral can get you approved at a more manageable rate. Pairing two or three credit-building products with an ITIN personal loan can produce a FICO score in the 650+ range within 12-18 months, even starting from zero U.S. credit history.

## How do I apply step by step?

*A question we hear often:* what does the actual process look like from first search to funded account?

1. Confirm your ITIN is active. An expired ITIN will halt an application immediately. If yours expired, review our [ITIN renewal guide](/how-to-get-an-itin) before applying.
2. Gather documents upfront. Collect your CP565 letter, photo ID, two months of pay stubs (or two years of tax returns for self-employed), and a utility bill showing your NJ address.
3. Start with credit unions. Princeton FCU and other NJ-based community credit unions typically offer the lowest APRs. Membership is often open to anyone who lives or works in the county.
4. Compare at least two lenders. Even a 2-3 percentage point difference in APR translates to hundreds of dollars over a 36-month term.
5. Watch the debt-to-income ratio. Keep it below 40% before applying. Lenders lean on this number heavily when ITIN borrowers lack a traditional credit score.
6. Apply and confirm deposit details. Upload your IRS ITIN letter, identity documents, and bank statements. Once approved, sign the loan agreement and confirm your direct deposit information.
