Texas is home to one of the largest immigrant entrepreneur communities in the country, and the lending market here has grown to match that reality. CDFIs (Community Development Financial Institutions), nonprofit microlenders, and mission-driven credit unions operate across the state and explicitly accept an ITIN in place of a Social Security Number. You do not need to be a citizen or permanent resident to access real business capital in Texas.

## Can I actually get a business loan in Texas with just an ITIN?

Yes, and it is more straightforward than many borrowers expect.

CDFIs, microlenders, and nonprofit lenders accept an ITIN as the primary personal identifier in place of an SSN. You will still need an EIN for your business, a business bank account, and standard business documents.

One important change took effect in 2026. Under SBA Policy Notice 5000-876441, effective March 1, 2026, SBA rules require that 100% of all direct and indirect owners of a small business applicant be U.S. citizens or U.S. nationals. ITIN holders and green card holders are both excluded. That closes the SBA 7(a) and 504 door, but it does not touch the broader private market. CDFIs, nonprofits, and community credit unions are actively lending to ITIN holders across Texas right now.

## Which lenders in Texas accept ITIN for business loans?

*A question we hear often:* Texas borrowers frequently want a concrete short list, not just a category name. Here are the verified options.

LiftFund is headquartered in San Antonio and covers Texas and 12 neighboring states, offering loans from $500 to $1 million for underserved business owners. DreamSpring covers Texas within its 27-state footprint.

PeopleFund is a Texas-focused nonprofit CDFI based in Austin that has lent over $130 million to more than 3,000 unique small businesses, creating or retaining over 11,000 jobs across the state. PeopleFund explicitly serves immigrant-owned businesses and accepts ITINs.

Accion Opportunity Fund accepts an ITIN in place of a Social Security number for certain applicants, which opens access to immigrant entrepreneurs and others without traditional U.S. identification.

Kiva is worth considering for smaller amounts, especially for startups with no credit history. Kiva requires no collateral. DreamSpring offers uncollateralized options for loans under $20,000 with a 650+ credit score.

## What rates and terms should I expect?

*This one comes up a lot:* ITIN borrowers often worry they will face punishing rates. The reality depends heavily on which lender type you choose.

CDFI rates typically run 8% to 15% APR. That is higher than SBA rates of 5.5% to 12%, but nowhere near the merchant cash advance products that carry factor rates translating to 40% to 150% effective APR.

Accion charges simple interest on its term loans. Rates start at 8.49% for the most qualified borrowers and go up to 28.99% for borrowers with weaker credit profiles or shorter operating histories. The rate reflects your individual risk profile, not a blanket formula.

For the smallest loans, Kiva is a compelling entry point: 0% interest, no minimum credit score, no minimum time-in-business requirement. The catch is the crowdfunding model, which means your network needs to help fund the campaign before money disburses.

Lenders also look at whether your business generates enough cash flow to cover debt service. A debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) of 1.25 or higher is commonly required, meaning your business earns $1.25 for every $1.00 in debt obligations. At CDFI lenders, hitting that threshold often matters more than your credit score.

## What documents do I need to apply?

*Readers frequently ask:* what paperwork is involved, and whether being an immigrant or ITIN holder adds extra steps.

The documentation list is similar to what any small business owner would submit, with one difference: your ITIN as the personal tax identifier rather than an SSN. Most Texas CDFIs and microlenders will ask for:

- Valid ITIN (must be current and not expired)
- EIN (Employer Identification Number) for your business entity
- Government-issued photo ID such as a passport, consular ID (matrícula consular), or state ID
- 12 months of business bank statements
- Business tax returns or a year-to-date profit-and-loss statement
- Proof of business registration: DBA filing, LLC articles of organization, or equivalent
- Business plan (required by some lenders for startups)

CDFIs are built for underserved borrowers, immigrants included. A business bank account, 12 months of statements, and a clear business plan are the three documents that move your application forward fastest.

If you have not yet set up a business bank account, do that before you apply. Lenders want to see that your business finances are separate from personal finances, and a dedicated account also makes your cash flow story easier to present.

## What if my credit score is low or I have no U.S. credit history?

*A question we hear often:* many immigrant business owners in Texas are creditworthy by any cash-flow standard but have a thin or nonexistent U.S. credit file. CDFI lenders are built for exactly this situation.

CDFIs offer business loans ranging from $1,000 to $250,000 and many do not require an SSN. They use alternative underwriting that looks at cash flow, business history, and character rather than credit scores alone.

Many CDFIs accept credit scores of 550 or above, and Kiva sets no credit minimum at all. If you have rent payment history, utility payment records, or a track record with suppliers, bring that documentation. These alternative credit signals carry real weight at mission-driven lenders. If your score is below 600, starting with a Kiva loan or a smaller LiftFund microloan and repaying it on time is the fastest way to build the file you need for a larger loan 12-18 months later. You can also pair this strategy with an [ITIN credit builder loan](/articles/itin-credit-builder-loan) to build your personal credit file at the same time.

## Step-by-step: how to apply for an ITIN business loan in Texas

1. **Get your EIN first.** Apply free at IRS.gov. Most lenders will not process a business loan application without one.
2. **Open a dedicated business bank account.** Use your ITIN and EIN. Several Texas credit unions and online banks accept ITINs for business accounts.
3. **Gather 12 months of bank statements** and prepare a simple profit-and-loss statement if your lender requires one.
4. **Choose your lender tier.** If you have no credit history, start with Kiva. If you have at least a 600 score and 6+ months in business, apply to Accion or LiftFund. If you are Texas-based and want a local relationship, contact PeopleFund directly.
5. **Submit your application.** Most CDFIs have online portals. Bilingual assistance is available at LiftFund and PeopleFund.
6. **Complete the underwriting interview.** Many CDFIs will schedule a call to understand your business before approving. Be ready to explain your revenue model and how you will use the funds.
7. **Review your offer carefully.** Confirm the full APR (not just the monthly rate), the repayment term, and whether there are prepayment penalties. Accion, for example, offers terms up to 60 months with no prepayment penalties and accepts ITIN applications, with rates starting at 8.49% on loans from $5,000 to $250,000.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I get a business loan in Texas with only an ITIN and no SSN?**
Yes. CDFIs, nonprofit microlenders, and some credit unions in Texas accept an ITIN in place of an SSN. You will still need an EIN for your business entity, a business bank account, and proof of income or cash flow.

**What credit score do I need for an ITIN business loan in Texas?**
Requirements vary by lender. Accion Opportunity Fund accepts scores as low as 600. Kiva has no minimum credit score. PeopleFund and DreamSpring evaluate applications case by case, often focusing on cash flow and business history more than FICO alone.

**Are SBA loans available to ITIN holders in Texas in 2026?**
No. Under SBA Policy Notice 5000-876441, effective March 1, 2026, SBA-guaranteed programs (7a and 504) require 100% U.S. citizen ownership. ITIN holders and green card holders are both excluded. CDFI and nonprofit microlenders remain open and actively lending.

**How much can I borrow with an ITIN business loan in Texas?**
Loan sizes depend on the lender and your business profile. Kiva goes up to $15,000 at 0% interest. Accion Opportunity Fund lends $5,000 to $250,000. LiftFund offers up to $1 million for qualified businesses. DreamSpring goes up to $2 million for commercial real estate.

**What documents do I need to apply for a Texas ITIN business loan?**
Most Texas CDFIs and microlenders require: a valid ITIN, EIN, government-issued photo ID (passport or consular ID), 12 months of business bank statements, business tax returns or a profit-and-loss statement, and proof of business registration (DBA, LLC, or articles of incorporation).

**How long does it take to get funded through a CDFI in Texas?**
Timelines vary. Kiva campaigns take 30-45 days to fund through its crowdfunding model. Accion typically funds in 3-7 business days after approval. LiftFund and PeopleFund can take 2-6 weeks depending on loan size and documentation completeness.
